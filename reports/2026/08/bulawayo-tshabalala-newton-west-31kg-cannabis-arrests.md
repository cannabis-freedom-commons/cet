Bulawayo police arrested two Tshabalala suburb residents, Annet Sebele, 38, and
Fortune Tshabalala, 42, in separate anti-drug operations in the city. [@1]
Detectives from the CID Drugs and Narcotics unit arrested Sebele on 1 August
after a tip that she was operating a drug supply business from her home. [@1]
She was carrying four cobs of cannabis weighing 1.8 kilogrammes hidden in a
satchel, and detectives also found empty sachets used for repackaging. [@1]
Questioning led detectives to a nearby house believed to be a drug storehouse,
where Tshabalala was arrested. [@1]

In a separate operation in Newton West on 2 August at about 4am, detectives
acted on information that drugs were being sold from a house. [@2] Police saw
suspects loading an unidentified substance into a white Honda Vezel; the
suspects drove into the yard, locked the gate, jumped over the durawall and
escaped through nearby bushes. [@1] A search of the house recovered 36 cobs of
cannabis weighing about 30 kilogrammes. [@1] Detectives also recovered 58 boxes
of Broncleer cough syrup containing 50 bottles each and 447 loose 100ml bottles,
which police valued at US$26,429. [@1]

The two operations together yielded 31.8 kilogrammes of cannabis, police said.
[@1] Police said investigations were continuing to identify and arrest the
suspects who escaped. [@1] Provincial police spokesperson Inspector Nomalanga
Msebele thanked members of the public for the information that led to the
arrests and seizures. [@1]

Broncleer, a codeine-based cough syrup, has increasingly appeared in Bulawayo
drug seizures, while cannabis remains one of Zimbabwe's most commonly trafficked
illicit drugs. [@1]
