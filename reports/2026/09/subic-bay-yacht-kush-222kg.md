Four Thai and Malaysian crew members of the yacht were still being sought for
questioning after the search at the Subic Bay Yacht Club. [@2] The Bureau of
Customs–Port of Subic acted on 2 September 2026 on information about the
distressed sailing yacht. [@1] The vessel had arrived at the club on 28 August
2026 with four crew members aboard after sending a distress signal en route from
Kota Kinabalu, Malaysia, to Taiwan. [@2] Repairmen found plastic bags suspected
to contain contraband in an engine-room compartment and reported them to the
club's management. [@3] The information indicated the vessel might be carrying
prohibited items, prompting authorities to conduct an inspection. [@1] District
Collector Geniefelle Lagmay issued a mission order authorising the inspection
and search. [@1]

The first inspection found 98.332 kilograms of kush, which the Bureau of Customs
valued at about P162.2 million. [@1] Around P204.44 million worth of additional
drugs was uncovered on 5 September during the continuing stripping and
inspection of the yacht. [@4] The further inspection yielded an additional 109
packages of kush weighing about 123.91 kilograms. [@3] With that find, the BOC
said the total haul from the vessel stood at 198 packages of kush worth a
combined P366.65 million. [@8] Combining the bureau's two published weights
gives about 222.24 kilograms of marijuana. [@6] Bulgar Online put the total at
more than 222 kilograms across 198 packages, with an estimated street value of
P366.65 million. [@7] The Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority's first account put
the recovery at 89 bags weighing about 89 kilograms and worth P106.8 million,
subject to laboratory tests. [@2]

The operation was carried out with the Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority Law
Enforcement Department, the Philippine Coast Guard, the Philippine Drug
Enforcement Agency and Justice Department representatives. [@5] The seized items
were turned over to the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency for custody and
disposition. [@4] No charges had been filed against the crew, the Subic Bay
Metropolitan Authority said, pending further investigation. [@2] The Olongapo
City Police Office led the search for them with local and regional intelligence
units. [@2] The Bureau of Customs did not say that the crew members had been
arrested, charged or identified as the owners of the kush. [@5]

The Subic-BOC said it would investigate the owners and recipients of the
contraband and any links to local and international drug syndicates. [@3] The
bureau presented the seizure as part of President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr.'s
directive to strengthen border protection and stop the trafficking and entry of
illegal drugs. [@1]
