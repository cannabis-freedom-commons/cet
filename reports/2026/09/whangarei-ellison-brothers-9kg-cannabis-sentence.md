Jamie Ellison, 33, and Jay Ellison, 29, appeared for sentencing in the Whangārei
District Court on joint charges of possession of cannabis for sale and a
representative charge of offering to sell cannabis. [@1] Judge Philip Rzepecky
sentenced the brothers to nine months' home detention. [@1] Crown lawyer Eilish
O'Connor said the brothers were essentially running their own cannabis operation
or business together. [@1]

On 8 January 2026 police stopped the Ellisons in Waipu because of Jamie
Ellison's driving, with Jay Ellison in the passenger seat. [@1] While speaking
to the men, police noticed a smell of cannabis coming from the vehicle. [@1] A
search of the vehicle uncovered 2.4kg of cannabis packaged in ziplock bags of
varying sizes and weights. [@1] Police also seized the brothers' mobile phones.
[@1] Forensic analysis uncovered a Facebook Messenger account under the name Jay
Prouse with evidence of 42 cannabis deals involving offers to supply 15.6kg of
cannabis. [@1] On 25 February police executed a search warrant at one of the
brothers' Waipu homes and located a further 6.9kg of cannabis. [@1]

O'Connor said the cannabis deals were sophisticated over seven months, with
different strains and price points advertised online. [@1] Jay Ellison's lawyer
Adam Pell argued the operation was not particularly sophisticated because
everything led back to his client, who dealt under his mother's surname. [@1]
Jamie Ellison told the court he had battled addiction for 20 years before
engaging with rehabilitation. [@1] Judge Rzepecky said Jay Ellison had made
significant progress in rehabilitation and was still a young man with care and
custody of a vulnerable child. [@1]
