The Iquique Court of Appeals rejected a customs official's protection appeal
against the early termination of his short-term contract after marijuana was
found in his wallet inside the Quillagua customs post. [@1] The official was a
sociologist who had joined the National Customs Service in December 2025 on a
contract extended until 1 November 2026. [@2] On 11 September 2026 he lost his
wallet after finishing his shift at the Avanzada Aduanera de Quillagua. [@2]

Another worker found the wallet in a restricted-access area and discovered 1.2
grams of a substance that tested positive for marijuana in a preliminary test.
[@2] The find was reported to the Public Prosecutor's Office, which ordered the
intervention of the OS7 unit of Carabineros. [@2] On 14 September he was removed
from his duties, his institutional items were withdrawn and he was told an
administrative inquiry would be opened. [@2]

The former official said he had not been present when the wallet was opened and
described the dismissal as a covert sanction imposed without a criminal
conviction or completed disciplinary inquiry. [@1] He asked the court to set
aside the resolution, order his reinstatement and order payment of wages for the
separation period. [@2] Customs argued it had full legal authority to end a
contract early when services were no longer deemed necessary, the condition
governing his employment. [@1] The court held that early termination of a
short-term contract is not a disciplinary sanction and therefore requires
neither a prior inquiry nor a determination of criminal or administrative
liability, but is a lawful exercise of authority. [@2]

El Boyaldía summarised the same find as a substance that tested positive for
marijuana weighing 1.2 grams. [@3]
