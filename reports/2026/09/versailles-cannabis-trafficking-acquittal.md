A six-year prison sentence was overturned and Ahmed A. was definitively
acquitted on 9 September 2026. [@2] He had been sentenced to six years for
taking part in a large cannabis trafficking operation between Morocco and the
Paris region. [@1] The Versailles Court of Appeal ruled after an essential part
of the proceedings was destroyed. [@2]

The case began in October 1999, when an anonymous tip led the Hauts-de-Seine
judicial police to a network moving hundreds of kilos of cannabis. [@1] The
cannabis was bought in the Rif, shipped by boat to Spain and then brought by
convoy to the Paris region. [@1] About ten suspects were arrested and tried at
the time, but Ahmed A. escaped by fleeing to Morocco. [@1] According to Le
Parisien, he was described in the proceedings as the network's logistician,
finding vehicles, GPS devices and binoculars. [@1] Conversations in the case
file mentioned 130 kilos of cannabis seized in Algeria and 700 kilos thrown into
the sea by smugglers during a Mediterranean crossing. [@1]

Ahmed A. was arrested on 4 April 2023 at Orly airport, nearly 24 years after the
investigation opened. [@1] Police had been warned of his arrival and arrested
him as he presented a false passport in the name of Eddy Merabet. [@1] The case
was divided between Paris and the Nanterre criminal court. [@1] Part of the
Paris file was destroyed in a fire, so Ahmed A. was acquitted in Paris in March
2024. [@1] The Nanterre criminal court sentenced him to six years in prison that
same year for acts linked to the same network. [@1] His lawyer, Me Tarek
Koraitem, appealed, arguing that the Paris and Nanterre proceedings rested on
the same evidence and could not be separated. [@1] The defence invoked the rule
against trying someone twice for the same acts. [@1] The appeal court held that
the Paris acquittal, caused by the documents lost in the fire, prevented the
prosecution in Nanterre from continuing. [@1]
