California Attorney General Rob Bonta on 13 May 2026 announced the arrest of and
felony charges against unlicensed cannabis retailer Pin Hsien Hsu for evading
about $7.1 million in sales tax. [@2] Hsu operated unlicensed cannabis
dispensaries in Los Angeles, Orange and San Bernardino counties from April 2019
to November 2022 and allegedly failed to report about $80 million in marijuana
sales to the tax agency. [@1] Prosecutors allege that Hsu, 46, ran about 30
unlicensed dispensaries, failed to file sales tax returns and did not report
about $80 million in sales. [@3] Prosecutors charged Hsu with 66 counts
including failing to file a sales tax return, engaging in business without a
permit and money laundering. [@3] If convicted on all charges, he faces more
than 50 years in state prison. [@3]

During searches investigators seized about $2.2 million in cash, 125 pounds of
contraband untaxed cannabis products, 62 electronic devices and 14 boxes of
related evidence. [@2] Orange County inmate records show Hsu was booked into
jail on 29 April and released on 2 May. [@4] It was not clear whether Hsu had
entered a plea or was represented by an attorney. [@3] The investigation by the
Department of Justice and the Department of Tax and Fee Administration began in
2020 after an illicit-dispensary operation in San Bernardino County found a
cloud-based point-of-sale system supporting illegal cannabis sales across about
30 dispensaries. [@4]

All of the storefronts, which did not charge customers sales tax and included
names such as High Tide Church, Fullerton Fire House and Best of Buddha, were
shut down. [@4]
