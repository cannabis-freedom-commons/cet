Georgia's Interior Ministry said officers of its central criminal police
department detained 12 people on charges of illegally acquiring, storing and
selling a particularly large quantity of drugs and cultivating plants containing
narcotic substances. [@1] The detainees include Georgian citizens and foreign
nationals, and some had previous convictions. [@1]

Searches of the detainees' homes and of places they pointed out produced drugs
and psychotropic substances in particularly large quantities, prepared for sale.
[@2] Among the haul were about one kilogram of mephedrone, ketamine, MDMA,
methadone, alpha-PVP, psilocybin mushrooms, morphine tablets, pregabalin,
hashish, tetrahydrocannabinol and marijuana. [@1] Police also seized firearms
and materials for packaging doses. [@1] Some of the drugs were in postal parcels
addressed to the detainees and intercepted by officers. [@1] To expose some of
those held, police carried out a controlled replacement of parcels sent to them
and then seized a particularly large quantity of drugs. [@2]

Officers found specially equipped greenhouses where cannabis was illegally grown
and cultivated in particularly large quantities. [@3] Police took special
equipment and materials for packaging drugs from the owner. [@1]

The ministry said the charges carry up to 20 years in prison or life
imprisonment. [@2]
