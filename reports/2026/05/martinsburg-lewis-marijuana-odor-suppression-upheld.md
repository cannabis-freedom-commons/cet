The West Virginia Supreme Court of Appeals denied the state's request for a writ
of prohibition in the Berkeley County case against Aaron Curtis Lewis. [@1]
Chief Justice Haley Bunn wrote the 29 May 2026 opinion. [@1] The court held that
the warrant was overbroad and failed to make the connection between the alleged
crime and the items to be seized. [@1] The court declined to decide whether the
smell of marijuana alone could have provided probable cause to search Lewis's
home. [@1]

Patrolman R.J. Holloway of the Martinsburg City Police Department set out the
search in an affidavit submitted to a magistrate on 24 July 2020. [@2] Holloway
detected a strong odor of marijuana when Lewis's 18-year-old son opened the door
of Apartment 4. [@2] Before obtaining a warrant, officers entered the apartment
in what the affidavit called a security sweep and saw a bundle of money and two
clear bowls with a leafy substance on the stove. [@1] The warrant alleged only
possession of controlled substances, while its list of items to be seized
included heroin, methamphetamine, firearms and evidence of drug trafficking.
[@2] Officers seized two tubs of marijuana, three bags of marijuana, a bag of
suspected heroin, a bag of suspected crack cocaine, a pistol, a magazine,
ammunition and $66 in cash. [@2]

A Berkeley County grand jury indicted Lewis in February 2022 on charges of
possessing crack cocaine with intent to distribute, possessing a controlled
substance involving fentanyl with intent to distribute, and felony firearm
possession by a prohibited person. [@2] Lewis moved to suppress the evidence in
October 2023. [@2] Circuit Judge Debra McLaughlin granted the motion, ruling
that the odor of marijuana alone did not establish probable cause to believe the
home held evidence of drug trafficking, heroin or methamphetamine. [@1] The
judge also refused to apply the good faith exception because the affidavit was
bare bones. [@2]

At April 2026 arguments, assistant attorney general Holly Mestemacher argued
that marijuana odor provides probable cause and that without the evidence the
state could not proceed with charging Lewis. [@1] Lewis's lawyer, Cameron
LeFevre, told the justices they need not answer the marijuana-odor question
because the case had other errors, including an improper security sweep. [@1]
The attorney general's office said it sought the ruling because prosecutors and
police need clear guidance. [@1] West Virginia legalised medical marijuana in
2017, and every state surrounding it has legalised medical or recreational
marijuana. [@1]
