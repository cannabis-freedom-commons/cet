Jennifer Devery, a Rotorua woman, was sentenced to seven months' home detention
for supplying cannabis oil in the Rotorua District Court. [@1] Judge John
Bergseng noted a causal connection between her troubled background, including
PTSD, and the offending, and took account of her rehabilitation work. [@1] She
received discounts of 15 percent for addiction issues, 15 percent for remorse
and 25 percent for her guilty plea. [@1] The starting sentence was 20 months'
imprisonment, reduced to 14 months because of time spent on restricted bail
conditions. [@1] That term was then commuted to a home detention sentence of
seven months. [@2]

Devery ran an online business through Facebook under the profile page CBD New
Zealand, advertising cannabis oil in various quantities and prices alongside
legal natural health balms. [@1] Between 18 October 2023 and 17 October 2024 she
supplied cannabis oil and natural health balms to about 521 customers around New
Zealand, receiving $32,445.50 in cash deposits. [@3] Between 18 October and 4
December 2024 she supplied about 57 further customers, receiving $4,495.50. [@1]
It was accepted that supply of illegal cannabis oil, including within balms and
oils, made up more than half of the business. [@1] The agreed summary of facts
said it was not possible to specify how many transactions involved illegal
cannabis oil rather than legal balms. [@1]

The case came to light after a Napier customer ordered three THC cannabis oil
bottles through the Facebook page and Devery posted them the next day in a NZ
Post drop box at Westend Dairy in Rotorua. [@1] NZ Post staff in Napier noticed
the parcel's smell on 9 October and called police, who said it reeked of
cannabis. [@1] Police searched Devery's home on 10 October and found packaging
and large quantities of empty capsules, along with $3,635 in cash, mostly in $50
notes, in a Nike bag, a drawer and under her bedroom mattress. [@1]

Devery said she began supplying cannabis oil after her sister-in-law died
following a cancer diagnosis. [@1] She told NZME that the offending was not
about the profit and said she had a lot of remorse. [@1]
