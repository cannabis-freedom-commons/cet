Andrew Cooley, a 32-year-old lorry driver of Ballyoran, Dundalk, County Louth,
was jailed for five years for importing more than €1 million worth of cannabis
through Dublin Port. [@1] He pleaded guilty at Dublin Circuit Criminal Court to
importing more than 50 kilograms of cannabis at Dublin Port on 7 June 2025. [@1]
Judge Elma Sheahan imposed a seven-year sentence with the final 24 months
suspended on strict conditions. [@2] He is a 32-year-old lorry driver from
County Louth. [@3]

The articulated lorry and refrigerated trailer he was driving was stopped at the
port, and he said he had nothing to declare and was carrying Amazon goods. [@1]
An inspector smelled cannabis, and 31 packages were found under clothing, with a
street value of €1,007,000. [@1] Cooley took ownership of the drugs but told
gardaí he did not know the value, had no weighing scale, and estimated they were
worth 10 grand. [@1]

He said he had transported the drugs because of a threat from dealers to whom he
had previously owed a debt, although that debt had been repaid a decade earlier.
[@1] The court heard he had been known to gardaí as a drug user, had built up a
debt 10 years earlier, and that his parents’ home had been petrol-bombed as a
result. [@1] His parents settled the debt, he had been clean of drugs since
2016, and he was no longer on the Garda radar. [@1] He went into custody after
the seizure, and a Garda information form warning of a credible threat to life
or safety was served on his family. [@2] He was granted compassionate bail in
February when his mother died. [@2]
