Two men face felony charges over marijuana carried on domestic flights into
Orlando. [@2] Court records filed in the Ninth Judicial Circuit name Avante
Garrick, 25, charged with drug trafficking and bringing marijuana into Florida.
[@2] Alvonte Cooper, 22, is charged with drug trafficking, importing marijuana
and using commercial travel to commit a crime. [@2]

Undercover officers and drug-sniffing dogs from the Orlando Police Department
intercepted the marijuana in two seizures at Orlando International Airport. [@2]
On 7 May, according to a court affidavit, officers watched Cooper take two large
suitcases off baggage belt 30 and tear the airline tags off the handles. [@2]
The bags held 57.6 pounds (about 26 kilograms) of marijuana in 50 vacuum-sealed
packages, wrapped in blankets and packed with dryer sheets. [@2] On 1 July
officers found 144.4 pounds (about 65.5 kilograms) across five suitcases whose
locks had been glued shut. [@2] Together the two seizures came to more than 200
pounds (about 91.6 kilograms). [@3]

The station reported that smugglers use ordinary domestic flights to carry bulk
marijuana into Central Florida, based on police records it obtained. [@1] Both
flights came directly from Los Angeles International Airport, which police call
a major source city for illegal drugs. [@2] Michael Cluck, a retired Border
Patrol intelligence agent, said organisations use domestic flights, mail
carriers and highways because they bypass international border checks. [@2] A
TSA spokesperson said the agency's mission is aviation security and that
screeners pass suspected crimes to local airport police. [@2] Keith Jeffries, a
former TSA federal security director at Los Angeles, said it was unclear how the
marijuana got onto the flights and that trafficking “can happen on any domestic
route — and it does.” [@4]
