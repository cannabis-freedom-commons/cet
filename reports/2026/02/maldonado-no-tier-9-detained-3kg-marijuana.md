Maldonado police detained nine people in 14 simultaneous raids during Operation
No Tier, a sweep against a drug-trafficking network. [@1] Four of the detainees
already had active judicial warrants for drug trafficking, illegal weapons
possession and aggravated theft. [@1] The Interior Ministry said those held were
four men and a woman aged 20 to 25 with no prior records, a Brazilian man, a
20-year-old with a record identified as A.J.N.V., and a 17-year-old. [@2] On 10
February a Maldonado court formalized four men, the woman and A.J.N.V. for
continued drug supply combined with arms trafficking and ordered 150 days of
preventive detention. [@2] The 17-year-old was found responsible for a serious
drug-supply offence and given a 12-month measure restricting his liberty. [@2]

Officers seized three kilograms of marijuana. [@1] They also seized three
kilograms of cocaine. [@1] The haul included nine firearms — 9 mm pistols, a
revolver, a shotgun and a rifle — plus cartridges, mobile phones, radios and
surveillance cameras used to monitor drug-sale points. [@1] The Interior
Ministry put the cash seized at 321,481 Uruguayan pesos. [@2] At a press
conference the police chief gave the cannabis amount as 3.5 kilograms of
marijuana and the cocaine as 1.5 kilograms. [@3] He also cited 295,000 Uruguayan
pesos in low-denomination notes. [@3]

The operation was authorized by Maldonado's Fourth Turn Prosecutor's Office and
aimed at an organization trafficking drugs in urban and tourist areas of the
department. [@1] The 14 raids covered homes, warehouses and rural properties,
with support from multiple coordinated police units. [@1] The Interior Ministry
said the network sold drugs at several points in San Carlos. [@2] The ministry
linked the operation to the Pabellón XIII anti-drug raid carried out on 22 July
2025. [@2] Local reporting said the raids were a large deployment in the Brisas
del Monte area of San Carlos. [@3]
