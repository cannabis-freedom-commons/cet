Police in the Cordillera Administrative Region reported confiscating suspected
marijuana worth P364 million in the region between 1 January and 18 February
2026. [@1] Regional police chief Brig. Gen. Ericson Dilag gave the figures at a
press briefing at the Police Regional Office CAR headquarters in La Trinidad,
Benguet. [@1] Operatives took more than 1.07 million grams of dried marijuana
leaves, more than 1.22 million marijuana plants and 35,800 seedlings. [@1] Dilag
said the haul came from 182 operations during the six-week period. [@1]

He said the total was nearly triple the P123 million in suspected marijuana
seized in 130 operations in the same period of 2025. [@1] Dilag attributed the
increase to drone surveillance, saying that making police hike up a mountain
every time would be difficult. [@1]

The regional police set up a dedicated unit of 49 drone operators, named the
Smart Quadrotor Unit for Advanced Deployment, Detection, Reconnaissance,
Observation, and Networked Enforcement, or SQUADRONE, to detect marijuana
planting sites beneath dense forest canopies. [@1] Dilag said SQUADRONE was
deployed in the fourth quarter of 2025 after its operators were trained. [@1]
GMA Integrated News reported that SQUADRONE has more than 60 drone units and
that PRO-CAR plans to acquire more for internal security and search and rescue
work. [@2] Dilag said marijuana cultivation has become a source of livelihood
for some residents of far-flung areas, with financiers funding the plantations.
[@2]
