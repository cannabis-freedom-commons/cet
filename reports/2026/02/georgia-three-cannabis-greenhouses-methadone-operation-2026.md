Georgia's Interior Ministry detained both Georgian citizens and foreign
nationals. [@1] In recent days police carried out operations against organised
crime in Tbilisi and other regions. [@2] Giorgi Bukhrashvili, director of the
Adjara police department, announced the results at a ministry briefing. [@2] The
ministry did not disclose how many people were detained. [@4]

Officers found three greenhouses used for cultivating narcotics. [@1] Cannabis
was being grown in the greenhouses illegally and in especially large quantities,
the ministry said. [@1] Police seized the materials needed to grow the drug.
[@1] Searches of the detainees' homes and of locations they identified produced
drugs prepared for sale and materials for packaging them. [@1] The same
operations also turned up firearms, automatic weapons and ammunition. [@1]
Officers also seized up to 28 kilograms of methadone. [@1] The ministry said it
was the largest quantity of methadone Georgian police had ever seized. [@2]

The investigation covers illegal drug production, trafficking, cultivation and
firearms offences, the ministry said. [@3] Those offences carry up to 20 years
in prison or life imprisonment. [@4]
