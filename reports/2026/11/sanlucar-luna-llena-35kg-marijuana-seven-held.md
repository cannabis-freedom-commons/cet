Guardia Civil and Policía Nacional dismantled a large-scale cocaine trafficking
organisation in a joint operation in the province of Cádiz. [@1,7,8] Seven people
were arrested, six of them with previous drug-trafficking records, in the
operation named Luna Llena-Manantia. [@1,4,7] The operation was directed by the duty
judge of the Mixed Court number 4 of Sanlúcar de Barrameda. [@1,4,6,7] The inquiry
began after a van carrying 33 bundles of cocaine was intercepted on the AP-4
motorway near Los Palacios, Seville, and its only occupant was arrested. [@1,7,8] The van had left a home in the area around Sanlúcar de Barrameda before investigators followed it to the AP-4. [@8]
With judicial authorisation officers carried out eleven simultaneous searches of
homes in Sanlúcar de Barrameda and Chipiona, where the other six people were
detained. [@1,4,5,7,8]

Alongside 1,823 kilograms of cocaine, officers seized 35.35 kilograms of dried
marijuana buds, vacuum-packed and ready for distribution, and 171,300 euros in
cash. [@1,4,5,6,7,8] Officers also impounded nine vehicles, eleven motorcycles and a jet
ski. [@2,4,6,7,8] Officers also found hunting weapons, which were still being examined. [@4,7] Two of the nine vehicles had been stolen. [@4,7] Two underground hideouts, or zulos, built and fitted out to conceal
the drugs were found on registered properties. [@1,4,6,7] One had previously been used
to grow marijuana and held 20 further bundles of cocaine packaged identically to
those in the van. [@3,4,7,8]

The network was based around the mouth of the Guadalquivir river and had reused
the infrastructure and contacts it already maintained for marijuana cultivation
and maritime hashish trafficking to expand into large-scale cocaine transport
and distribution. [@1,4,5,7,8] Its logistics structure allowed it to bring, store, guard
and move the drug from Cádiz to points across Europe. [@1] The group used
surveillance cameras, drones, watchmen and lookouts known as aguadores to
protect its activities. [@1,6] The group changed phones, vehicles and operating sites frequently and altered its routes, making diversionary movements to check for police surveillance. [@6] The operation was run jointly by the EDOA
(Organised Crime and Anti-Drug Unit) of the Guardia Civil in Cádiz and the GRECO
(Organised Crime Response Group) of the Policía Nacional in Cádiz. [@1,4,7] The searches were supported by the Guardia Civil's reserve and security groups (GRS), its rapid action group (GAR), the Cádiz USECIC, the dog unit and the prevention and reaction units (UPR) of Jerez and El Puerto de Santa María. [@4]
