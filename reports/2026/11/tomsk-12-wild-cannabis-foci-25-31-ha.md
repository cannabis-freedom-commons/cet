In 2026 Rosselkhoznadzor inspectors recorded 12 agricultural plots in Tomsk
Oblast overgrown with a drug-containing weed, with a combined area of 25.31
hectares. [@1] The agency said all 12 foci were eliminated. [@2] Viktor
Matrosov, deputy head of state land supervision at Rosselkhoznadzor's Siberian
interregional administration, reported the year's results at a meeting of the
Tomsk District antinarcotic commission on 24 November 2026. [@2]

The coordinates of the patches were sent to the regional drug-control authority
and to local councils so that action could be taken promptly. [@1] Tomsk Oblast
was declared free of wild cannabis on agricultural land in 2026. [@1]
Rosselkhoznadzor said systematic monitoring of farmland reveals unused plots and
allows timely action against cannabis growing on them. [@1]

For 2027 the commission planned to check whether rural settlements were ready to
destroy drug-containing plants at the start of the next farming season and to
continue building a database of where they grow. [@2] Owners of overgrown plots
were to face responsibility, with closer cooperation planned between the
interior ministry, prosecutors and Rosselkhoznadzor. [@1] Under Russian law,
landowners face administrative and criminal liability for illegally cultivating
prohibited plants. [@1]
