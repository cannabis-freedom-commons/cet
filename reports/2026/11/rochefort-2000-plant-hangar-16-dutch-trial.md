Sixteen Dutch nationals, most of them absent, were called before the court in
Rochefort on Tuesday 17 November 2026 in a major cannabis-cultivation case. [@1]
The facts date from January 2020 to January 2021 and concern a plantation of
more than 2,000 plants set up in a hangar, fed by a clandestine electricity
connection. [@1] Police intervened in January 2021 after several months of
surveillance. [@1] All the defendants are suspected of having taken part in the
activity to different degrees. [@2]

For the prosecution the investigation revealed a well-oiled organisation: phone
records placed several defendants near Rochefort nearly 1,400 times, cameras
monitored the site remotely, and fingerprints and movements completed the case.
[@1] A weekend DJ in the road-sign sector was presented as one of the site's
keepers. [@2] He said he had come only twice. [@1] Another, active in the
international vehicle trade, admitted driving several Bulgarian nationals to the
industrial site and picking them up six days later. [@1]

The prosecutor demanded three years' imprisonment and a fine for the seven
defendants considered the main actors, and two years for the others. [@1] ORES,
the regional electricity operator, claims 218,000 euros for the stolen
electricity, a sum whose allocation the defence contests. [@1] Nearly six years
after the events, several defence lawyers invoked a violation of the
reasonable-time guarantee, recalling that the indictment chamber noted in 2023
that the investigation had shown no progress since January 2022. [@1] One
defendant, 22 at the time of the facts, has since earned a commerce degree,
married and is expecting a child; his lawyer asked for a declaration of guilt or
community work, while others sought acquittal, suspension of the pronouncement
or a suspended sentence. [@2] Judgment is expected on 5 January. [@1]
