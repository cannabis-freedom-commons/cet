Antonino Precaj, 33, was jailed for 27 months at Manchester Crown Court in
September 2026 after admitting being concerned in the production of cannabis.
[@1] Precaj later sought permission to appeal, arguing that his sentence should
have been suspended because of his previous good character, remorse, family
circumstances and strong prospects of rehabilitation. [@1] The renewed
application for leave to appeal was refused, leaving the 27-month prison
sentence in place. [@1] Judges concluded that appropriate punishment could only
be achieved by an immediate custodial term and rejected claims that the sentence
was excessive. [@1]

Court documents show Precaj travelled from London to the Ramsbottom area in
February 2025 to assist in establishing a cannabis farm on Bolton Street. [@1]
The Court of Appeal heard he was seen visiting a hydroponics supplier before
travelling to the property, which was later converted into a cannabis
cultivation site. [@1] Judges said Precaj recruited another Albanian national,
Argit Asllani, to work at the grow. [@1] Flight details were sent to Asllani
before he travelled to the UK in March 2025, and the court found Precaj likely
collected him from the airport before directing him to the Ramsbottom address.
[@1] Messages showed Asllani regularly reported to Precaj about the progress of
the operation, including sending photographs of cannabis plants and seeking
instructions on maintaining the grow. [@1] When officers raided the Bolton
Street property on April 22, 2025, they discovered 70 cannabis plants and found
the electricity supply had been bypassed. [@1] Asllani was arrested at the scene
after being found asleep inside the building. [@1] The potential yield of the
crop was estimated at between 2.7kg and 5.17kg of cannabis. [@1]

The case formed part of the wider Operation Bedham investigation, which
dismantled an organised crime group responsible for supplying Class A and Class
B drugs across Greater Manchester. [@1] At sentencing, Judge Johnston found
Precaj had played a significant management role in an operation capable of
producing substantial quantities of cannabis for commercial supply. [@1] The
appeal court highlighted as an aggravating feature Precaj's involvement in
recruiting and directing Asllani, over whom he exercised control. [@1] While he
had no previous convictions, had indefinite leave to remain in the UK and was
the father of a young child, the judge concluded the seriousness warranted
immediate custody. [@1] In the earlier Manchester Crown Court hearing, Precaj
confessed to being concerned in the production of cannabis. [@2]
