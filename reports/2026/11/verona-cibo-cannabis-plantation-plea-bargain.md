Pier Paolo Spinazzè, known as street artist Cibo, plea-bargained 1 year, 9
months and 10 days in prison for cannabis plants he grew with a friend in two
wooded areas near San Giovanni Lupatoto. [@1] The plea agreement also included a 3,400-euro fine, with the prison term suspended. [@3] The case ended on the morning of
19 November before judge Clara Aprea, where the prosecution and defence
presented a plea agreement conditional on an apology from Spinazzè. [@1]
Spinazzè was in court and, as required by the deal, handed over a handwritten
note apologising to the community. [@1] By 26 November the appeal had received 73 donations of between 5 and 100 euros and raised almost 2,000 euros towards a 6,000-euro target. [@3] After the sentence Spinazzè launched a GoFundMe appeal to cover his legal costs, saying he wanted to draw attention to cannabis legalisation and its importance for therapeutic use. [@3] The street artist, known for his
anti-swastika writings, had been arrested at the end of September and said he
grew the plants for personal use, saying marijuana helped relieve pain from the
multiple sclerosis he suffers from. [@1] Spinazzè said the search had also taken the medical cannabis he received on prescription from the national health service, leaving him without his therapy since the arrest. [@3]

Finance officers of the Verona provincial command arrested two local residents
in flagrante after finding them in two cannabis plantations of about 200 square
metres in public woodland in San Giovanni Lupatoto. [@2] The pair were located
after helicopter overflights by the Venice air unit spotted the plantations
hidden in vegetation, followed by months of observation coordinated by the
Verona prosecutor. [@2] Officers seized the plants, up to two metres tall with a
total gross weight of over 60 kg, together with the equipment found. [@2] Later
house searches seized further cannabis plants at one arrestee's home and about
53 grams of marijuana at the other's. [@2] The plantations used a centralised
irrigation system fed by water-collection drums. [@2] Hidden camera traps in the
woods remotely monitored anyone approaching the crops to detect checks in time.
[@2] The two arrestees were investigated for alleged cultivation and unlawful
possession of drugs. [@2]

The judge validated the arrest, and Cibo remained free without any restrictive
measure pending the hearing adjourned to 19 November. [@2] After the hearing he
criticised those who attacked him after his arrest, starting with Forza Italia
MEP Flavio Tosi, against whom he said he had filed an aggravated-defamation
damages claim. [@1]
