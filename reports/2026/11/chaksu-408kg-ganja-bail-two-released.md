The Rajasthan High Court in Jaipur granted bail to two men accused of
transporting nearly 408 kg of ganja, a commercial quantity under the NDPS Act.
[@1] Police stopped the two men’s vehicle on a national highway in June 2025 on
a secret tip and allegedly recovered 407.97 kg of ganja in 14 bags. [@1] Both
accused have been in custody since the June 2025 stop. [@1] Police at Chaksu,
Jaipur registered the case as FIR No. 233/2025 after the highway stop and
search. [@2]

Their first bail pleas were withdrawn, one with liberty to return after the
seizure officer testified, and these second pleas were filed after he was
examined. [@1] Counsel argued that the officer admitted in cross-examination
that no document showed the information was sent to his superior, as Section 42
of the NDPS Act requires. [@1] The defence also pointed to the absence of
independent witnesses, 21 samples drawn from a single bag, and samples reaching
the FSL 14 days after they were drawn with no explanation. [@1] In a reportable
order, Justice Anil Kumar Upman said the bar on bail in Section 37 of the NDPS
Act is not absolute and must be read reasonably. [@1]

The Court noted their custody of more than 16 months and a trial likely to take
time, and allowed both pleas without commenting on the merits. [@1] Each accused
must furnish a personal bond of Rs 5 lakh with two sureties of Rs 2.5 lakh each,
mark attendance at the local police station every month and keep their mobile
phones active. [@1] The case is Deepak and Himanshu Mata @ Harsu Mata v. State
of Rajasthan, decided on 2 November 2026 as citation 2026:RJ-JP:44902. [@2]
