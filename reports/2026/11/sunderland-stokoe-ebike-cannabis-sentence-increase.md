Gloria Stephenson, 86, was walking her daughter's dog across a zebra crossing in
Sunderland on 16 May 2025 when Billy Stokoe's e-bike hit her. [@1] She was
pronounced dead at the scene. [@2] Stokoe, then 19, held only a provisional
licence and was riding one-handed while holding a mobile phone on a bike whose
front brake was missing. [@2] He briefly checked on Mrs Stephenson before riding
off, then changed his clothes, hid the bike at a friend's house and handed
himself in at a police station just over an hour later. [@3] A blood sample
taken when he handed himself in showed he was three and a half times over the
legal limit for cannabis. [@2]

Stokoe admitted causing death by dangerous driving, causing death by dangerous
driving while unlicensed, causing death by driving while uninsured and driving
with cannabis over the specified limit. [@2] Newcastle Crown Court sentenced him
to six years and nine months' detention on 15 May 2026. [@2] The Attorney
General, Ellie Reeves, referred the sentence to the Court of Appeal under the
unduly lenient sentence scheme. [@2] The increase followed a campaign by Mrs
Stephenson's family. [@1]

On 17 November 2026 three judges quashed the original sentence and replaced it
with eight years and six months' detention. [@3] Lord Justice Stuart-Smith,
sitting with Mrs Justice Farbey and Judge Mark Lucraft KC, said there should
have been a “significant” uplift to the starting point of the sentence. [@3] Mrs
Stephenson's daughter Julie Francis said no sentence would bring her mother
back. [@4] She said the original sentence had been unduly lenient. [@4]
