Rafael Quintero Mellizo and Jorge Gamboa Anchico, both Colombian nationals, were
detained on a Friday in Punta Arenas. [@1] A Carabineros patrol near midday saw
the drivers of a blue Hyundai Accent and a light-blue Suzuki Swift exchange
something between the two vehicles at a road intersection in villa Alfredo
Lorca. [@1] According to the formalization, Quintero handed bags of drugs to
Gamboa, who passed money to him. [@1] Bags of marijuana lay on the Hyundai's
passenger seat, the car gave off a strong smell of the substance, and Gamboa
held two bags in his right hand. [@1] Neither man carried identity documents, so
they were taken to the First Commissary, where OS7 staff ran field tests, and
the detention was completed at 17:20. [@1]

Prosecutors attributed to Quintero 2.1 grams of cocaine, 3.6 grams of marijuana,
ketamine and more than 236,000 pesos in cash. [@1] They attributed to Gamboa 5.1
grams of cocaine, 1.2 grams of marijuana and 142,000 pesos. [@1] Officers also
seized four telephones and the Suzuki. [@1] Prosecutor Wendoline Acuña
formalized both as perpetrators of completed small-quantity trafficking under
article 4 of Law 20,000. [@1]

At Saturday's hearing the Guarantee Court of Punta Arenas rejected the
prosecution's request for overnight home arrest for both men. [@1] Judge Ignacio
Low Miranda accepted the defence argument, based on the police's own account,
that Gamboa took part only as a buyer. [@1] The judge noted that the drug was in
Gamboa's hand, that no substances or dosing implements were found in his
vehicle, that his companion carried nothing, and that the amount did not rule
out personal consumption. [@1] He found that neither man had a criminal record
and that the quantities were minimal, so deprivation of liberty was
disproportionate. [@1] He rejected the claimed lack of family roots based solely
on their status as foreigners, noting the defence proved family ties in the
region. [@1]

Both men were left under a national travel ban and monthly signing. [@1] They
must sign on the last Friday of each month at the First Commissary from 27
November, and the investigation will run 90 days. [@1]
