Leah Robinson, 25, a prison officer at HMP Lowdham Grange in Nottinghamshire,
was jailed for 28 months at Nottingham Crown Court. [@3,5] She had pleaded guilty
to conspiracy to supply a Class B drug and to misconduct in public office. [@1,5,6]
She was caught on 16 August 2023, when a drugs dog indicated towards her as she
arrived for a shift and a search found cannabis resin in a latex glove hidden
inside her body. [@2,5] She told staff that a second package of drugs was in her
car outside the prison. [@1,6]

About 2 kg of cannabis resin was found on her. [@3,6] The court heard the resin
was worth between £6,000 and £12,000. [@3,6] Judge Mark Watson said Robinson had
met couriers at least six times and estimated she brought in between 1.1 kg and
2.2 kg of cannabis. [@2,5] Robinson collected cannabis from couriers in Nottingham
car parks, including a B&Q car park. [@2] Prosecutors said codewords such as
"Lemon Drizzle", "Mario" and "Milk" were used for the drug. [@1,5,6]

Patryk Dziewiatkowski, 32, the inmate who headed the operation, was jailed for
four years and seven months. [@2,5] Serving prisoner Jordan Jones was jailed for
two years and one month. [@2] Three other defendants, Oscar Ingram, Bilal Khan
and Casileigh Jones, were also sentenced for acting as drugs couriers. [@3]
Dziewiatkowski's mother, Malgorzata Szubrych, received 18 months suspended for
two years for money laundering. [@2] Szubrych also relayed messages between the pair and made money transfers as part of the network. [@6] Robinson was paid £4,260, while Szubrych's
bank account recorded £41,190 in unexplained credits. [@1] Judge Watson said
Robinson had been "groomed, corrupted and recruited" by Dziewiatkowski, with
whom she was "infatuated". [@3,5] Dziewiatkowski was released in July 2023 and deported to Poland, and Robinson travelled four times to eastern Europe to visit him. [@6] They became close in early 2023 while Dziewiatkowski was in prison and exchanged romantic messages by phone. [@6] Her barrister said she was 21 when the offending
began and had been diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder after
witnessing two suicides while working at the prison. [@1] Robinson's mitigating barrister was Emma Swindell, who told the court Robinson had come to court expecting a custodial sentence and took responsibility for her actions. [@5] Seven defendants were
sentenced in the same hearing. [@4] A Proceeds of Crime Act hearing, which could strip Robinson of cash and assets, was scheduled for a future date. [@5]
