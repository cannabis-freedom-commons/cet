The Audiencia Provincial de Almería cut by €270,982 the fine on a man who grew
marijuana to sell in the city, reducing it from €350,000 to €79,018 while
keeping his two-and-a-half-year prison term for a public-health offence of
notoria importancia (a quantity threshold that aggravates the sentence). [@1]
The court partly upheld the convict's appeal and also lowered the fines for
electricity fraud and usurpation. [@1] It sided with the other defendant, who
had been cleared of all charges and appealed to recover the Audi that the
first-instance ruling had ordered confiscated, and ordered the car returned
without charging the costs of its seizure. [@1]

The Policía Nacional seized 2,722 plants and 208 cuttings in the operation
behind the case. [@1] The searches took place on 12 April 2023 at six properties
in Almería, four holding plants, cuttings or drying material, along with lamps,
transformers, extractors and air conditioners. [@1] At the other two properties
officers seized, among other items, €1,410 in cash, two electric scooters, two
mobile phones, a bicycle, a scale and a piece of bud. [@1] Analysis of the drugs
gave 73.18 kg of cannabis and a further 7.76 kg of leaves at net dry weight, and
the judgment put the substance's value on the black market at about €158,038.
[@1] The four cultivation properties had irregular electricity connections, two
of them owned by Andalusia's housing and rehabilitation agency and used without
its consent. [@1]

The court applied two mitigating circumstances already recognised at first
instance — the man admitted the facts at the start of the trial and the case sat
idle for over a year for reasons beyond the defendants' control — and lowered
the fines by one degree. [@1] The electricity-fraud and usurpation fines fell to
one month and 15 days each at €18 a day, from nine and six months. [@1] It kept
the two-and-a-half-year term as proportionate to the seizure, almost seven times
the notoria-importancia threshold, and the difficulty of running the crops, and
rejected a drug-dependency mitigation. [@1] It found unproven the first-instance
finding that the car was bought with drug money and used for trafficking, and
that it belonged to the convict, accepting that the absolved woman was the
owner, a position the prosecution fully backed. [@1]
