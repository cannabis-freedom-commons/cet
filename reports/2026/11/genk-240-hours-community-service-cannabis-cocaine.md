A 35-year-old man from Genk was sentenced by the judge in Tongeren to 240 hours
of community service for dealing cannabis and cocaine. [@1] About two months
earlier he had suffered a brain haemorrhage in prison after being beaten up.
[@1]

The case came to light in July when police stopped a drug user after a deal.
[@1] The user named the 35-year-old as his supplier, and investigators raided
his home in Genk. [@1] They found cocaine, cannabis and cash at the home. [@1]
The prosecutor said the man was dealing in an association because the buyer
stated he was always supplied by someone else. [@1]

The Tongeren prosecutor pointed to the man's record of 16 previous convictions,
five of them for drugs, and requested an effective three-year prison sentence.
[@1] His lawyer asked for community service or a conditionally suspended
sentence. [@1] The lawyer pointed to an incident weeks before the hearing at
Hasselt prison, where the man had been held since his arrest. [@1] He said the
man was beaten black and blue, suffered a brain haemorrhage and had only left
hospital two weeks earlier. [@1]

The judge found the man fit to work and said his future prospects should not be
compromised. [@1] He was given 240 hours of community service, to be replaced by
24 months in prison if he does not carry it out. [@1] He was also ordered to pay
an 8,000-euro fine and a 2,140-euro forfeiture. [@1]
