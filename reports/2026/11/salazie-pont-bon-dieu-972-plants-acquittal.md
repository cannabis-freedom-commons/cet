Surya Kumar Auckloo, who lives at Belvédère, Lalmatie, was acquitted by the
Supreme Court nine years after his arrest in a cannabis cultivation case. [@1]
The judgment was delivered on Thursday 19 November 2026 by judge L. Aujayeb, who
found that the prosecution had not proved guilt beyond reasonable doubt. [@1]
Sandeep Kumar Domun, the first accused, was sentenced on 25 November 2026 to 14
years of penal servitude and a fine of Rs 50,000. [@2]

The case began on 10 August 2017, when officers of the Anti-Drug and Smuggling
Unit (ADSU) raided a market-garden plantation at Salazie, Pont-Bon-Dieu. [@1]
They uprooted 972 cannabis plants at four different places. [@1] The seizure
weighed nearly 5.4 kg and was estimated at Rs 2,916,000, TopFM reported. [@2]
Domun was seen pulling weeds around the plants and, after his arrest, led
officers to three more places where plants were growing. [@2]

Domun implicated Auckloo and said they had grown the cannabis together; Auckloo
was arrested the same day. [@1] Searches of the two men's homes found nothing
incriminating. [@1] Auckloo told the court he was working as a mason at
Fond-du-Sac on the day of his arrest and had received several calls from Domun
asking him to come to the site. [@1] He said he was arrested about 300 metres
from the family land and that police assaulted him and forced him to admit the
land was his. [@1] The officers denied the allegations. [@1]

The judge found gaps in the investigation: photographs produced in court showed
differences between the land Auckloo farmed and where the plants were found, so
the court did not rule out two separate plots. [@1] The court found Auckloo's
testimony coherent and sincere and did not exclude that he had been assaulted by
police. [@1] Domun had pleaded guilty to illegal cannabis cultivation with an
allegation of trafficking, and the judge held that the number of plants
indicated cultivation for commercial purposes. [@2] Time already spent in
detention will be deducted in full from his sentence. [@2]
