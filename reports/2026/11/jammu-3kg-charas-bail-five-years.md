The Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh High Court granted bail to a man accused of
possessing three kilograms of charas who had remained in custody for nearly five
years without conclusion of his trial. [@1] The successive bail application was
filed by Idrees Ahmed Khan, who faced trial under Sections 8/20, 29 and 60 of
the NDPS Act in a case registered by the Narcotics Control Bureau, Jammu. [@1] A
single bench of Justice Mohd Yousuf Wani noted that Khan had been in custody
since January 16, 2022, and that the trial was likely to take further time. [@1]
The court held that his long incarceration of five years warranted bail subject
to reasonable terms and conditions. [@1] The High Court directed that Khan be
released on bail on personal and surety bonds of two lakh rupees each, with two
near relatives each liable for one lakh rupees. [@1]

According to the prosecution, the NCB received information on January 16, 2022,
that two people, including Khan, were transporting narcotics in a Tata Sumo from
Srinagar towards Jammu and were likely to reach Ban Toll Plaza, Nagrota. [@1]
Officers intercepted the vehicle and recovered four newspaper-wrapped packets
from a cavity in the rear door containing three kilograms of charas, excluding
the packing material. [@1] Khan allegedly told investigators that Nazir Khan had
handed him the consignment for delivery to co-accused Sirajbhai Gafurbhai Vahora
in Jammu. [@1] The NCB filed a complaint against Khan and two other accused; on
March 11, 2023, the trial court framed charges under Sections 8/20, 29 and 60 of
the NDPS Act against Khan and Sirajbhai and discharged the third accused, Mohd
Asif Wani. [@1]

The trial court rejected his first bail application on June 6, 2025, because the
restrictions under Section 37 of the NDPS Act were attracted. [@1] Khan argued
that only three of six prosecution witnesses had been examined by September 25,
2025, despite the trial court directing the prosecution to lead evidence in May
2023. [@1] The High Court recorded that all six witnesses had since been
examined and arguments were fixed for November 20, 2026, but found the trial
likely to take further time. [@1] The court acknowledged that the Section 37 bar
was attracted but held that the period already spent in custody could not be
overlooked and granted bail. [@1]
