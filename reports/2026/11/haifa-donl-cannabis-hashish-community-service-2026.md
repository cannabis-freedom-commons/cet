Aviran Donl of Haifa was convicted in the Haifa Magistrate's Court of possessing
a dangerous drug not for personal use, driving under the influence of alcohol
and driving with a licence expired for more than two years. [@2] The drugs were
found on 26 April 2024 in the car he was driving on Ibn Gabirol Street in Haifa.
[@2] Officers seized about 1.2 kg of cannabis divided into 90 packages and about
100 g of hashish in 18 aluminium wrappings. [@1] Two vials of liquid cannabis
extract of 9.50 ml each and 0.6413 g of m-METHYLMETHCATHINONE, a stimulant that
is not counted as cannabis, were also seized. [@2]

Four probation reports were commissioned, all of them negative. [@1] He said he
committed the offences because he was in financial difficulty and wanted money
for his divorce proceedings. [@1] He joined a treatment group at the probation
service but later missed meetings, cut contact and stopped participating. [@1]
The probation service declined to recommend treatment and urged a tangible
punishment instead. [@2]

Prosecutors asked for at least ten months of actual imprisonment and sought to
have him declared a drug dealer. [@1] His lawyer Sagie Akko said the amount of
cannabis seized was not among the highest and asked for a term of community
service. [@1] Judge Tal Tadmor-Zamir set a range from community service to 18
months in prison, citing the harm to public health, the division into dozens of
packages and about three years of driving on an invalid licence; six earlier
convictions counted against him, his confession in his favour. [@1]

She refused to declare him a drug dealer because no profit from the drugs had
been proved. [@1] Judgment was given on 25 November 2026. [@2] The court imposed
eight months' imprisonment, to be served as community service in Tel Aviv. [@2]
She also gave him six months of suspended imprisonment for three years. [@2] His
driving licence was disqualified for six months and he was fined 10,000 shekels.
[@2] Some 1,900 shekels seized at his arrest were forfeited to the state. [@2]
An appeal was allowed within 45 days, after which the drugs were to be
destroyed. [@2]
