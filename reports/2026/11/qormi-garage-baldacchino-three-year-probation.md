Renald Baldacchino, 38, also known as 'il-Garru', of Qormi was found guilty of
supplying cocaine and heroin and of possessing cannabis, among other drug
offences. [@1] The court gave him a probation order instead of a prison sentence
because he had completed a drug rehabilitation programme. [@2] He was placed
under a three-year probation order, with his probation officer ordered to submit
written progress reports every six months. [@2]

The case dates to 16 March 2018, when police raided a garage in Qormi where
Baldacchino lived and nine people inside were arrested. [@1] The search
uncovered weighing scales, self-sealing bags, straws, pipes and foil and other
drug paraphernalia. [@1] Police found two firearms, pepper spray, nunchucks and
€3,000 in cash on Baldacchino. [@1] An expert analysis found cocaine on several
items, heroin on foil, and 26.05g of cocaine at about 18% purity. [@1]

Two of the men arrested during the raid confirmed they regularly bought cocaine
and heroin from Baldacchino and testified that he prepared crack cocaine and
provided tables, pipes and bottles so buyers could consume the drugs on-site.
[@2] One witness said Baldacchino weighed the drug and sold it for €20 or €30,
while customers consumed it inside the garage. [@1] He also testified that
Baldacchino provided tables, pipes, bottles and straws to facilitate consumption
on the premises. [@1] Baldacchino denied selling drugs, saying he used heroin
and cannabis to ease pain from serious health problems. [@1] He claimed the
scales were for weighing vitamins for his reptiles and that the plastic bags
were lunch bags. [@1] The court rejected his explanations, finding that witness
testimony, expert findings and the diary and phone contents established he had
been operating a drug-selling business. [@1]

The proceedings had been referred to the Drug Court mechanism under the law on
rehabilitation of persons caught with drugs. [@1] In October 2023, the president
of the Rehabilitation Board informed the court that Baldacchino had successfully
completed the programme. [@1] Magistrate Caroline Farrugia Frendo said his
progress showed he was capable of changing direction and responding positively
to rehabilitation. [@1] The court stressed that this did not diminish the
seriousness of the offences, but considered an effective prison sentence was not
the most appropriate measure. [@1] Baldacchino was warned that breaching the
order or reoffending could result in him being sentenced for these offences.
[@1] The court ordered the €3,000 confiscated and Baldacchino to pay €2,215.72
in court-appointed expert expenses within six months. [@1]
