The Second Criminal Court at the Hama justice palace in Syria sentenced one man
to death for drug trafficking on 24 November 2026, after his repeat offence
triggered the harsher penalty provided by law. [@1] Hama's prosecution called
the verdict a first of its kind against a drug dealer in the governorate. [@1]
Muhammad Ahmad al-Nu'asan, the general attorney in Hama, said drug trafficking
normally carries life imprisonment but the sentence is increased for trafficking
in schools or prisons and for repeat offenders. [@1]

The court said it based the ruling on Article 39 of Law No. 2 of 1993. [@1] It
said the seized quantities were meant for trade and comprised three kufuf of
hashish, 6,000 Captagon pills, 200 grams of narcotic substances and an
electronic scale for weighing them. [@1] Legal expert Ahmed Ibrahim said the
courts move from a lighter sentence or life imprisonment to death when a
previous conviction has not deterred the defendant. [@1]

Syrian Interior Ministry spokesman Nour al-Din al-Babaa praised the deterrent
judgments against those convicted of trafficking and distributing drugs. [@1] He
said they crowned continuing field and investigative work by the Anti-Narcotics
Directorate to pursue criminal networks, dismantle them and refer those involved
to the courts. [@1]
