A young man convicted of driving under the influence of psychoactive substances
has lost his appeal against a suspended prison sentence. [@1] The Court of
Appeal Cluj rejected the appeal on 19 November 2026 and upheld the sentence
given by the Turda court. [@1] The sentence is one year, eight months and ten
days in prison, suspended, with supervision for three years. [@1] He was also
banned from driving for two years and ordered to perform 60 days of unpaid
community work. [@1] The decision is final, and he must pay 500 lei in appeal
costs. [@1]

The judgment describes him as 23 years old at the time of the first-instance
trial. [@1] The offence took place on the night of 14 to 15 April 2023. [@1] He
left Florești with three passengers in his car, intending to reach another town.
[@1] At about 02:23 on 15 April, police identified the car on strada Băii in
Câmpia Turzii, at number 10. [@1] Officers saw that he had dilated pupils, spoke
incoherently, was drowsy and had delayed motor reactions. [@1] In his hoodie
pocket police found several envelopes with a crystal-type substance and plant
material smelling of cannabis. [@1] A bag of plant material was also found on
the back seat of the car. [@1] He told investigators he had smoked cannabis
three days earlier and had snorted a line of crystal in Câmpia Turzii after
stopping the car. [@1]

The toxicology report cited in the judgment found CMC and THC-COOH, a cannabis
metabolite, in his blood. [@3] Medico-legal experts concluded that he had
consumed CMC before the trip and was under its influence at the wheel. [@2] The
Turda court sentenced him on 12 August to one year and four months in prison for
driving under the influence of psychoactive substances. [@1] The court annulled
the suspension of a previous one-year, one-month sentence for qualified theft
and merged the two. [@1] In the appeal he asked for a lower sentence and for the
driving ban to be cut from two years to one. [@1] The judges held that the
sentence reflected the seriousness of the offence and his criminal record and
that a reduction would not be an adequate individualisation. [@1] The conviction
concerns driving under the influence of psychoactive substances. [@3]
