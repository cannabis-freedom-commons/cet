A Wicklow fisherman was jailed for three and a half years for attempting to
smuggle more than €400,000 worth of cannabis through Dublin Airport. [@1] Seamus
Carroll, of Avondale Crescent, Arklow, Co Wicklow, pleaded guilty last May to
importation of cannabis at Dublin airport on 25 October 2024. [@1] Dublin
Circuit Criminal Court heard that the 35-year-old carried out the offence after
serious drug-debt intimidation including threats to his family, violent assaults
and the burning out of his mother's car. [@1]

Customs officers identified Carroll as a figure of suspicion, searched his bags
and found 21kg of cannabis with a then estimated street value of €422,920. [@1]
Garda Jennifer Craven agreed that the official valuation had changed from
€20,000 to €14,000 per kilo, reducing the estimated value to about €296,000.
[@1] Garda Craven accepted Carroll's account that he acted under duress towards
drugs gangs to whom he owed a €50,000 debt. [@1] She agreed that his mother was
threatened with having to sell the family home to pay the debt and that her car
was burnt out shortly afterwards. [@1]

Judge Elma Sheahan found that Carroll acted under duress because of serious
intimidation of him, his family and his then partner. [@1] The judge noted his
guilty plea and genuine remorse, his long work history and that he is the father
of a young child. [@1] She accepted Carroll did not offend for direct monetary
gain but to reduce a debt he had built up. [@1] Judge Sheahan backdated the
sentence to July to take account of time spent in custody on remand. [@1]
Carroll had two previous District Court convictions for drug dealing, from 2022
and 2023, along with some public order convictions. [@1]
