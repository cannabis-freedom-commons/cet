Manitoba's director of criminal property forfeiture has asked a court to forfeit
five properties it says were used to produce cannabis for the black market,
under Health Canada licences limited to personal medical use. [@1,4] The statement
of claim, filed in the Court of King's Bench of Manitoba, says the RCMP received
information in April 2022 that medical cannabis and illicit tobacco were being
sold from Health Canada-approved production sites in the province. [@1,3,4] The RCMP
investigation was named Project Defund and used surveillance, production orders
and warrants, the claim says. [@1,2] The properties are two in Winnipeg's Maples
neighbourhood, one in South Pointe, and one each in the rural municipalities of
Brokenhead and Piney. [@1,2] The claim says police identified a network of people
who gave falsified information to financial institutions to obtain mortgages and
used the money to buy the properties. [@1,4] In one case a defendant said he was
an assistant manager earning $48,000 a year when his only legitimate income was
Canada Pension Plan benefits, the claim says. [@1,3] The Malden Close bungalow was bought in 2017 for $309,000, the filing says. [@3] In another, a defendant gave
paystubs and a T4 showing $149,999.98 a year at a Winnipeg meat processor where
she was not employed at the time, the claim says. [@1] The property, a parcel with a bungalow, garage and shop in Beausejour, was mortgaged in 2023 on a claim of employment at a Winnipeg sausage manufacturer, the filing says. [@3] The people obtained
Health Canada licences to grow cannabis for their own medical use, then produced
quantities far exceeding what a person could reasonably consume and sold the
cannabis on the black market, using the proceeds to pay mortgages and buy more
properties, the province alleges. [@1]

At the RM of Piney property police observed about 3,200 plants, above the 1,677
the licences allowed for personal use. [@1,2,3] Huang and Han own the Piney property, where a 10,506-square-foot building held the 1,677 plants their personal-use licences allowed, the filing says. [@3] Business records found at that
property referenced cannabis sales above $320,000 a year. [@1] The claim says the RM of Piney council approved a conditional use and variance order in 2021 permitting medical cannabis production at the property, but not for commercial use. [@2] Police executed
search warrants at four of the properties in December 2024. [@1,2] In the
basements of two Winnipeg properties officers found a fully operational cannabis
grow operation with hundreds of plants. [@1] At the Brokenhead property, hydro
consumption was significantly higher than normal usage and consistent with a
large-scale cannabis grow operation, the claim says. [@1] The claim says police seized cellphones from some of the properties and found conversations and photographs about the production and trafficking of cannabis and contraband cigarettes. [@2] Officers found
cannabis and contraband cigarettes, including approximately 22 pounds (about 10
kilograms) of cannabis bud at the RM of Piney property. [@1,2,3] At one property, police found handwritten lists of customer names and currency amounts, the claim says. [@2] At the home on Mandan Road in the Maples, police found 260 cartons of contraband cigarettes alongside the lists of customer names and money owed, the filing says. [@3] The claim also notes an absence of signs of personal cannabis use, such as rolling papers, bongs and pipes, at some of the properties. [@2]

Seven people are named as defendants, along with financial institutions that
hold registered interests in the mortgages. [@1,2,3] The defendants are Van Hung Ha, 57, Weihao Chen, 56, Bingli Mai, 47, Xu Huang, 37, Jiang Lin Lu, 52, and Kim Tu Ha and Yi Han. [@3] The claim alleges falsifying an
employment record, possession of cannabis for the purpose of distributing, and
selling cannabis without authorization. [@1,2] On 13 November 2026 Court of King's
Bench Justice Brian Bowman granted an interim order barring anyone from selling
the five properties for 60 days or until the court orders otherwise. [@1,2,4] No
statement of defence had been filed and the claims had not been proven in court.
