Deondre Burgin, 22, of Covington, Kentucky, was sentenced to 5.5 years in
federal prison on drug, firearm and stolen-vehicle charges. [@3] Chief U.S.
District Judge David Bunning imposed the sentence on Friday for possession with
intent to distribute marijuana, possession of a firearm in furtherance of drug
trafficking and receipt of a stolen motor vehicle. [@2]

According to his plea agreement, law enforcement determined in July 2024 that a
vehicle reported stolen in Cleveland, Ohio had been sold to a person in Kentucky
and was later found at Burgin's residence. [@2] Authorities tracked a Hellcat
stolen out of Cleveland in April 2025 to a home in Latonia Lakes. [@1] According
to court papers, the stolen car had been involved in police pursuits in
Covington and Cincinnati. [@1] On 8 May 2025 officers searched Burgin's
residence and recovered the stolen vehicle along with large bags of marijuana, a
loaded handgun fitted with an extended magazine and a drum magazine. [@2]

Burgin admitted that he intended to distribute the marijuana and that he kept
the firearm to further that trafficking. [@2] Investigators said they found
hundreds of pictures of drugs and guns on Burgin's phone that led them to
believe the drugs were for sale. [@1] Court papers said Burgin bought the car
for $5,000 and admitted running from police. [@1] The FBI conducted the
investigation and Assistant U.S. Attorney Kyle M. Winslow prosecuted the case
for the United States. [@2]
