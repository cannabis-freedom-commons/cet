Thomas Bissett, 42, of Ross-on-Wye, appeared at Hereford Magistrates' Court
charged with possessing heroin, possessing cannabis with intent to supply and
possessing a knuckleduster at his home. [@1] He had pleaded guilty to all the
charges at an earlier hearing. [@1] Prosecutor Liz Blacklock told the court that
intelligence gathered by police based in Ross led them to search Bissett's home
in Verschoyle Gardens on 1 October 2026. [@1]

Officers found about 345 grams of cannabis stashed in a variety of bags and
pouches, while a wrap of heroin was accepted as being for personal use. [@1]
They also found specialist weighing scales and a number of lists containing
customers' names. [@1] A drug expert told the court that the quantity indicated
Bissett was a dealer with a significant role in supplying cannabis. [@1] Police
also found £1,475 in cash in his trouser pocket, believed to be the proceeds of
drug dealing, though he was never charged over it. [@1] The knuckleduster was
found in his bedside cabinet and had not been used in a threatening way. [@1]

Defence lawyer Matt Lewis said Bissett admitted a significant role but that his
dealing was not wholesale and involved only a small number of people. [@1]
Presiding Justice Michael Williams said supplying cannabis was the most serious
charge and had passed the custodial threshold, but suspended a six-month prison
sentence for a year because of Bissett's home circumstances. [@1] For 12 months
Bissett must keep out of trouble, attend a six-month drug rehabilitation
requirement and meet the Probation Service, with the remainder of the sentence
to be served if he breaches the terms. [@1] The magistrates fined him £200 for
the heroin wrap and knuckleduster, plus a £154 mandatory surcharge and £85 court
costs, and ordered the destruction of the drugs, paraphernalia and
knuckleduster. [@1]
