Police in Bavaria ran a larger traffic check on Friday evening in the districts
of Weilheim-Schongau and Garmisch-Partenkirchen, focusing on drivers under the
influence of drugs. [@1] Nearly 70 officers from three police headquarters took
part. [@3] After a training session, officers checked 321 vehicles at several
checkpoints. [@3] Eight drivers showed drug-typical signs and all eight
preliminary tests reacted positively to THC, the intoxicating ingredient of the
cannabis plant; blood samples were ordered and taken. [@2]

The eight drivers now face administrative offence proceedings. [@2] For a first
offence the standard penalty is a 500-euro fine, a one-month driving ban and two
points in Flensburg, and the licensing authority is informed. [@2] Police
vice-president Michael Siefener said criminal proceedings and possible permanent
revocation of a driving licence could follow where unfitness to drive was
suspected. [@2]

Siefener said cannabis legalisation had made police fear more people would drive
under the influence of drugs. [@1] In 2025 police in the Oberbayern Süd area
recorded 943 drivers under the influence of drugs or other intoxicants, 50 more
than in the previous year, and 560 of them had consumed cannabis. [@1] A driver
whose breath-alcohol test read 2.6 per mille faced criminal drunk-driving
proceedings, and another was reported for exceeding the 0.5 per mille limit.
[@2] Two other drivers had no licence, one carried a prohibited imitation weapon
and three vehicles were barred because technical changes had voided their
operating permits. [@3]
