In November 2025, people approached medical-cannabis grower Canarva seeking
about 130 kg of cannabis using forged documents and licences. [@3]

The buyers transferred 920,000 shekels to the company's account to secure the
deal. [@3]

Three people then came to the company's growing farm in the Arava with what were
said to be forged guard certificates to collect the cannabis. [@1] The three
left the farm without receiving any cannabis, were later arrested, and were
indicted before the Beersheba District Court. [@1]

Police also investigated people linked to the money transfer, but prosecutors
said the evidence was insufficient for criminal charges while indicating the
seized money was meant to fund the cannabis purchase. [@1] The state therefore
sought forfeiture in a separate civil proceeding, which under the Dangerous
Drugs Ordinance can forfeit property intended for certain drug offences even
without a criminal conviction. [@1]

In a decision dated 15 November, Judge Rotem Kudler Ayash noted the request and
decision were personally served and no response was filed, and granted
forfeiture of the full sum to the state treasury. [@1] The Beersheba District
Court thus ordered 920,000 shekels forfeited to the state treasury for the
failed fraudulent purchase. [@2]
