Mamadou Djouldé Bah appeared before the correctional court in Mafanco on
Thursday 19 November 2026, prosecuted for possessing, consuming and selling
Indian hemp — 25 boules, or balls — and for deliberate assault. [@1] He had been
held in detention since 16 November 2026. [@1] At the bar he acknowledged the
facts alleged against him. [@1]

The case arose from an operation to intercept holders, consumers and sellers of
Indian hemp. [@1] Adjutant-chef Cheick Traoré, a police officer and civil party
in the case, said his team went into the field to apprehend suspected offenders
and that Bah and his group attacked them. [@1] Bah said a backpack he carried
held one million Guinean francs that the officer tried to take, that the officer
struck him on the ear with his rifle, and that he grabbed the officer's arm only
to stop the violence. [@1] Under insistence from the prosecution he admitted the
money came from selling Indian hemp and that he had been intercepted with the
remaining balls in his bag. [@1] The officer gave a different account, saying
his team went to Kénien where Bah's group threw stones at them and Bah
challenged him to fight alone before his arm was twisted and fractured in the
struggle. [@1]

The prosecution asked the court to convict Bah, impose 18 months' firm
imprisonment and a two-million-Guinean-franc fine, and order the destruction of
the seized drugs. [@1] The court deferred the case to 3 December 2026 for its
decision. [@1]
