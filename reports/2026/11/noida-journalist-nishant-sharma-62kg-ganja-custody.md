Nishant Sharma, a journalist, was sent to 14 days' judicial custody in a ganja
trafficking case registered at Noida's Phase-1 police station. [@1] Additional
Sessions Judge Sandeep Chaudhary accepted the police remand application on 19
November 2026 and ordered Sharma held in district jail until 2 December 2026.
[@1] The case is crime number 309/2026, registered under sections 8, 20 and 29
of the NDPS Act and section 308(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, India's penal
code. [@1]

The remand order records that the case began when police arrested Tinku and
Muzaffar Ansari and recovered 62.400 kg of ganja from them. [@1] Noida police
put the ganja's value on the international market at about ₹16 lakh. [@3]
Additional Deputy Commissioner of Police Manisha Singh said Phase-1 officers
arrested Muzaffar Ansari and Tinku near the Nursery Gate in Sector 8 on 25 June
2026. [@3] Police said the two had long smuggled ganja brought from West Bengal
and supplied it in Delhi and the National Capital Region. [@3] The order records
that the two named a woman, Hema, who said in police custody that she worked
with Rajkumar and Nanhe. [@1] Nanhe then named Sharma and two other co-accused,
and police recovered more than 21 kg of ganja on his indication during custody
remand. [@1]

Police told the court that Irfan Malik, alias Nanhe, of JJ Colony, Sector 17,
Noida, said he paid monthly money to 'local journalists Raja Maurya, Tahir and
Nishant Sharma' to run the illegal ganja trade. [@2] According to the same
account, Nanhe said tweets were posted against him and more money demanded when
he objected. [@2] Sharma was arrested on that basis and accused of buying and
selling illegal ganja with the co-accused. [@1] His lawyer objected that he had
been falsely implicated on a co-accused's unproved statements and had not been
told the grounds of arrest. [@1]

The court said the case diary showed prima facie that his role lay in the
purchase and sale and running of the illegal ganja trade. [@1] It also recorded
that a co-accused's statements alone cannot prove the offence, but must be
examined with other facts and circumstances in the investigation. [@1] The court
found no clear irregularity in the arrest, granted the remand and ordered a
warrant under section 187 of the BNSS. [@1] The remand order does not say what
action police have taken against Raja Maurya and Tahir. [@1] The allegations in
the co-accused's statement have not been proved in court. [@1]
