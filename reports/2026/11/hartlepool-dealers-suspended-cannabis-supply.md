Durham Crown Court sentenced Hartlepool dealers Kayleigh Robson and Louis
Ludgate, who were in the car when police stopped it. [@1] Robson, 32, admitted
possessing cannabis and cocaine with intent to supply, acquiring criminal
property and simple possession of ecstasy. [@1] Ludgate, 37, admitted possessing
cannabis with intent to supply, possessing criminal property, carrying a bladed
article in public and refusing to give police his phone passcode. [@1] Judge
Nathan Adams imposed a two-year sentence on Robson, suspended for two years,
plus 210 hours of unpaid work under probation supervision. [@1] Ludgate was
given a four-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months, with probation
supervision. [@1]

Police stopped the car in Hartlepool on 3 January 2025 and recovered zip-up bags
containing vacuum-packed cannabis and cash. [@1] A search of Robson's home found
eight bags of cocaine, more cash and a set of scales. [@1] Robson gave police
her passcode, revealing messages about dealing cocaine and cannabis dating back
around a year. [@1] The recovered drugs were valued at about £1,200, the cash
totalled £1,190, and a small amount of ecstasy in Robson's bag was considered
for personal use. [@1]

Robson's lawyer said she dealt to fund her partner's drug addiction while in an
abusive relationship, spending 'not a penny of the proceeds' on herself. [@1]
The court heard Ludgate was using cannabis to relieve symptoms of chronic
fatigue syndrome. [@1] Judge Nathan Adams said the dealing may have been
'low-level' but appeared to have continued for a year. [@1] The judge said
Robson played 'a limited function' in street-level supply and was 'unlikely' to
become involved in crime again. [@1] Both defendants face financial inquiries
under the Proceeds of Crime Act, with the case due back in court on 1 March.
