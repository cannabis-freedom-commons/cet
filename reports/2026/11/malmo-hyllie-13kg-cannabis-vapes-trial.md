On 22 July this year Danish police arrested a Malmö resident, and Malmö police
then searched the man's home in Hyllie. [@1] Officers smelled cannabis from an
open window outside the flat, as noted in their memo. [@1] Inside a closet the
smell was stronger, and officers found many vacuum packs of cannabis on shelves,
on the floor and in black rubbish bags. [@1] The 33-year-old man is a Danish
citizen who said he works for the railway company DSB, and he admitted storing
the drugs. [@1] He denied committing a crime. [@1] He now stands trial for
aggravated drug crime. [@1]

In the living room stood cartons of e-cigarettes, and police seized almost 13
kilos of cannabis and 2,637 THC vapes in total. [@1] A phone image showed liquid
from a bottle being poured into a beaker on a scale next to lined-up
e-cigarettes. [@1] On a return visit police found bottles that looked like hair
oil but contained THC oil, seizing 76 decilitres in total. [@1]

He said he was drawn into drug handling after meeting a man called Victor at a
party in Marbella, who he said lives in Los Angeles and offered him extra money.
[@1] He said parcels were sent to Denmark, where he collected them to drive over
the bridge to Sweden. [@1] He claimed he wanted to withdraw after discovering
the parcels held drugs, but said Victor threatened him and his family with
death. [@1] Police found no threats when reviewing 32 of his chats in the
encrypted service Signal. [@1] They found many messages about drug deliveries,
payments and meetings with buyers. [@1] In other messages he discussed
transports to Norway and cannabis deliveries in Malmö, offering to drive and
fetch drugs. [@1] He said he also tried to sell cannabis on his own to clear the
stock. [@1] The prosecutor also demands that he be expelled from Sweden and
banned from returning. [@1]
