Kain Abery, 22, of Curling Way in Newbury, appeared at Reading Magistrates'
Court for sentencing on 18 November 2026. [@1] Magistrates gave him an 18-month
conditional discharge, telling him they would not punish him for possessing
cocaine and cannabis provided he kept out of trouble for the duration of the
order. [@1] He was also ordered to pay £85 costs and a £26 victim support
surcharge. [@1] He had formally admitted possessing cocaine, a Class A drug, and
cannabis, a Class B drug, in Newbury on 29 December 2025. [@1]

A plain-clothes officer spotted him in Bridge Street, Newbury, suspected he was
riding an electric bike illegally and called uniformed colleagues to stop and
search him. [@1] The search found a Tesco carrier bag holding 70g of cannabis.
[@1] A search after he was taken into custody found a bag of white powder
concealed in his rucksack. [@1] Abery told police he smoked about 10 joints a
day and admitted in an interview under caution that the drugs were for his
personal use. [@1] Newbury Today put the potential value of the drugs at up to
£1,000. [@1]

The court clerk asked the prosecution why the case had taken so long to reach
court given Abery's admissions at the time and his early indication of guilty
pleas, and no explanation was given. [@1] Defence solicitor Emma Turner said it
was incredible the matter had taken so long and told the court her client had
mental health issues, a long-standing drug problem and did not always take his
prescribed medication. [@1] After retiring to confer, magistrates acknowledged
the delay in bringing the case to court and cited it as a factor in their
deliberations. [@1] Abery also had a previous conviction from July, when he was
fined £200 for possessing a swordstick at his home. [@1] An HMCTS court-list
entry, case 43SW0576926, records the 18 November 2026 hearing at Reading
Magistrates' Court on offences of possessing a Class B drug, cannabis or
cannabis resin, and a Class A drug, cocaine. [@2]
