National Police arrested 16 people in Valencia, Alicante and Zaragoza as alleged
members of an organisation principally dedicated to cultivating and
internationally trafficking marijuana, while also trafficking human beings to
guard and maintain the plantations. [@1] The detainees were 13 men and three
women aged between 27 and 53. [@2] Two of the suspects have been remanded in
custody. [@2] All face allegations of drug trafficking, membership of a criminal
organisation, electricity fraud and human trafficking. [@1] Investigators said
some of the victims lived in cramped, squalid conditions and were not allowed to
leave the properties where they were forced to care for the crops. [@2] The
network allegedly brought in Albanian citizens with very limited resources and
forced them to live in inhuman conditions, never leaving the site, receiving
food only during occasional visits. [@1]

In total, police carried out 10 searches and dismantled six cannabis
plantations, together with the equipment used to steal power from the grid. [@2]
Officers seized more than 2,800 plants, with a combined weight of 745 kilos.
[@1] The first lead in Puzol yielded about 1,400 plants and two residents
detained. [@1] A plantation on an isolated rural site in Alicante province held
more than 700 plants. [@2] The pair first arrested, a couple, survived crowded
into a minimal space without going outside, tending the crop. [@1] The woman had
arrived in Spain only days earlier through Barcelona airport, where a man
resident in Zaragoza collected and moved her. [@1]

The investigation, led by the Sagunt local station with support from the
Zaragoza judicial police brigade and GRECO Levante, began in March after a
marijuana greenhouse was detected in a chalet in a Puzol housing estate. [@1]
The network was described as an Albanian-Spanish criminal organisation. [@1] Its
leaders located detached chalets, renting them for cash with false documents.
[@1] They then installed growing infrastructure and made illegal connections to
the electricity supply. [@1] Harvested marijuana was sent abroad by postal
parcels. [@1] In one intercepted shipment officers found more than two kilos of
buds. [@1] The group also distributed other substances such as cocaine,
sometimes used as payment to intermediaries who carried food to the crops or
housed caretakers between growing periods. [@1] Its leaders ran a rigid
hierarchy and used threats or violence against other members. [@1]
