Sashastra Seema Bal officers stopped a woman at the Banbasa checkpoint in
Champawat district, Uttarakhand, on 18 November 2026 after finding suspicious
material in a transparent polythene bag. [@1] Laboratory tests by the Narcotics
Control Bureau confirmed the material was hydroponic ganja. [@1] The NCB’s
Dehradun zonal director, Devanand, said the woman, Varsha Raikwar, was arrested
under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act. [@1]

Officers seized 8.3 kg of hydroponic ganja at the border checkpoint, a haul that
officials valued at around Rs 2.5 crore. [@1] Hydroponic ganja is grown indoors
under controlled conditions, in nutrient-rich water rather than soil. [@1]
Devanand said it was the fifth hydroponic ganja seizure in Uttarakhand in the
past year and the largest single seizure in the state so far. [@1] An NCB
officer said hydroponic ganja fetches around Rs 35 lakh per kilogram, against Rs
15,000-20,000 for conventional cannabis. [@1]

During interrogation the woman told authorities the drug had been supplied by an
accomplice in Thailand for a recipient in Delhi. [@1] She said she carried it
from Thailand to Nepal and was entering India over the land border at Banbasa
when she was intercepted. [@1] She had been promised Rs 20,000 for carrying two
bags into India and had already received Rs 6,000 in advance. [@1] Devanand said
she had previously been arrested at an airport in Gujarat on suspicion of
carrying hydroponic ganja from Thailand. [@1] The NCB said it would strengthen
coordination with other agencies, particularly the border force, and its
intelligence network against trafficking involving operatives in Thailand, Nepal
and India. [@1]
