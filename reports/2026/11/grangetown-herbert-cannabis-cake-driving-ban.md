A dad was caught drug driving after being unknowingly fed a 'cannabis cake' at a
party. [@1] Daniel Herbert, 37, pleaded guilty at Teesside Magistrates' Court to
driving with a proportion of a specified controlled drug above the specified
limit. [@1] The court heard he was a man of previous good character. [@1]

Police stopped Herbert driving a Ford Transit van on Argyle Road in Grangetown
on 20 May 2026. [@1] Prosecutor Michael Embleton said officers could smell
cannabis in the van and a blood test showed Herbert was over the drug-drive
limit for delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). [@1] Magistrates fined him £120
and ordered him to pay a £48 victim surcharge and £85 court costs. [@1] He was
disqualified from driving for 12 months and the funds will be deducted from his
universal credit and PIP payments. [@1]

Defence lawyer Daniel Burke said Herbert was not a cannabis user and had been
fed an edible cake at a party without knowing it contained cannabis. [@1] 'He
felt dizzy and awful afterward,' the court was told. [@1] Burke said Herbert
asked others, learned the cake contained cannabis and decided not to drive home
from the party. [@1] He said Herbert felt fine the following day so got behind
the wheel, adding that THC can stay in someone's system for a significant
period. [@1] The court heard the cannabis found in the van belonged to a friend
and Herbert was not the sole occupant. [@1]
