Nashik taluka police took Vaman Santu Gumbade into custody as the suspect after
the raid. [@4,5] The raid was carried out at midnight on Saturday 21 November
2026, when officers seized 980 kg of green plants, leaves and branches
resembling ganja. [@1] The crop was being grown in a remote hilly spot in the
Naikwadi area near Girnare, concealed behind ambadi and tomato crops. [@1,5] It
was planted on a hill field at Gat No. 145 within the Naikwadi village boundary.
[@1] The field was far from the main road and could only be reached on foot
because vehicles could not get to it. [@1] Naikwadi is surrounded by hills,
ravines and forest, which keep some farm plots out of sight. [@3]

Police valued the seized material at 1.47 crore rupees, working it out at 15,000
rupees a kilogram. [@1] The cultivation came to light through a tip-off, and the
ganja plants had been grown behind other crops to create the appearance of
ordinary farming. [@1] Maharashtra Times reported that the ganja occupied about
30 are, or 0.3 hectares, on the hill field. [@2] Police said the seized plants
were about four to six feet tall. [@2] The haul consisted of fresh, wet plants
pulled up with their roots, together with leaves and branches. [@2]

A case was registered under sections 8(c), 20(a), 20(b) and 22 of the NDPS Act
and section 238 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. [@2] Police inspector Mrudula
Naik is conducting the further investigation. [@1] A preliminary inquiry
indicated this was the suspect's first attempt, and Nashik superintendent of
police Dr D. S. Swami said the hunt for possible accomplices continued. [@1]
Police said checking the illegal drug trade before the approaching Sinhastha
Kumbh Mela was a major challenge. [@1] Cultivation of this kind is rare in
Nashik district, and the seizure of close to a tonne of ganja-like material was
treated as serious. [@2]
