Rowan Webster, 26, and Ollie Wilson, 22, entered guilty pleas at Mold Crown
Court on Friday, 30 October 2026. [@1] They admitted possessing 140 grams of
cannabis and 27.2 grams of cocaine with intent to supply. [@1,3] The charges
relate to their arrest at The Garage in Forden, near Welshpool, on 8 February
2024. [@1] A search found 27.2 grams of cocaine worth about £2,500 at street level and 140 grams of cannabis valued at up to £1,100. [@3] Wilson told officers "I've screwed up. Someone paid me £60 to pick them up", the court heard. [@3] A black Audi A4 was stopped on the A490 at Forden on 8 February 2024 after intelligence suggested the pair were returning from Merseyside with drugs. [@3] The court heard that both men were acting as drug couriers. [@1,3] The pair admitted similar trips before on behalf of another man, although they were charged only over the 8 February 2024 journey. [@3]
Recorder Robert Philpotts adjourned sentencing until 26 November and ordered
pre-sentence reports on both defendants. [@1] He made their cooperation with the
reports a condition of bail and said it was “in your interests”. [@1] The drugs and mobile phones seized by police were ordered to be forfeited and destroyed. [@3] The judge noted that neither man had reoffended between arrest and sentencing and that both had made genuine efforts to rebuild their lives. [@3] They must complete 150 hours of unpaid work, comply with six months of trail monitoring and pay £700 in prosecution costs. [@3] He sentenced each of them to 18 months' imprisonment, suspended for 18 months. [@3] Judge Timothy Petts said they were "young and exploited into doing this" but had both agreed to do it. [@3] Simon Rogers, for Webster, said his client had shown genuine remorse and had been "open and honest" about his offending. [@3] Wilson, who was 20 when the offences were committed, had stopped using cocaine and found work, his barrister Dafydd Roberts said. [@3] “You will
receive substantial credit for your pleas of guilty,” the recorder told them.
[@1]

They gave no indication of plea at that hearing. [@2] Prosecutor Helen Tench
told the magistrates that the offences were not suitable for trial at the lower
court. [@2]
