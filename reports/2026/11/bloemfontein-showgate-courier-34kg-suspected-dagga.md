Police in Bloemfontein opened an enquiry docket after two suspicious boxes were
delivered to a courier company at Showgate in Currie Avenue on Saturday 14
November. [@1] A staff member who received the boxes from a delivery driver
noticed a suspicious smell, opened the first box and found transparent plastic
bags containing dried leaves believed to be dagga, then alerted the police. [@1]
The courier receipt reportedly listed an unknown sender with an address in
Pretoria Glen Extension 27, Soweto, Johannesburg. [@1] Two intended recipients,
aged 29 and 31, later arrived to collect the parcels and police took them in for
questioning. [@1] The Local Criminal Record Centre was called to the scene to
process the exhibits and investigators lifted fingerprints for further
examination. [@1] The two parcels were left with the company while efforts
continued to trace their collection and origin. [@1]

The two boxes weighed 17.3 kg and 17 kg respectively, bringing their combined
weight to 34.3 kg, and police put the estimated street value of the suspected
dagga at approximately R450 000. [@1] Parkweg Station Commander Brigadier Teboho
Raymond Jacobs activated a task team to establish whether the dried leaves were
cannabis (dagga) or hemp. [@2] As part of the preliminary investigation, the
task team inspected Basic Pienk Land plantation in Weltervreden, along
Rustfontein Road, as well as Meeklands in Rankelenyane, Parkfontein, Thaba Nchu.
[@1] Management at both plantations produced copies of permits authorising
activities relating to hemp under the Plant Improvement Act 54 of 1976. [@1]
Police took samples from the two plantations and from the dried leaves in the
boxes, and all samples will be sent to the Forensic Science Laboratory for
analysis to establish whether the contents are cannabis or legally cultivated
hemp. [@1]
