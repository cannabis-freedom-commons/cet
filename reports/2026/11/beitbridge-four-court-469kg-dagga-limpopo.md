Four men appeared before the Beitbridge Regional Magistrate's Court charged with
unlawful dealing in dangerous drugs after police said two vehicles carried
469.436 kg of marijuana smuggled through the Limpopo River. [@1,3] The accused
were identified as Rabson Mashingaidze, Charles Makanga, Emmanuel Sibanda and
Connias Muleya, aged 54, 39, 35 and 35, when they appeared before Regional
Magistrate Charity Maphosa. [@1,3] The four, represented by Muchiwande Sithole,
pleaded not guilty. [@1,3]

Prosecutors alleged that on 21 July 2026 at Makakavhule under Chief Tshitaudze
the four, acting in common purpose, procured, imported, possessed and
transported 469.436 kg of marijuana via Maroyi, an undesignated crossing on the
Limpopo River. [@1,3] Police detectives said they received information at about
5PM that day that two vehicles carrying dagga were travelling from the Maroyi
crossing toward Beitbridge town. [@1,3] Detectives lay in wait and spotted a red
Honda Fit registration AFH 5439 and a silver Toyota Probox registration AHI
5770. [@1,3] Sibanda was allegedly driving the Honda Fit with Muleya as passenger,
while Mashingaidze drove the Probox with Makanga as passenger. [@1,3] The drivers
manoeuvred out of the ambush, made a U-turn and sped off towards Palm River
Company. [@1,3] Police gave chase and caught up with the vehicles after they
reached a dead-end. [@1,3] A search of the Honda Fit allegedly yielded 10 bags,
each containing one large cob of dagga, and four boxes containing smaller cobs.
[@1,3] In the Probox, detectives allegedly recovered 16 bags, each containing one
large cob of dagga, and five boxes containing smaller cobs. [@1,3] The State said
a total of 26 large cobs and 143 smaller cobs were recovered from the two
vehicles. [@1,3] The accused failed to produce a licence or permit authorising possession of the dagga, leading to their arrest. [@3] The dagga was weighed at the ZIMPOST office in the accused's
presence at 469.436 kg with an estimated street value of US$46,943.60. [@1,3]

The Forensic Science Laboratory in Beitbridge confirmed the material was dagga.
[@1,3] Police seized the two vehicles, the accused's cellphones and the dagga,
referring the phones to the Cyber Bulawayo Laboratory. [@1,3] Police said the
dagga was believed smuggled from South Africa into Zimbabwe through an illegal
Limpopo River crossing. [@2] The four have appeared in court with the matter
pending. [@2]
