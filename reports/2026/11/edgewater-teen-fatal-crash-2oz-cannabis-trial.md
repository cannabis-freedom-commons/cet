A teenager fatally struck a pedestrian in Edgewater while riding an electric
motorbike in July last year. [@1,3] Shelley Spinks, 59, died after she was struck
by the then-17-year-old rider at a park in a northern Perth suburb. [@1,3] The
teenager, who cannot be named due to his age at the time, was originally charged
with manslaughter, no authority to drive and using an unlicensed vehicle on a
road. [@1] Earlier this year, he pleaded guilty to dangerous driving in Perth
Children’s Court, and is fighting the manslaughter charge at trial, which began
on Monday. [@1]

A blood sample was formally analysed and found to contain THC at 9.1 micrograms
per litre. [@1,3] A toxicologist concluded the accused was most likely impaired by
drugs and unable to control his motorbike at the time. [@1] More than two ounces
of cannabis were found in the teenager’s bag following the accident, including
two ounces in separate bags and a smaller amount in a prescription container.
[@1,3] Evidence on the 17-year-old’s phone suggested the two packaged ounces were
to be sold later that afternoon. [@1] Defence lawyer Simon Watters accepted the
teenager had cannabis in his blood but insisted it was a prescribed substance
that did not affect his control of the vehicle. [@1]

Spinks, a nurse originally from New Zealand, was taken to Joondalup Health
Campus where she later died from injuries sustained in the collision. [@1] The
teenager went on trial in the Perth Children's Court accused of the manslaughter
of nurse Shelley Spinks, 59, at Edgewater's Picnic Cove Park in July last year.
[@2] State prosecutor James Nicholls said the teenager had been fleeing police
during a chase and had ignored directions to stop before the fatal impact. [@2]
Blood samples taken from the boy contained THC, and the defence said he had been
prescribed cannabis. [@2]
