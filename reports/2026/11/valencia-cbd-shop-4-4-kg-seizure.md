The investigation began on 28 September 2026 when a Valencia patrol found a
young man, accompanied by his father, consuming a substance that looked like
cannabis on a public street. [@1] Both told officers they had bought it in a CBD
shop whose assistant had assured them its consumption was legal. [@1] Officers
connected this with earlier accounts of people buying similar products at the
same shop and with neighbours' complaints about consumption of the substances in
the neighbourhood. [@1] Their analysis led to a specific inspection on 5
November 2026. [@1]

Valencia's local police seized 651 cannabis-derived units with a total weight of
4.4 kilograms at the shop on Calle Doctor Manuel Candela. [@1] The action
followed surveillance and investigation by officers of the Comisaría de
Proximidad del Marítimo, the local police neighbourhood unit that linked earlier
interventions and residents' complaints to the shop's activity. [@1] During the
inspection officers found that the shop advertised CBD flowers, hashish and
other cannabis derivatives, with offers visible from the street. [@1] They also
found advertising that attributed supposed health benefits to the products. [@1]
Inside, officers found products prepared for sale both in the display cases and
in the back room. [@1] An inventory recorded 321 units of cannabis flowers
weighing 3,648 grams and 330 units of resinous or hashish-like products weighing
799 grams. [@1]

Police also found sales records and records of financial transactions. [@1] The
estimated commercial value of the seized products, based on the advertised
prices, could exceed 10,000 euros. [@1]
