Zahir Abdalla, 22, of Slough, was given a 40-week prison sentence suspended for
18 months at Reading Crown Court on Thursday 26 November 2026. [@1] He pleaded
guilty to two counts of being concerned in the supply of Class B drugs, namely
cannabis and cannabis resin. [@1] The offences date back to 2024, when Abdalla
was 20 years old. [@1] Abdalla initially denied the offences and was due to
stand trial next spring before changing his pleas. [@1]

The court heard that just under 2kg of cannabis and cannabis resin, with an
estimated street value of £17,085, was found alongside weighing scales. [@1] A
CCTV camera overlooked a wheelie bin in which a large quantity of cannabis was
found. [@1] Prosecutor Richard Jones said messages recovered from a mobile phone
were consistent with involvement in drug supply. [@1] Recorder David Edwards
remarked during mitigation that the presence of CCTV covering the drugs
indicated a sophisticated operation. [@1] Defence lawyer Johan Grefstad pointed
out that many houses have CCTV cameras and said he had recently installed one
himself. [@1] The judge responded that he was not sure the exchange would assist
the defendant significantly. [@1]

Mr Grefstad said Abdalla had acted under pressure from a debt to his drug dealer
and had made limited financial gain. [@1] Abdalla, who had no previous
convictions, was employed at the Mars factory and hoped to return to the
engineering sector. [@1] The court heard that he had expressed remorse and spent
time reflecting on his actions while in custody. [@1] Recorder Edwards told
Abdalla he was not going to prison that day. [@1] The judge accepted the Crown's
account that Abdalla had acted as part of a wider scheme. [@1] He concluded
there was 'just about' a realistic prospect of rehabilitation. [@1] Abdalla was
ordered to complete 150 hours of unpaid work and six months of GPS monitoring.
[@2] He must also pay £1,630 in costs over six months and a victim surcharge.
