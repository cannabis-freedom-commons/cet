Bilal F., Mounir H. and Chakib M. lived together in a house in Caspe from which
they sold drugs to local buyers. [@1] The flat under surveillance from February was on Calle Lafuen in Caspe, where officers saw many brief visits. [@2] For home deliveries the three used a car
in which the housing for the passenger airbag had been specially prepared to
conceal their merchandise. [@1,2] The men used a Volkswagen Golf to carry drugs to the flat. [@2] While the three devoted themselves to that
illicit business they managed to capture an important portion of the drug market
in the surrounding comarca. [@1]

According to the published summary, the accused trafficked in marijuana, cocaine
and hashish. [@1,2] The seized substances had a combined illegal-market value of 8,254.89 euros. [@2] The Guardia Civil regarded the flat they operated as a very
active drug sales point in the comarca. [@1]

Guardia Civil officers detained all three men on 13 March. [@1,2] Zaragoza's
provincial court sentenced the three men to three years in prison on that
Monday. [@1,2] The defence was still to prove their roots in Spain before any expulsion decision. [@2] Suspension of the prison terms pending proof of drug rehabilitation was still to be decided. [@2] None had a criminal record, but the deal did not mean automatic release. [@2] They must jointly pay Endesa about 2,000 euros for damage from the illegal power connection. [@2] Defended by Javier Catalán Catalán, each man also accepted an 8,000-euro drug-trafficking fine and a 270-euro electricity-fraud fine. [@2] The three-year terms followed a deal reducing the four years first sought by the prosecution. [@2] The concealment in the passenger airbag compartment and the regular
home-delivery runs by car were central to how the group held its significant
local market share. [@1]
