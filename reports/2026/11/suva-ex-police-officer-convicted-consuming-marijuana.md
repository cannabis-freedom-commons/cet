A former Fiji police officer has been convicted at the Suva Magistrate Court
after he was found to be under the influence of drugs while in uniform following
a urine test in June 2020. [@1] Magistrate Yogesh Prasad convicted Rhys Tasleem
Janif on the afternoon of 25 November 2026 after finding that the prosecution
had proved all elements of the offence. [@1] The court convicted Janif of
consuming marijuana. [@1] Janif did not appear in court and was represented by
his lawyer, Joji Cakau. [@1] Janif's sentencing hearing has been scheduled for
Friday. [@1]

The court heard that the incident began on June 24, 2020, when a senior police
officer raised concerns about the accused's suspicious and irrational behaviour.
[@1] The officer's conduct, including his red eyes, prompted a senior officer to
direct him to undergo a urine test at the Fiji Police Medical Centre on
suspicion that he was under the influence of drugs. [@1] The accused was
escorted to the medical centre, where a doctor explained the testing procedure
and obtained his signed consent before collecting a urine sample. [@1] The
doctor testified that Janif asked whether his urine sample could be swapped with
the doctor's sample. [@1] The urine specimen was subsequently sealed and
forwarded to the Fiji Police Forensic Laboratory for analysis. [@1]

The court dismissed the defence argument that the officer's constitutional
rights had been breached, finding that the accused had been informed of the
procedure, signed the consent form and voluntarily provided his urine sample.
[@1] It also accepted the prosecution's evidence on the chain of custody,
finding that the sample had been properly collected, sealed and delivered for
testing. [@1] The court further considered the accused's request to swap his
sample with the doctor's as evidence of consciousness of guilt. [@1]
