Gary Michael (Boo) Boudreau, 50, was sentenced to eight years in prison in
Halifax provincial court on 25 November 2026. [@1] The charges stemmed from a
major RCMP cocaine and cannabis trafficking investigation. [@1] He had pleaded
not guilty but was convicted after trial the previous year of cocaine
trafficking-related charges, money laundering, possession of proceeds of crime,
a cannabis offence and two firearms charges linked to an unloaded shotgun found
in his bedroom. [@1]

Judge Elizabeth Buckle concluded that Boudreau profited greatly from his crimes.
[@1] She said greed motivated the drug and proceeds of crime offences. [@1] The
judge said that although legitimate income from rental properties and a
construction firm could have supported a comfortable life, he lived a luxurious
lifestyle. [@1] Boudreau was not directly involved in handling large amounts of
cocaine, she said; he set up meetings between associates and supplied 'cut',
chemicals he bought by the barrel to dilute the drug and increase quantity and
profits. [@1]

Boudreau was a main target of the 2020 investigation, which led to 14 arrests
and included listening probes hidden in vehicles, extensive surveillance and a
review of financial records. [@1] Police found a Walmart bag containing $20,150
on his kitchen counter when they searched his home on 9 December 2020. [@1]

Prosecutors sought 13 years and the defence asked for three and a half years.
[@1] Crown prosecutor Len MacKay welcomed the sentence and said he hoped it
would deter other drug traffickers. [@1] Defence lawyer Pat MacEwen said it was
'a little higher than we had hoped' and did not rule out an appeal. [@1]
Prosecutors have already appealed Buckle's summer decision not to order
forfeiture of Boudreau's lakeside home, instead ordering him to pay a $240,000
fine. [@1] Boudreau told the court he understood the seriousness of the case and
had had 'a lot of time to reflect' since being charged. [@1]
