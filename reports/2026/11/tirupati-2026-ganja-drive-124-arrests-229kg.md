Tirupati police intensified action against ganja traffickers and consumers,
booking 16 alleged traffickers under the PD Act and arresting 124 people so far
in 2026. [@1] Police opened around 275 history sheets against habitual offenders
and are using rapid testing kits to trace supply sources. [@1] In the latest
action, a repeat trafficker facing four NDPS cases involving 222 kg of ganja was
detained under the PIT NDPS Act and shifted to Rajahmundry Central Prison. [@1]
District superintendent L. Subba Rayudu said police are giving special attention
to eliminating ganja and other narcotics from Tirupati given its importance as a
spiritual centre. [@1]

So far in 2026, police registered 46 cases, seized 229 kg of ganja and arrested
124 people. [@1] In 2024, police registered 85 cases, seized 872 kg of ganja and
arrested 223 people. [@1] In 2025, the figures fell to 68 cases, 762 kg and 179
arrests. [@1] Police tested around 12,000 people so far, of whom 430 tested
positive for ganja consumption. [@1] More than 350 of those who tested positive
were workers from other states who had come to the district seeking employment.
[@1]

Rapid testing kits are being used to identify consumers and trace their sources
of supply. [@1] Police teams are monitoring railway routes used to transport
ganja to Tirupati from other states. [@1] A specially trained dog squad is being
deployed to detect drug consignments on trains. [@1] The police and the EAGLE
anti-narcotics wing are coordinating operations against suppliers, local sellers
and habitual offenders. [@1] Rayudu urged the public to report ganja-related
activity to the police or Dial 112, assuring informants that their identities
would remain confidential. [@1] Rayudu said police will not hesitate to invoke
preventive detention against repeat offenders. [@1]
