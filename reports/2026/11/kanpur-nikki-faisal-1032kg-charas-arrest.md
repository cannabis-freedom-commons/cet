Nazirabad police in Kanpur arrested Shazia alias Nikki, wife of slain
history-sheeter Saeed Nate, and her associate Faisal. [@2] DCP Central Atul
Kumar Srivastava said the pair were detained during a checking drive early on
Thursday, 26 November 2026, near the railway boundary on the road from Gumti
Crossing towards Coca-Cola Chauraha. [@2,4] Officers seized 19 bundles of charas
weighing 10 kg 320 g. [@1,4] Police valued the seized charas at 10 crore rupees.
[@1]

The two had come from Nepal by tourist bus and were preparing to hand the charas
to associates for onward supply through Jaipur and Mumbai. [@3] Police said the couple bought charas cheaply in Nepal and sometimes in Bihar and supplied it in Kanpur, nearby districts, Ajmer and Mumbai. [@4] During
questioning Nikki said she had bought the charas in Nepal for 25 lakh rupees.
[@2] The network bought charas at 25,000 rupees a kilogram and sold it for up to
one crore rupees a kilogram. [@1] Officers also recovered 8,600 Indian rupees,
275 Nepali rupees and bus tickets from the pair. [@1]

A message on Nikki's phone warned her not to come to Kanpur because police had
laid a trap. [@1] Nikki's husband Saeed Nate was shot dead in March 2012 and his
associates then handed her control of his network. [@3] About five years ago a relative brought her into drug trafficking and gave her contacts in Nepal and Mumbai. [@3] Faisal is 12 years younger than Nikki and began living with her a few years after her husband was murdered. [@3] Police said Nikki had
moved her son, daughter and son-in-law to Mumbai, where they helped distribute
the drugs. [@2] Jagran reported that she wore a burqa to travel by tourist bus while smuggling, and that her children and relatives in Mumbai were also part of the gang. [@3] Srivastava said the accused named alleged smugglers in Nepal and
Mumbai and teams had been sent to trace them. [@2] Srivastava said teams were also looking for more traffickers, including Nikki's relative, and tracing a gang in Ajmer. [@3] A case was registered against both under the NDPS Act, and police were searching for other gang members through the seized phone and other contacts. [@4]
