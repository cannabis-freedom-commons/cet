A German man was checked at the LändleCan hemp fair in Hohenems in January 2026.
[@1] Finance-police officers found 12.7 grams of cannabis herb on him, and the
police then seized it. [@2] He had a container of medically prescribed cannabis
in his open backpack in the exhibition hall and handed the officers his
prescription and other documents. [@3] Finance-police officers seized, packed
and labelled the herb and handed it to the police, which completed the seizure
on the car park outside the hall. [@3] He signed the seizure protocol and a
consent to the destruction of the drug. [@3]

He complained to the Landesverwaltungsgericht Vorarlberg that taking a
prescribed medicine in full, without medical assessment, a time limit, an
alternative measure or written reasoning, was a disproportionate interference
with his right to health care and private life. [@2] He carried a copy of a
German prescription for the cannabis. [@2] The court accepted that he is a pain
patient who needed the cannabis for medical reasons. [@3] A Schengen certificate
he also carried had expired and was issued for Luxembourg, France and Spain, not
Austria. [@3]

The Landesverwaltungsgericht dismissed the complaint on 6 November 2026. [@3] It
held that cannabis herb is not a medicine containing a controlled substance in
Austria but a Suchtgift, a controlled narcotic. [@2] Under the
Suchtgiftverordnung, bringing cannabis herb into Austria or carrying it there is
prohibited, so the German prescription did not make the import lawful. [@3] The
court found the officer could assume the seizure of the 12.7 grams was
justified. [@3] It ordered him to pay 887.20 euros in costs. [@3] It declared a
revision to the Administrative Court inadmissible. [@3]

The checks at the fair drew criticism from Hohenems city councillor Bernhard
Amann, who said police and finance police had overshot. [@2]
