Federal agents arrested three Cuban nationals on Thursday 19 November 2026
during a bust of a marijuana growing operation in Embden, Maine. [@1,3] The three
men were at the home on Barron Road during the search and were arrested there.
[@2] They are Lester M. Fuentes Gonzalez, 38, of Embden; Hector A. Roque de
Escobar, 60, of Homestead, Florida; and Osvaldo Guillermo Gamez Fernandez, 66,
of Hialeah, Florida. [@1] Each is charged with conspiracy to manufacture more
than 100 marijuana plants and manufacturing more than 100 marijuana plants. [@1,3]
All three made initial court appearances on Friday in U.S. District Court in
Bangor. [@1]

In the Thursday search agents found 200 marijuana plants and 120 pounds (about
54 kilograms) of processed marijuana at the Barron Road property. [@1] The live
plants were in the basement, the garage and an outbuilding, and the processed
marijuana was in the garage, an agent's affidavit said. [@2,3] Homeland Security
Investigations agents searched the home, and the Drug Enforcement Administration
also took part, a spokesperson for the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District
of Maine said. [@2] Agents also found grow lights, heaters, cellphones and trash
bags filled with stems, leaves and root balls from earlier harvests. [@2,3]

Roque is the registered agent of the limited liability company that owns the
Embden home and has a 2016 federal conviction in Colorado that followed a search
of a marijuana grow house, court records showed. [@2] Gamez told agents he lived
at the home as a caretaker and watered the marijuana plants when he was told to.
[@2] Two electricity accounts at the home have been in Fuentes' name since 2024,
and one used well above 8,000 kilowatt-hours a month from January, the affidavit
said. [@2,3] The charges follow an 11-month investigation into the Barron Road
home, which court records say has been used to grow marijuana since at least
January. [@2] The three men are being held at the Somerset County Jail in East
Madison. [@2] Each faces a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 40 years in
prison if convicted. [@2] Fuentes and Roque are lawful permanent residents of
the United States, and the court documents did not give Gamez's immigration
status. [@2]
