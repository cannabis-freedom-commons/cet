Police stored the ganja recovered in recent sustained district-wide raids in the
station malkhana as required. [@1] In the last 15 days police conducted
operations from Cooch Behar's border areas to different parts of the town and
registered eight cases. [@1,2] Those operations destroyed about 1,200 kg of ganja
plants and separately seized about 70 kg of ganja. [@1,2] Police arrested eight
men and six women. [@1,2]

The number of rats grew in step with the growing sacks of ganja. [@1] At night
when the station empties, the rats begin their ‘party’ in the malkhana. [@1]
They cut open the sacks, eat the ganja inside and scatter the remainder across
the floor. [@1,2] In the morning officers arriving for duty collect the scattered
ganja and rebag it. [@1] The seized sacks had earlier been kept beside the station lock-up, where rats also cut them open and scattered the ganja. [@2]

Seized ganja is normally burnt and destroyed in a magistrate's presence after a
set time. [@1] Rats are eating a large share before that destruction, leaving
officers struggling to reconcile weights in court and records. [@1] Officers
suspect the rats are drawn by the strong smell of the ganja. [@1] Repairing the
malkhana, poison and traps have not helped. [@1]
