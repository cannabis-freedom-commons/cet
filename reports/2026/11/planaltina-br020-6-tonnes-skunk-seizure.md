Officers of Brazil's Federal Highway Police (PRF) and the Distrito Federal Civil
Police (PCDF) seized about six tonnes of skunk on Sunday 22 November 2026 on
BR-020 in the Distrito Federal. [@1] The driver was arrested in flagrante and
taken to the PCDF's Drug Repression Coordination (CORD) with the vehicle and the
seized material. [@3] He was booked for interstate drug trafficking and illegal
possession of a firearm. [@3]

The PRF called it the second-largest drug seizure in the force's history. [@1]
Police valued the load at up to R$ 180 million. [@1] The drugs were hidden in
compartments inside the two tank-type trailers of the truck. [@1] G1 reported
that the vehicle had come from Amazonas and was headed for the Centro-Oeste
region. [@2]

Acting on shared intelligence and with sniffer dogs, PRF officers stopped the
truck at km 30 of BR-020. [@1] The driver said he had left Amazonas for the
Centro-Oeste and would receive R$ 12,000 for the transport, but could not give
the exact loading point or delivery address. [@1] Because of the
inconsistencies, officers took the truck to the PRF operational unit in
Planaltina for a more detailed inspection. [@1] When the compartments were
opened, officers found the drugs along with a 7.62-calibre rifle bearing
Peruvian Revolutionary Guard identification and ten ballistic plates. [@1] The
material was sent for preliminary analysis at the PCDF's Institute of Forensic
Medicine, which confirmed it was skunk and put the total weight at about six
tonnes. [@1] The driver, the vehicle and the seized material were referred to
CORD for the appropriate legal steps. [@1]
