Christopher Emilien, aged 33 and living at Batterie-Cassée, was convicted of
possession of dangerous drugs (cannabis and synthetic cannabinoids) for the
purpose of selling on 20 November 2026. [@1] The Intermediate Court sentenced
him to three years of penal servitude and a fine of Rs 25,000. [@1] Two days
spent in police custody and 49 days on remand are to be deducted from the
sentence. [@1]

The case dates to 5 September 2025. [@1] The Trou-aux-Biches ADSU, which had
targeted the suspect since a 19 August operation based on information, went
early that morning to wasteland at Roche-Bois. [@1] Emilien fled, abandoning a
brown leather bag containing aluminium foil and cellophane sachets. [@1] The
court found that 24.40 g of drugs in five individually wrapped black packets
were seized, with resale value assessed at Rs 122,000 at Rs 5,000 per gram. [@1]
Police had initially valued the seizure at Rs 50,400, and the Forensic Science
Laboratory report was placed on file. [@1]

A police officer told the court that the accused had fully cooperated during the
investigation and admitted the facts without evasion. [@1] Emilien, a father of
two, admitted from the dock that he had made a mistake and was represented by
assigned counsel. [@1] He was found guilty under sections 30(1)(f)(ii) and
47(5)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs Act, while synthetic cannabinoids under Schedule
I, Part II carry up to Rs 1 million and five to 20 years. [@1] Magistrate Essoop
held that the five-year minimum would be disproportionate in light of comparable
cases, crediting a timely guilty plea, absence of previous convictions,
apologies and cooperation. [@1] The magistrate stressed the dangerousness of
synthetic drugs, recalling Supreme Court case law describing them as more toxic
and addictive than heroin, and found aggravating factors outweighed mitigation
so a severe prison term was required. [@1]
