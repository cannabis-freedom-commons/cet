Ñuble prosecutors, PDI detectives and Gendarmería dismantled five criminal
structures that brought drugs into the Chillán prison by throwing packages into
the exercise yards. [@2] Fourteen people were detained in the operation, among
them operators who worked outside the prison and kept links with organisations
running inside it. [@2] All of them were arrested in the early hours of Tuesday
17 November 2026. [@1] A Chillán guarantee court formalised the accused for
illicit drug trafficking and set a four-month investigation period. [@4] Eight
of the 14 were remanded in pre-trial detention. [@1] One accused was placed
under provisional internment, and five were given total house arrest plus bans
on leaving the country and on contacting each other. [@1] Prosecutor Andrés
Salgado of the Ñuble prosecutor's criminal-analysis unit, the SAC, said the five
structures were run from inside the prison with operational arms outside. [@2]

More than 200 detectives executed 22 search warrants at homes in Chillán,
Chillán Viejo and San Carlos. [@2] PDI prefect Nelson Contreras, acting head of
the Ñuble police region, said the house raids turned up close to seven kilos of
drugs. [@1] Gendarmería carried out simultaneous interventions at the prisons of
Chillán and Rancagua. [@2] The heads of the five structures were moved to other
prisons around the country, Gendarmería's acting regional director, colonel
Cristopher Parra Carvajal, said. [@1] The regional prosecutor, Rolando
Canahuate, said the drugs doubled or multiplied fivefold in sale value once
inside the prison. [@3]

The packages thrown into the prison yards held coca paste, marijuana, cocaine,
ketamine, clonazepam and bulking agents. [@1] The drugs were bought in Chillán,
Chillán Viejo and San Carlos, then stored, dosed and packed to survive the
throws. [@1] Inmate couriers, known as correos, collected the packages inside
and handed them to prisoners who resold them to other inmates. [@1] Four men
specialised in the throws and trained the technique to reach the prison yard,
said subprefect Jonathan Silva, head of the PDI anti-narcotics brigade. [@1] The
throwers were paid between 80,000 and 100,000 pesos for each throw. [@2] The
investigation ran for two years and used wiretaps, filming, surveillance and
analysis of phones seized from inmates and from people arrested for drug
trafficking. [@4]
