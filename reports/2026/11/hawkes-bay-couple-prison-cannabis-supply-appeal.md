A Hawke's Bay business couple supplied cannabis to a Mongrel Mob member in
prison. [@1] The offending involved communicating with a jailed Mongrel Mob
member and supplying cannabis which he sold in prison. [@1] Their offending
occurred in early 2023. [@1] The couple were due to stand trial in late 2024 but
pleaded guilty to charges of conspiring to sell cannabis a week before the trial
was to commence. [@1] The couple have had name suppression since their arrest in
early 2023. [@1] Intercepted phone messages between the couple and the prisoner,
who had smuggled a cellphone into prison, were produced in evidence. [@1]

Their lawyer said one ounce of cannabis entered the prison, which the prisoner
turned into 130 joints sold at $20-$30 each, with no commercial gain to the
couple who gave it at no cost. [@1]

After pleading guilty in 2024 they indicated they would apply for a discharge
without conviction and for permanent name suppression. [@1] Those applications
went before Judge Russell Collins in the Napier District Court in July last
year. [@1] The applications for permanent name suppression and for a discharge
without conviction were both declined. [@1] They appealed to the Court of
Appeal, which heard the case in Wellington in July before Justices Ellis, Mander
and Gault. [@1] In their decision released on Tuesday the judges ruled that the
couple knew what they were doing and later sought to minimise their actions.
[@1] The judges said the impact of drugs in prison was the key aggravating
factor of the offending. [@1] The couple's appeal against the refusal of a
discharge without conviction was dismissed. [@1] The couple's appeals against
refusal to grant permanent name suppression were dismissed. [@1] They were
granted interim name suppression to allow time to file an appeal to the Supreme
Court. [@1]
