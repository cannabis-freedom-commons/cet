On 19 November 2026, Republican Police seized 212.60 kilograms of Indian hemp
and arrested four people in Avogbanna, a district of Bohicon municipality. [@1]
The operation followed months of intelligence on illicit transportation of
psychotropic substances through the area. [@1] Police had been informed of the
convoy's imminent passage when a mechanical failure forced the van to stop at a
garage close to the police station. [@1] Police kept the garage under
surveillance through the night before intervening at the opportune moment. [@1]
Officers apprehended four suspects, including the driver, his escort and two
mechanics present at the scene. [@1]

Welders had built a large hidden compartment beneath the van's floor, closed by
bolted side panels so the vehicle looked completely empty. [@1] The van was
towed to Avogbanna police station and searched in the constant presence of the
apprehended men. [@1] Unscrewing the side panels revealed 406 packets of Indian
hemp carefully packaged in the secret compartment. [@1] Officers also found
several Ghanaian and Nigerian licence plates meant to disguise the vehicle's
identity during border crossings. [@1]

In preliminary hearings the four suspects named an Avogbanna resident who heads
a Nigeria-based import-export company as the true owner of the vehicle and
cargo. [@1] A search of the alleged owner's home was unsuccessful and he is now
the subject of an active search warrant. [@1] Police presented the case as a
setback for a transnational drug trafficking network operating in Zou
department. [@1]
