The Civil and Criminal Chamber of the Castilla y León High Court (TSJCyL)
confirmed the two-year-three-month prison sentence that the Audiencia Provincial
de León imposed on a woman for drug trafficking causing serious harm to health.
[@1] The high court rejected her claims of a breach of the presumption of
innocence, error in the assessment of the evidence and a disproportionate
sentence, and gave full credibility to the National Police officers who
witnessed the handover. [@1] The woman had appealed the Audiencia Provincial's
judgment over the June 2024 events. [@4]

The ruling fixed the handover at about 17:45 on 20 June 2024 at the junction of
San Antonio and Peña Pinta streets in León, near the city's hospital. [@3]
National Police officers on patrol there saw the woman approach a man waiting by
a car and hand him two plastic wrappers closed with a pink rubber band. [@2] The
wrappers held 2.73 grams net of cannabis resin, valued at 18.34 euros, and 0.52
grams net of cocaine of 64.78 percent purity, valued at 42.30 euros. [@2] The
buyer told officers at the scene that the woman had just handed him the
substances, but at trial he and the accused denied a transaction and said they
were friends who had arranged to go to a supermarket. [@2]

The woman had a final 2017 conviction from the Audiencia de Zamora, which
sentenced her to four and a half years in prison for a similar offence. [@2] The
high court said the Audiencia had already fixed the legal minimum within the
range of two years three months to three years, so the sentence was not
disproportionate. [@2] The judgment can be appealed in cassation to the Supreme
Court's Second Chamber within five days. [@2]
