Forty-one (former) members, sympathisers and acquaintances of the Hells Angels,
including alleged leaders Carlo D. and Gianluca P., appeared before the Tongeren
criminal court on charges of arson, drugs, weapons and assault. [@1] The
prosecutor sought no prison terms and agreed to a simple declaration of guilt
for all defendants because the case had stalled for too long. [@1] The defence
asked the court to declare the prosecution expired and impose no further
penalties. [@1] Alternatively, it asked for acquittal for lack of evidence and
doubt. [@1]

In March 2018 investigators raided eighteen locations. [@1] The federal judicial
police found an arsenal including a rocket launcher, eight firearms, 800
cartridges and crossbows. [@1] They also found about three kilos of speed,
hashish and other items including Nazi paraphernalia, bulletproof vests and
police logos. [@1] In May 2017, after an anonymous tip about a square farm in
Herk-de-Stad, investigators uncovered an inactive drug lab in the stables. [@1]
The prosecution also linked an active amphetamine lab in Zulte to the case. [@1]
The town is claiming 17,000 euros for clearing the Herk-de-Stad lab. [@1] The
case also covers the deliberate torching of a Peugeot 807 owned by the ‘Lustige
Bikers’ non-profit. [@1]

The inquiry began almost ten years ago after a former Hells Angels leader who
wanted to leave the club received threats and then brought incriminating
information to police. [@1] According to the prosecutor, the clubhouse in Genk
served as the base of operations. [@1] The prosecutor blamed high workload and
higher-priority investigations for the delay. [@1] A verdict is expected in a
month. [@1]
