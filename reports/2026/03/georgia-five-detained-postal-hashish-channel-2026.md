Police in Georgia uncovered a large channel bringing drugs into the country by
post disguised as food supplements, according to the Interior Ministry. [@1]
Five people were detained in the case, citizens of Georgia, Russia, Kyrgyzstan
and Ukraine. [@1] The ministry did not say whether those people were connected
to one another. [@1] Investigators said the detained people planned to sell
narcotics and psychotropic substances in especially large quantities that had
been delivered to them from abroad in disguised parcels. [@1]

It remains unclear who sent the drugs. [@1] Police carried out a controlled
delivery of the parcels, which confirmed the suspects' criminal intent. [@1]
Officers seized at least 20 kilograms of narcotics and psychotropic substances
as physical evidence from the parcels and from the suspects' homes. [@1] The
seized items included 311 MDMA tablets, up to 9 kilograms of mephedrone, more
than 2 kilograms of methadone, up to 4 kilograms of hashish, up to 1.5 kilograms
of clephedrone, marijuana, and a mixture of ketamine and alpha-PVP. [@1]

The investigation is continuing under four articles of Georgia's Criminal Code
covering illegal purchase and storage, attempted purchase and storage, and
facilitation of sale of narcotic and psychotropic substances in an especially
large amount. [@1] The detainees face up to 20 years' imprisonment or life
imprisonment. [@1]
