A police sergeant and her civilian spouse were freed of drug-related charges
four years after they were arrested. [@1] Sergeant Tamica Taylor and Royan
Harris walked free from the St Thomas Parish Court on Monday after a judge
upheld their attorney's submission that there was no case for them to answer.
[@1] Attorney Marcus Goffe said the judge, in dismissing the charges, described
the evidence as tenuous. [@1] Goffe said his clients were happy with the
outcome, which he said should not have been brought in the first place. [@1]

Harris was arrested in February 2021 and charged with possession of, dealing in
and cultivating ganja after a search of his house and a small farm. [@1] Taylor,
his partner and a police sergeant, was arrested a year later and charged with
possession of and dealing in ganja. [@1] The charges were laid on a legal
opinion from the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions. [@1] Taylor was
suspended without pay after she was charged. [@1] Police found more than 900
cannabis plants at the property in Dumfries, St Thomas, most of which Taylor
said officers destroyed during their operation. [@3] Taylor said Harris was a
caretaker at a Rastafarian camp where cannabis was grown for sacramental
purposes. [@3] She said police charged him even after a priest from the camp
came to the station to claim the cannabis. [@3]

The acquittal was handed down at the Morant Bay Parish Court in St Thomas. [@3]
An expert from a government laboratory testified that there were no systems in
place between 2015 and 2024 to test the seized substance to determine exactly
what it was. [@3] Goffe said the 2015 Dangerous Drugs Amendment Act meant that
only cannabis with a THC content of 1.0 per cent or higher qualifies as ganja.
[@3] Taylor had filed internal complaints about sexual harassment and
victimisation, her attorney said. [@2] She said she would seek compensation for
her lost salary at the appropriate time. [@3]
