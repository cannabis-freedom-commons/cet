Immigration police arrested an Israeli drug dealer and two Australian associates
on Koh Phangan in Surat Thani province, seizing drugs believed intended for sale
to foreign tourists. [@1] The operation was announced on 8 March by Pol Maj Gen
Chutharet Yingyongdamrongsakul, commander of Immigration Division 6, after the
arrest of a Brazilian suspect named Pedro on Koh Phangan on 5 March. [@1]
Officers located the Israeli national, identified only as OR, at Coconut Lane in
Koh Phangan subdistrict and escorted him to his accommodation for inspection.
[@1]

The room search uncovered 389.35 grams of cocaine, some in opaque white blocks
that authorities believed had recently been smuggled into the country concealed
inside the body. [@1] Other drugs found included ecstasy in powder and tablet
form, ketamine and cannabis resin. [@1] Investigators also found equipment used
to divide and package the drugs, which they believed was for preparing
substances for distribution among customers in the tourist area. [@1] All the
drugs and equipment were seized and OR was arrested on charges of illegally
possessing and distributing narcotics, then handed to investigators at Koh
Phangan Police Station. [@1] Thairath reported ecstasy in powder and pill form,
ketamine, cannabis resin and various drug repackaging tools among the drugs
found in OR's room. [@2] Thai Newsroom reported that the cannabis resin and
packaging devices were found in the suspect's hotel room alongside 389.35
grammes of cocaine. [@3]

Kian and Mika, both Australian nationals, were encountered inside the same
accommodation. [@1] A drug test found cocaine in Kian's system and he was
arrested on charges of illegal drug use. [@1] Mika refused an order from
officers of the Office of the Narcotics Control Board to undergo a drug test and
was charged with resisting the lawful order of an authorised narcotics officer.
[@1] The police commander said the operation followed government policy under
Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul and National Police Chief Kittirat Phanphet
to intensify action against transnational crime in tourist destinations. [@1]
