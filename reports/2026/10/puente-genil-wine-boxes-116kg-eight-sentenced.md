The Second Section of the Córdoba Provincial Court sentenced eight people to a
combined 17 and a half years in prison for drug trafficking and money laundering
in a network operating in Puente Genil that included a marijuana plantation,
cocaine distribution and bulk drug transport. [@1] The sentence was issued on 22
October and was declared final with the agreement of the parties. [@1] The court
suspended the prison terms of seven of the eight convicted people on condition
they do not reoffend for two years, and deferred the decision on the eighth
until the enforcement stage. [@1] The court acquitted all the defendants of the
criminal-group charge after the prosecution withdrew it, and also acquitted a
ninth defendant against whom no charges were maintained. [@1] The court applied
mitigation for undue delay to all eight because proceedings lasted more than
five years, plus mitigation linked to drug dependence and, for the transport
businessman, confession for helping clarify the facts. [@1]

On 23 June 2021 the Guardia Civil intercepted a lorry on the A-318 toward Puente
Genil that had come from an industrial warehouse in Herrera, Seville. [@1] Its
trailer carried four pallets of red-wine boxes in which some of the bottles had
been replaced with vacuum-packed bags of marijuana. [@1] Officers seized 218
bags holding 115.976 kilos of drugs, valued at nearly 596,000 euros. [@1] The
earlier Guardia Civil account of the same interception said officers found more
than 200 vacuum-packed bags of marijuana hidden in wine boxes in the lorry
passing through Puente Genil, weighing 115 kilos. [@2] The lorry was driven by a
woman accompanied by her partner, the manager of a freight-transport company,
and both received the heaviest fines of 300,150 euros each plus two years in
prison each. [@1]

In March 2021 the investigation uncovered an indoor plantation of 164 marijuana
plants in a house in Puente Genil. [@1] The Guardia Civil seized more than seven
kilos of cannabis there, worth over 40,000 euros. [@1] Its grower was sentenced
to two years in prison and a 22,000-euro fine, plus a further penalty for
defrauding electricity through an illegal grid connection. [@1] Cocaine and a
precision scale were seized at the home of a married couple and the husband's
son who distributed drugs. [@1] The son was additionally convicted of money
laundering and given a further year and a half in prison and a 22,000-euro fine
after buying a 40,000-euro home with almost half paid in cash despite declared
income of only 4,345 euros between 2017 and 2020. [@1]
