The Himachal Pradesh High Court upheld the acquittal of three accused persons in
a case involving the alleged recovery of 2.002 kg of charas under the NDPS Act,
holding that a discrepancy in the colour of the tape used to seal the seized
contraband raised doubts about the identity and safe custody of the case
property. [@1] The court noted that police witnesses said the contraband was
wrapped in yellow tape when seized, but it was found wrapped in brown tape when
opened before the Trial Court. [@1] The Division Bench of Justice Vivek Singh
Thakur and Justice Bhupesh Sharma remarked that the case property before sealing
was wrapped with yellow tape, but on being opened in the court it contained two
polythene packets wrapped with brown tape containing charas. [@1] The case arose
from an incident dated 26 December 2016, when a police team intercepted a
Mahindra XYLO vehicle at Dohri Diwar near the Subathu bifurcation in Solan,
Himachal Pradesh, acting on secret information about the transportation of
narcotics. [@1] During the search, the police allegedly recovered 2.002 kg of
charas from a backpack kept between the legs of accused Kapil @ Dhillo, who was
travelling in the vehicle with two other accused persons. [@1] The three were
subsequently charged under Sections 20 and 29 of the NDPS Act. [@1]

The Special Judge, Solan, acquitted them on 10 September 2018, citing
inconsistencies in the prosecution evidence, and the State challenged the
acquittal before the Himachal Pradesh High Court. [@1] The High Court observed
that the prosecution failed to establish the recovery of charas from the
conscious possession of the accused persons, as the two independent witnesses
did not support the alleged recovery. [@1] It further noted material
contradictions in the testimonies of police officials regarding the receipt of
secret information, recording of reasons of belief under Section 42(2) of the
NDPS Act and the place where the backpack was searched. [@1] The court also
found discrepancies in the colour of the tape used to wrap the seized contraband
and doubts regarding custody of the seal, raising questions about the identity
and safe custody of the case property. [@1] Holding that the trial court had
taken a possible view based on the evidence, the High Court dismissed the
State's appeal and upheld the acquittal. [@1] The decision is dated 29 October
2026. [@1]
