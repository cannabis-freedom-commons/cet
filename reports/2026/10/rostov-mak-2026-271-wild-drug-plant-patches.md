Police in Rostov Oblast reported destroying 271 patches of wild drug-containing
plants covering 19,069 square metres during the regional operation Mak-2026.
[@1] The operation's stated aim was preventing crime in the trade in drugs of
natural origin, and the Don region is among the territories best suited to
growing drug-containing plants. [@1] Besides the Rostov Oblast interior ministry
headquarters, the operation involved the FSB border directorate for the region,
the North Caucasus transport police, the Defence Ministry, Rosgvardia, the
Agriculture Ministry, Cossack organisations and regional authorities. [@1] More
than 500 interior ministry officers went on duty every day during the
operation's active phase. [@1]

Officers issued more than a hundred official orders to destroy such patches, and
all were carried out on time. [@1] Police drew up 322 administrative offence
reports and solved 159 drug crimes. [@1] About 35 kg of banned substances were
taken out of circulation, of which 29,073 grams were of plant origin. [@1]

The operation's prevention work covered wild cannabis growing on vacant lots,
abandoned fields and household plots. [@1] Some of those detained during the
operation were growers who kept plantations at their homes. [@1] Growing
drug-containing plants on a large scale, from 20 cannabis plants, is a crime
under article 231 of the Russian Criminal Code, punishable by a fine of up to
300,000 roubles or up to two years in prison, and by up to eight years for a
group or on an especially large scale. [@1] Landowners and land users must
destroy wild cannabis growing on their land themselves, and ignoring an official
order carries an administrative fine. [@1]

The multi-agency operation in Rostov Oblast was reported completed on 27 October
2026. [@2]
