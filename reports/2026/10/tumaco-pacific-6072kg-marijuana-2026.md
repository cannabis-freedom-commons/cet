Tumaco Coast Guard units detected a Go Fast motorboat in the southern Pacific
whose crew tried to escape when they noticed the authorities. [@1,2] The attempt
started a pursuit that ended with the interception of the boat and the capture
of two Colombian citizens. [@1,2] The two detainees and the seized material were
taken to port and then placed at the disposal of the judicial authorities with
the confiscated substance. [@1,2] The Navy had not reported the identities of the
detainees or the criminal structure to which they were allegedly linked. [@1,2]

Inspecting the boat, officers found 240 bales in different compartments
containing 12,125 rectangular packages initially identified as narcotics. [@1,3]
Criminal-investigation (Sijín) officers in port ran the homologated preliminary
identification test, known as the PIPH, on the material. [@1,2] The test confirmed
the cargo was 6,072 kilograms of marijuana, more than six tonnes. [@1,2] The Navy
calculated that the shipment would be worth more than 6.3 million dollars on
illegal international markets. [@1,2] The military forces said the operation prevented the distribution of about one million doses of narcotics on illegal international markets. [@2] Naval intelligence indicated Costa Rica as
the intended destination, a country used as a transit point on maritime
drug-trafficking routes toward international markets. [@1,2]

The Navy did not state the marijuana's embarkation point, the organization
owning the shipment, or whether transshipments were planned during the voyage.
