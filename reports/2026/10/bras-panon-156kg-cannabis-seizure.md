A Mauritian court kept suspected skipper Ravindra Chimajee in custody on 20 October 2026 over an alleged attempt to import cannabis from Réunion. [@19] He was placed in pretrial detention at Beau-Bassin prison on a provisional allegation of conspiracy to import cannabis. [@19]

## The Bras-Panon seizure

Witnesses alerted Réunion gendarmes after seeing two men moving luggage towards a boat near the Rivière du Mât at Bras-Panon on 26 July. [@1,2,3,5,21] Officers found cannabis in ten sacks and arrested two men, who were later brought before a prosecutor. [@1,2,3,5,23] The gendarmerie deployed roughly 20 officers, divers and a helicopter, which spotted a boat leaving the area. [@5] One account said a boat seen offshore was found the next day near Le Morne, Mauritius. [@9,26] Local reports gave weights from 150 to 157 kg, while Mauritian accounts of the case used 155.7 kg and estimated the shipment’s value at 234 million Mauritian rupees. [@2,6,7,8,9,12,13,18,26]

## Arrests and boat seizures

Mauritian police arrested Yogajee Govind, known as Gulshan, at Chamarel on 31 July, and separately arrested Chimajee at a clinic in Baie-du-Tombeau. [@9,10,11,12,13,17,21,23,26] Govind and Chimajee were remanded after being charged in August with conspiracy to import cannabis, and Govind also faced cultivation and methadone charges. [@10,26] A search of the family home uncovered four cannabis plants. [@21,23] Govind is the husband of minister delegate Véronique Leu-Govind, who stayed in government after the opposition called for her resignation. [@9,11,12,13,21,23] Police in Mauritius announced a fifth arrest on 4 September, and Réunion La 1ère reported that ten people were being prosecuted by early September, five on each island. [@4,6,7,20,25] Police are interested in Rutnee’s relations with skipper Kris Chimajee and his connection with Shaan Kumar Choolun, alias Mithun, whose interrogation began at the end of the previous week with priority given to ammunition seized at his home at Dilo Pourri, Au Morne, while his alleged role in the Réunion drug seizure is to be examined later. [@20] Rutnee appeared before the Weekend Court on Saturday and was returned to police custody, to reappear on Monday before the Bambous court. [@20] A search of Rutnee’s home seized a two-piece diving suit, a GoPro camera, clothes, a sports bag and a mobile phone with a damaged screen, which investigators are examining for possible use in the maritime part of the alleged traffic. [@20] The fifth suspect was 19-year-old Vikish Rutnee, alias Marvesh, arrested on Friday at La-Gaulette by Anti-Drug and Smuggling Unit officers of the Western Division on suspicion of links to the network behind the late-July attempt to bring 156 kg of cannabis from Réunion to Mauritius. [@20] Investigators also examined a possible link with a separate case in Madagascar. [@15,16]

On 7 September Mauritius’s Financial Crimes Commission seized two speedboats from Chimajee’s home and moved them to its Réduit Triangle headquarters. [@4,14,18,22] On the morning of 7 September the Financial Crimes Commission searched the home of former Junior Minister for Arts and Culture Véronique Leu-Govind in connection with the investigation into the 156 kg cannabis seizure in Réunion. [@20] She said she was keeping her seat as a member of parliament. [@24] Véronique Leu-Govind, the Mauritian MP and Junior Minister for Arts and Culture, resigned her ministerial post and announced on 3 August 2026 that she was also leaving the presidency of the Nouveaux Démocrates party. [@24] On Wednesday morning a former junior minister was summoned to the FCC to explain the purchase of a German-brand sedan, denied wrongdoing and left free, according to L'Express de Maurice. [@22] Officers were to examine the boats for cannabis traces and analyse satellite phones, documents and other devices seized in the searches. [@4,14] During the searches officers found several empty cartons that had reportedly been used to package satellite phones, according to Défimédia. [@22] Besides the two boats, Financial Crimes Commission inspectors seized a large vehicle, computers and mobile phones. [@22] The commission also examined possible money laundering linked to the alleged network. [@14,20,22] Financial analysts were examining the accounts of the seven people arrested, and the alleged brains of the network were also said to own real estate. [@22] The owner of the outboard motors and the man who surrendered at FCC headquarters were taken to the Bambous court, placed in provisional detention and told they were suspected of money laundering. [@22] The Leu-Govind search was one of five searches at suspects’ homes in the west aimed at pursuing possible money laundering, financing of drug dealing and asset recovery. [@20]

Two men described as the heads of the network were arrested around the searches: the first, known as L'Intelligent, during the speedboat seizure, and the second when he surrendered the next day accompanied by his lawyer, according to L'Express de Maurice. [@22]

Investigators also became interested in the presumed use of the deputy's mobile phone as part of their enquiries. [@24]
