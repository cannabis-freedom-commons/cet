Cuban customs found a cargo of marijuana hidden inside boxes of cereal and other
food products at José Martí International Airport in Havana, in what the
authorities described as an international drug-trafficking operation. [@2] The
General Customs of the Republic of Cuba said the seizure was the result of joint
work with the Anti-Drug Agency of the Interior Ministry. [@1] The discovery was
announced on social media by the institution's first deputy chief, Wiliam Pérez
González. [@3]

Pérez González said the marijuana came hidden in packages of cereal and food and
called the unity between Customs and the Anti-Drug Agency the service's greatest
strength. [@1] Customs confirmed the seizure on its Facebook page as part of
prevention work against drug trafficking at the country's main entry points.
[@1] The authorities published no weight, quantity or value for the marijuana.
[@3] They did not say whether anyone was detained. [@4] Officials gave no
details on the drug's exact origin or final destination, but called the case an
international trafficking operation. [@1]

Among the published images were instant drinks of the Mexican brand Zuko and
pancake flour, both widely sold in Mexico. [@2] One photograph showed a luggage
tag with the first letters of “México”, leading the outlets to suggest the
shipment may have arrived from Mexico, a possibility the authorities did not
confirm. [@1] José Martí is Cuba's main point of entry and has seen similar
seizures in recent years. [@4] The seizure was made public days after customs
reported nearly a kilogram of cocaine hidden in jars of cream, coffee and other
food on a separate flight from Panama. [@1]
