Costa Rica's National Coast Guard and the United States Drug Enforcement
Administration intercepted two boats linked to international drug trafficking in
the South Pacific and detained four suspects. [@1] The first boat was 32 feet
long with two 200-horsepower motors and carried no name, flag or registration.
[@2] Two Colombians surnamed Caicedo and Salazar, both 33 years old, crewed it,
with Caicedo listed as captain. [@2] A second Costa Rican-flagged boat named El
Jupiz approached the scene, and authorities presume it came to receive the
illicit cargo at sea. [@2] Two Costa Ricans surnamed Núñez, 38, and Fuentes, 19,
crewed the second boat. [@2] The four detainees were placed at the disposal of
the Public Ministry, which will determine their legal situation. [@2]

The Security Ministry said a fast boat heading toward Costa Rican coasts was
detected at 2:47 a.m. [@2] Coast guard officers located it off Playa Sirena, in
Puerto Jiménez canton on the Osa Peninsula, at 3:10 a.m. [@2] Aboard the first
boat were 16 drums, two of them with fuel, and 52 bales holding 2,443 packages
of apparent marijuana weighing about 1,585 kilos. [@1] Officers took both boats,
the four suspects, the fuel and the apparent drug to Golfito port. [@2] There
the Drug Control Police, working with the Public Ministry, seized the 2,443
packages, about 1,585 kilos of marijuana. [@2]

The operation was carried out on information from the DEA and with support from
a maritime patrol aircraft of the United States Southern Interagency Joint Task
Force. [@1] Coast Guard director Juan Carlos Alvarado said officers of the coast
guard, the Drug Control Police, the Special Support Unit and the Criminal
Intelligence and Analysis Directorate took part with DEA support. [@1] "This new
blow reinforces the joint strategy against organised crime and drug trafficking
in South Pacific waters," the Coast Guard said. [@1]
