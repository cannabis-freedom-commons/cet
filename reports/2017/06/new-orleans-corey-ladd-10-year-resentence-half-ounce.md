In 2011, Ladd was arrested in New Orleans for possessing half an ounce of
marijuana. [@1] Ladd was a passenger in a car pulled over for a broken brake
light in August 2011, and officers found the half-ounce of marijuana in a bag
tucked in his waistband. [@2] In May 2013, a jury convicted Ladd of felony
third-offense marijuana possession. [@2] Prosecutors invoked the
habitual-offender law, citing prior felony convictions for LSD and hydrocodone,
and Judge Karen Herman sentenced him to a 20-year prison term. [@2] After the
Louisiana Supreme Court ordered resentencing, the trial judge reduced the term
only to 17 years without parole. [@1]

In April 2016 the state 4th Circuit Court of Appeal called the 17-year term
excessively harsh and sent the case back for resentencing. [@2] On 9 June 2017,
after the district attorney withdrew the multiple-offender enhancement, Judge
Herman resentenced Ladd to 10 years in prison. [@2] With credit for time served
and good-time credit, Ladd became eligible for immediate release. [@2] He had
already served more than five years in prison and was to spend the remaining
four-and-a-half years on parole. [@1]

His girlfriend was eight months pregnant with daughter Charlee when Ladd was
incarcerated. [@1] By the June 2017 hearing Charlee was five years old and the
two had never met outside of prison. [@2] Ladd planned to live with his mother
in Kenner and work at a landscaping company after release. [@2]
