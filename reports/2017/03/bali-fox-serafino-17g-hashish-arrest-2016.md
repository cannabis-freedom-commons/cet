A former British war correspondent and an Australian were arrested in Bali with
17 grams of hashish in total, police on the island said on Monday, and they face
up to 20 years in prison. [@1] They were David Fox, 54, a former Reuters
correspondent, and Giuseppe Serafino, 48, an Australian businessman, detained on
Saturday for allegedly possessing small quantities of the drug. [@2] The two
were picked up during a police operation on Saturday, 8 October 2016. [@1] Fox
told investigators he had taken cannabis resin for years to cope with stress in
war zones. [@1] He began using hashish because of a Reuters assignment covering
a conflict zone in Somalia, said Nyoman Artana, deputy police chief in Denpasar.
[@2] The judges asked whether he would agree to addiction treatment in an Indonesian psychiatric hospital. [@4] Fox told the court he used the drug to deal with post-traumatic stress from two decades of covering conflict, and that he was addicted to hashish, which helped him sleep. [@4] Judges in Bali suggested sending Fox to a psychiatric hospital for treatment for cannabis addiction. [@4]

Police searched Serafino's home in the Sanur area of south Bali after residents
reported that a foreigner there was using drugs. [@1] Investigators seized about
seven grams of hashish there, supplied by Fox, according to police. [@1] Fox was
arrested later in a bar. [@1,4] He said he bought the drugs from another foreigner in the same bar in Sanur. [@4] Fox said the hashish was for his personal use and that he did not deal drugs. [@4] Ten grams of hashish were seized on him and at his
home. [@1] The Baron reported he had possessed 9.83 grams of hashish. [@5] ABC reported the pair were taken into custody with 17 grams of
hashish between them. [@3] Serafino said he used the drug to help fight cancer,
according to Artana. [@1] Fox worked for Thomson Reuters for more than 20 years,
with postings in Bosnia, Rwanda, Afghanistan and Iraq, and left the agency in
2011. [@1]

Both men faced up to 20 years in prison but avoided the death penalty because of
the small quantities involved. [@1] The Baron reported that they escaped a
capital charge because they were not accused of drug trafficking. [@2] ABC
reported that, if convicted, they faced between five years and life in prison.
[@3] Giuseppe Serafino had not yet been sentenced; prosecutors recommended one year and he was due to learn the outcome the following Tuesday. [@6] Prosecutors had not said whether they would appeal the sentence. [@6] Fox said he would begin rehabilitation after leaving prison. [@6] Head Judge Erwin Djong said Fox admitted what he had done, regretted it, would not repeat it, and had no previous conviction. [@5] The Baron reported he would be released in two months. [@5] ABC reported he had already served most of the sentence and should be released from Kerobokan prison in a few months. [@6] The prosecutor had asked for 12 months. [@5] On 9 March 2017 a Bali court sentenced Fox to seven months in prison for possessing hashish. [@5] At that stage ABC reported he faced a maximum of 12 years in jail, although most minor drug offenders in Indonesia received one or two years. [@4] Indonesia has one of the world's strictest anti-drug laws. [@1] Dozens of
people sentenced to death for drug trafficking, including the Frenchman Serge
Atlaoui, are on death row. [@1]
