U.S. Capitol Police arrested four DCMJ activists who lit cannabis joints on the
Capitol grounds on 24 April 2017. [@1] Speakers gave Jewish, Christian,
Rastafarian and secular humanist blessings while Capitol Police stood watch,
setting arrestees up for a First Amendment defence. [@4] The four were two men
and two women, taken into custody shortly after they started smoking that
afternoon. [@3] Capitol Police charged them with possession under federal law,
spokeswoman Eva Malecki said. [@3] Adam Eidinger, a co-founder of DCMJ, was
among them – his second marijuana-related arrest at a demonstration in less than
a week. [@1] The rally was DCMJ's #Reschedule420 smoke-in, pressing Congress to
remove cannabis from Schedule I. [@2] DCMJ had planned to hold it on the House
steps but moved to the Capitol lawn after Eidinger talked to Capitol Police
officers. [@2] Activists displayed joints, edibles and cannabis oil, and police
made no arrests until joints were lit. [@1]

Eidinger was the only one of those arrested to face trial; the others accepted
community service and a minimal or no fine from the D.C. Office of the Attorney
General. [@5] At D.C. Superior Court on 11 September 2017, Judge Shana Frost
Matini dismissed the possession charge after the government said it was not
prepared to try the case. [@5] The dismissal was without prejudice, leaving the
D.C. Attorney General a year to decide whether to retry him. [@5] Eidinger said
he had been offered three months in jail. [@5] A spokesperson for the D.C.
Attorney General said the office had offered Eidinger the same deal as the
others. [@5]

The smoke-in on 24 April was a planned act of civil disobedience to protest the
continued criminalization of cannabis, which is illegal to possess or use on
federal grounds. [@5] DCMJ also demanded that Congress remove the amendment that
had stopped the city regulating marijuana sales. [@1] "Basically, we have
legalization without commercialization," Eidinger said. [@1]
