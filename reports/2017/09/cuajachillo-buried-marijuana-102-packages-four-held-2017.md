Nicaraguan police detained four people on Thursday 14 September 2017 after
officers found 102 packages of drugs buried in a wooded plot in the comarca
Cuajachillo, in the municipality of Ciudad Sandino, department of Managua. [@1]
The operation began that morning when officers stopped a taxi loaded with drugs
in the El Zumen area of District III, Managua; police sources said the stop was
vital to finding the drug store in Cuajachillo. [@1] The taxi driver, Gabriel
Antonio Montenegro González, was carrying 20 marijuana blocks, and police
suspected he belonged to a group operating in several parts of the country. [@2]
A field test by the criminalistics laboratory came back positive for marijuana
on the load taken from the taxi. [@2] Two of the four detainees were held as the
owners of the drug and two more as suspects under investigation, and all four
were taken to the cells of the Auxilio Judicial Nacional pending prosecution.
[@1]

Officers dug out the packages, buried less than half a metre deep, and counted
102 packages of about one pound each, about 46 kg in total. [@1] The drug was
found about 150 metres from the home of Mayerling Reyes, partner of Elvis Martin
Blandón González, whom police sources named as the suspected owner. [@1] Along
the wooded path officers found 11,380 córdobas in a plastic bag, together with a
microchip and a micro SD card that police suspect hold information on drug
retailers. [@1] Police sources said Blandón is from barrio Santa Ana in District
II of Managua and had been under surveillance. [@1] Investigators drew up an
evidence report to hand to the Public Ministry for prosecution. [@1]

On 26 January 2018 Montenegro, Blandón and Mayerli Eduviges Reyes Arroliga,
described as members of the gang La Bomba, pleaded guilty to internal drug
trafficking before judge Adela Cardoza in Managua. [@3] The court account says
the three had 123 marijuana blocks in total, brought from Honduras for
distribution in Managua drug outlets using taxis and motorcycles, and that they
were arrested in operations in El Zumen and the comarca Filos de Cuajachillo.
[@3] They faced up to 10 years in prison and a fine, with sentencing set for 7
February 2018. [@3]
