The Supreme Court on 28 March 2023 set aside the Allahabad High Court order
dated 17 October 2022 granting bail to Ajay Kumar Singh @ Pappu and allowed the
Union of India appeal. [@1] The respondent-accused is alleged to be involved in
Case No. 687/2021 arising out of Case No. 1/2021 under Sections 8/20/27-A/29/32
of the NDPS Act at Police Station DRI Varanasi. [@1] On 13 February 2023 the
Supreme Court suspended the High Court bail order while issuing notice on the
special leave petition. [@2] Singh had not participated in the investigation,
had avoided arrest for more than one year and was arrested from a restaurant in
Raipur. [@1]

On 11 January 2021 the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence in Varanasi received
information that a truck would carry a huge quantity of ganja from Bhadrachalam
towards Jaunpur via Varanasi. [@1] At around 6 pm on the said date, the
aforesaid truck appeared, moving towards Prayagraj and was apprehended near Raja
Talab. [@1] The person driving the truck introduced himself as Om Prakash Yadav,
resident of district Ballia. [@1] Both occupants denied transporting ganja and
said the truck was loaded with cattle feed, but a search found 6 packets and 135
plastic sacks containing 1005 packets of varying size. [@1] The seized packets
were weighed and their gross weight was found to be 3971.600 kg. [@1] The driver
said an acquaintance of Singh gave him the truck loaded with ganja at Jamshedpur
for safe delivery in lieu of Rs. 50,000. [@1]

Prosecution material including the complaint, FIR and Section 67 statements
described Singh as the kingpin and organiser of the illicit ganja trade. [@1]
The High Court had directed release on bail keeping in mind the larger mandate
of Article 21 in light of Satender Kumar Antil. [@1] The High Court granted bail
mainly because Om Prakash Yadav and Amit Yadav had already been enlarged on
bail. [@1] The Supreme Court held the two co-accused were vicarious agents in
subordinate roles while Singh was the main person in drug trafficking. [@1] The
quantity of ganja recovered was admittedly of commercial quantity. [@1] The High
Court recorded no finding that Singh was prima facie not guilty and unlikely to
reoffend, while his antecedents indicated regular offending. [@1] Singh had been
involved in similar crimes in the past with several cases pending against him.
