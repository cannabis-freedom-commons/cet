Brian Jaftha, who had brought the constitutional case against the cannabis ban, was sentenced to two years in prison for possession, about five years after his arrest. Jaftha was expected to appear in the Windhoek Magistrate's Court on 26 April 2019. [@2] Police said the arrest came during the second phase of Operation Hornkranz, a joint operation by the Namibian Police and the Namibia Defence Force launched in December 2018 to curb crime in Windhoek. [@2] Shikwambi denied that 40 NDF officers were sent to arrest Jaftha. [@3] Police deputy chief inspector Kauna Shikwambi said Jaftha was charged with possession of cannabis with a street value of N$2,040. [@2] On the day of the arrest, police spokesperson Chief Inspector Kauna Shikwambi said Jaftha had been found in possession of marijuana and had not yet been charged. [@3] Ganja Users of Namibia president Brian Jaftha was arrested at his home in Havana Court, Katutura, Windhoek, on Wednesday 24 April 2019. [@2] Ndungula said Jaftha was beaten, kicked and assaulted by a heavily armed army officer. [@3] Ndungula said the officers searched the office without a search warrant. [@3] GUN's Borro Ndungula said 40 Namibia Defence Force officers, five Nampol officers and City Police officers took part in the operation. [@3] The arrest came a day after GUN handed a petition to Parliament calling for the repeal of the apartheid-era law that prohibits cannabis use and trade in Namibia. [@3] Ganja Users of Namibia said Jaftha was arrested at the organisation's office in Havana, Katutura, during what it called a military-style operation. [@3] He said the plant material was never tested and that the investigating officer had died and could not be cross-examined. His lawyer in the constitutional case said the sentence did not end that challenge. [@1]
