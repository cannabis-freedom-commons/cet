Belarusian customs officers intercepted a Lithuanian driver in a 1976 Chevrolet
Corvette on the Belarusian side of the Kamenny Log-Miadininkai checkpoint on the
Belarus-Lithuania border. [@1] The driver was crossing the Belarusian-Lithuanian
border and used the red corridor to file a declaration for temporary import of
his car into the EAEU. [@1] He declared no other goods requiring written
declaration. [@1] His behaviour aroused the customs officers' suspicion. [@1]
Customs sent the vehicle for examination using an inspection-scanning complex.
[@1]

The intercepted load weighed 73 kg of hashish. [@1] The judgment said he had bought 670 hashish plates weighing more than 64 kg through a mobile phone and planned to sell them in Belarus. [@2] A scan and follow-up search
found 134 briquettes of pressed dark-brown substance hidden in a structural
cavity in the rear of the car, closed off by a specially fitted false wall. [@1]
The substance was seized and sent for expert examination to the Lida
interdistrict department of the State Committee of Forensic Examinations for the
Grodno region. [@1] The expert conclusion confirmed that the substance was the
dangerous narcotic drug hashish. [@1]

The Grodno Regional Customs opened a criminal case under Part 2 of Article 328-1
of the Belarusian Criminal Code on illegal movement of narcotic drugs across the
border. [@1] He was prosecuted under part 3 of Article 328 of the Criminal Code for unlawful drug trafficking in a group and part 2 of Article 328-1 for moving drugs across the border in a group. [@2] The article carries a penalty of up to ten years of imprisonment, a
possible punishment rather than a sentence already imposed. [@1] The court press service described the offences as acquiring drugs for sale, storing, transporting and moving them across the EAEU customs border in a large quantity, and set the fine at 250 basic units. [@4] The judgment had not taken legal effect and could still be appealed and protested. [@2] The Chevrolet Corvette and the mobile phone were confiscated as instruments of the crime. [@2] The court stated that he had not inspected the hidden compartment or its contents and did not know he was carrying narcotics. [@2] Dalius did not admit guilt, saying a Lithuanian acquaintance had asked him to carry medicines for prosthetics and beauty and had loaded the car himself. [@2] The fine corresponded to 250 basic units, each valued at 37 rubles. [@3] The court found him guilty and imposed 15 years in a colony and a fine of 9,250 rubles. [@2] On 15 August 2023 the Oshmiany district court in the Grodno region sentenced Vandis Dalius, a Lithuanian citizen, for trying to smuggle the hashish into Belarus in January. [@2]
