Alexander Taytelbaum started the CBD company Mister CBD and the cbd-koning.nl
webshop in September 2019, selling hemp with high CBD and little or no THC. [@1]
In May 2023 about 3,500 of his CBD joints were seized and destroyed from shops
in Amsterdam. [@2] The operation followed the closure of ten shops in the city
over the sale of CBD cannabis. [@1] Three reopened after a preliminary
injunction, while seven Q&Q Tabak & Souvenirs shops stayed closed for three
months until the Amsterdam District Court said the illegality of the products
had not been proved and called the closures unlawful. [@1]

The seized joints had a market value of nearly 7,000 euros. [@1] Taytelbaum says
police never tested them and labelled them as containing THC rather than CBD.
[@1] He says CBD cannabis was taken while CBD oil present was left untouched.
[@1] He filed complaints with the health inspectorate IGJ and the food and
consumer authority NVWA over what he calls selective application of the Opium
Law. [@1]

His products contain at most 0.3 percent THC, the EU limit for industrial hemp.
[@1] He says NVWA and IGJ long told his company that 0.05 percent was the
effective ceiling. [@1] IGJ says it can act when a CBD product has more than
0.05 percent THC, the daily dose exceeds 160 mg of CBD or medical claims are
made. [@1] NVWA says products containing THC fall under the Opium Law. [@1]
Taytelbaum says landlords, courier firms and banks refuse to work with his
company. [@1] His lawyer has spent ten months asking the public prosecutor's
office what happened to the destroyed goods, with fees reaching 3,400 euros.
[@1] In February 2026 the Supreme Court asked the European Court of Justice
whether hemp from certified seed or with no more than 0.3 percent THC can be
treated as criminal. [@1]
