Mike Wittenberg, a 43-year-old from Valley Stream, New York, is home after
spending 18 months in a Dominican Republic prison. [@1] He led C3: Counter
Culture Convention, a networking business for the vape industry, and had held
similar events seven times before without trouble. [@1] The convention at the
centre of the case was held at a resort in Punta Cana, in the Dominican
Republic. [@2] That event took place in July 2023. [@1] He was taken aside to
sign for a 700-pound pallet of promotional items the vendors had shipped for the
convention. [@1] He was handcuffed in the back of a police car. [@2] Dominican
officials found pills, gummies and cartridges containing THC, the active
ingredient in marijuana, in the shipment. [@1] Wittenberg said the goods were
about 10 pounds of hemp-derived products that are federally legal in the United
States. [@1]

He said he was charged with international narcotics trafficking, which carries a
penalty of up to 20 years in the Dominican Republic. [@1] He said he was held
for four hours before being driven across the country and interrogated in
Spanish. [@2] He said inmates and guards threatened him constantly in the
prison. [@1] He said he lost more than 100 pounds in the prison because of
limited water and food. [@1] His mother, Ann Wittenberg, travelled to the
Dominican Republic between chemotherapy sessions and appealed to New York
Governor Kathy Hochul and President Joe Biden for help. [@1]

He said he was offered 18 months if he pleaded guilty, after about a quarter of
a million dollars in lawyers' fees and a letter his mother wrote to the
Dominican president. [@1] He said the United States embassy did nothing beyond
visiting him about once a month to check that he was alive. [@1] A State
Department spokesperson said US citizens are subject to the laws of the
countries they visit, and that the American government can provide assistance
but cannot effect their release. [@1] Wittenberg said he had lost his house and
business but kept his freedom and his family. [@1]
