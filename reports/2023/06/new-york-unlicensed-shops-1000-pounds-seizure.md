Since 7 June 2023, the New York State Office of Cannabis Management and
Department of Taxation and Finance jointly inspected 33 unlicensed storefront
cannabis businesses in New York City, Ithaca and Binghamton under a new
enforcement law signed in May. [@2] The two agencies issued notices of violation
to 31 of those businesses for illegally selling cannabis. [@2] The 31 businesses
also had orders to cease unlicensed activity posted on their doors. [@2] Those
businesses must now appear at an administrative hearing where their final fines
and penalties will be decided. [@2] The June statewide inspections also included
several shops in the Rochester-Finger Lakes region. [@1]

From the 31 businesses, officers seized and catalogued a minimum of 1,000 pounds
of illicit cannabis, with more still to be tabulated. [@2] The governor's office
put the estimated value of the seized cannabis at nearly $11 million. [@1] A
second account described the seizure as about 1,000 pounds of illegal marijuana
products worth about $10 million from 31 shops. [@3] That account also reported
Hochul gave the estimated value range as between $9 million and $11 million.
[@3]

Under the new law, the cannabis office can assess civil penalties starting at
$10,000 per day and rising to $20,000 per day for the most egregious conduct.
[@2] A business that keeps selling after a notice faces up to $20,000 per
additional day, and removing a posted notice can bring a $5,000 fine. [@2] The
state said enforcement would continue weekly as the cannabis office recruits
additional officers. [@2] The state budget provided $5 million for 37 additional
enforcement staff. [@2]
