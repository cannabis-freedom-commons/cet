Bermuda police and HM Customs arrested a 45-year-old man after a joint operation
in which customs officers intercepted a shipment found to contain MDMA, also
called ecstasy, shatter and cannabis. [@1] On Thursday 22 June 2023 the Bermuda
Police Service Drugs Unit was called in, and police and HM Customs ran the
operation under the name Operation Porcini. [@1] Police said the drugs seized in
that shipment had an estimated street value of $2.9 million Bermuda dollars.
[@1]

Officers from the Tactical Division and the Drugs Unit, led by Detective
Inspector Barker, later arrested the man and executed search warrants at
properties in Pembroke and St. George's in relation to the shipment. [@1] Acting
Detective Superintendent Sherwin Joseph, then in charge of the Crime Division,
called the seizure significant and said it had prevented a substantial amount of
controlled drugs from being distributed into the community. [@1] Joseph said
ecstasy in particular can cause severe impairment and permanent brain damage,
and that in some cases its use can lead to stroke, heart attack and death. [@1]

He said police would continue to work with HM Customs to identify and disrupt
the importation, sale and distribution of illicit drugs. [@1] Joseph said the
success of Operation Porcini showed the effectiveness of the joint work with
customs. [@1] A police spokesperson said no further information would be
provided and that the Drugs Unit was continuing its investigation. [@1] The
Royal Gazette reported that the intercepted shipment contained MDMA, known as
ecstasy or molly, with cannabis and the cannabinoid drug shatter, and that
warrants were executed at properties in Pembroke and St George's. [@2]
