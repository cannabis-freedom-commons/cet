Mozambican rapper Azagaia, known for his criticism of the government, announced
he would abandon his career after causing scandal by appearing on an evangelical
church television channel preparing a cannabis-like substance for consumption.
[@1] In a Sunday Facebook statement he said his life was at risk and that he
would offer his silence so he could see his children grow up, pointing to his
albums Babalaze and Cubaliwa. [@1] He said he would live in Namaacha, the land
where he was born, because it was the best he could do, preferring to die there
if he must. [@1]

Days earlier he had appeared on Television Miramar's Atraccoes programme
preparing a cigarette to wrap a substance he himself called suruma, as cannabis
is known in Mozambique. [@1] In that interview he confirmed he had recently been
detained by police for drug possession. [@1] He said he consumes the substance
on supposed medical advice because he suffers from epilepsy. [@1] The detention
was his second, after a July 2011 detention for possession of a few grams of
cannabis. [@1] He was freed a few days later, as had also happened after the
other detention. [@1]

Beyond cannabis problems with the Mozambican authorities, he had been called for
questioning by the Attorney-General's office, which viewed his song Povo no
Poder as incitement to violence. [@1] His songs are not played on public radio
and television. [@1]
