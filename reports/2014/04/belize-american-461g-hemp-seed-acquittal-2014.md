John Downard, a 49-year-old American who had lived in Belize for ten years, was
cleared of importing and distributing hemp seed on Monday 7 April 2014, when a
Belize City magistrate dismissed both charges. [@1] Magistrate Leslie Hamilton
upheld a no-case-to-answer submission by Downard's attorney, Rachael Montejo, at
the close of the prosecution's case. [@1]

Police arrested and charged Downard, a San Ignacio resident and the director of
Vortech Limited, on 4 December 2013, when he returned from a trip to Colorado
with 461 grams of hemp seeds. [@1] He was charged with one count of importation
of a controlled substance and one count of distribution of a controlled drug.
[@1] Downard's attorney said that when police found the seeds they believed they
were cannabis seeds. [@1] The trial began in February before Magistrate Hamilton
and went through several adjournments before the ruling. [@2]

The ruling rested largely on a Belize Forensic Lab test by analyst Diana
Bol-Noble, which reported: “cannabis resin not detected. Morphology of seeds
consistent with fruits from the cannabis plant.” [@1] Hamilton held that with no
resin detected, possession and transport of the seeds did not fall under the
Misuse of Drugs Act. [@1] He also found that the hemp seeds did not fit the
characteristics that define a controlled drug under the act. [@1]

Downard told the court that hemp seed is used in paper and in sprays for
mosquito bites, and that hemp seeds were already being sold in Belize at
Reimer's; he brought a bottle of hemp oil to show. [@1] Channel 5 Belize
reported that the imported substance was hemp seeds, which it said are used for
many purposes. [@2] The prosecutor said at the end of the trial that he would
refer the matter to the Belize Police Department to file a notice of appeal.
[@1]
