Jean-Claude Nestor, found guilty of trafficking heroin and cannabis brought from
Madagascar on the boat Glory 1, appeared before Judge Benjamin Marie Joseph at
the Assises and was sentenced to 30 years in prison. [@1] The 30-year sentence
was pronounced on Friday 21 February 2014 by Judge Benjamin Marie Joseph sitting
at the Assises. [@2] The judge said the 656 days Nestor had already spent in
detention would be deducted from the sentence. [@1] He had been found guilty of
importing 3.5 kg of heroin and 7.8 kg of cannabis aboard the fishing boat Glory
1. [@1] L'Express reported the conviction as three counts: importing 6,780.8
grams of gandia (cannabis), 1,575.1 grams of heroin and 971.3 grams of tablets.
[@2]

Nestor was the watchman of the Glory 1 and was arrested when the drugs were
found. [@1] In 2012, agents of the ADSU, Mauritius's anti-drug and smuggling
unit, had seized heroin and cannabis on the Glory 1, which had come from
Madagascar. [@1] When the boat arrived at the port, a Port-Louis ADSU team and
the National Coast Guard boarded it for a thorough search. [@1] They found
heroin pellets weighing 3.5 kg and 7.8 kg of cannabis, which police valued at 58
million rupees in total. [@1] The drugs were hidden in a raffia bag in the
ship's store. [@1] Nestor admitted in his police statements that he received the
drugs from Malagasy nationals and was to carry them to Mauritius on the Glory 1.
[@1]

Nestor named Jean-Alain Rose, a 48-year-old Roche-Bois resident, as the alleged
mastermind who ordered the drugs in Madagascar, saying he had acted only as a
courier. [@1] Rose was cleared at the assizes at the beginning of January. [@1]
The judge rejected as unfounded Nestor's claim that he had a wife and daughter
in Madagascar, finding he used it to seek greater leniency. [@1] L'Express
reported that the drugs had been concealed in a pipe, then a water tank and
finally the ship's store. [@2]
