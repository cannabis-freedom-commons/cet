Thirteen people were arrested in northern Madagascar: cannabis planters,
receivers, dealers and people employed to carry the drugs on their backs. [@1]
The operation ended on Tuesday 2 November. [@1] Because of the quantity seized,
only part of the drugs could be presented to the prosecutor as evidence. [@2]

Gendarmes seized and burned 5.3 tonnes of Indian hemp (chanvre indien) in the
north of the country. [@1] Officers destroyed dozens of hectares of plantations
and uprooted 1.32 million cannabis plants. [@1] The uprooted plants and the hemp
were burned on the spot. [@2] All the drugs were found across an area totalling
11 hectares. [@2]

The operation was launched on 22 November. [@2] It ran for about ten days, with
gendarmes reaching plantations hidden in nature reserves on the outskirts of
Ambanja and Ambilobe. [@2] The Diana region is often targeted by operations
against drug trafficking. [@1] Officials say such operations have destroyed
hectares of plantations and led to arrests over the years, but the network
regenerates after a lull. [@1] Cannabis from the area supplies the black market
in northern Madagascar, the central west and north-east, and as far as Tana
(Antananarivo). [@2]
