Malian customs officers seized nearly four tonnes of cannabis resin in Djicoroni
Coura, on the outskirts of Bamako, the Directorate General of Customs said in a
statement on Friday 7 May 2021. [@1,2] The operation followed a tip about a
"déchargement frauduleux", an unlawful unloading, early on Tuesday morning in
Djicoroni Coura. [@1,3] The key holder made the officers wait about three hours before opening the store. [@4] Officers carried out the seizure in the store of an
economic operator known to the customs service. [@1,3] Each parcel held three packets of twenty chocolate-coloured bricks. [@4] The load was packed in 107 white polyethylene bags printed « Graines et tubercules de Guinée ». [@4] The resin was stacked beneath cartons of air-conditioner spare parts. [@4] The suspected trafficker,
whom the statement did not name, tried to flee before he was arrested and handed
to the prosecutor of the Republic. [@1,3]

The customs service valued the resin at more than 8 million dollars. [@1,2] The directorate put the weight at 3 tonnes 745 kg and the market value at 4.9 billion CFA francs. [@3] The
suspect first said the load was kola nut, then tobacco and then shisha, before a
laboratory test confirmed cannabis resin pressed into slabs and blocks
resembling bars of soap. [@1,2] The shop owner's son, who opened the store, described the 35-kg sacks as kola nut. [@5] The resin compacts easily, letting traffickers
hide large quantities in small volumes, local observers noted. [@1,2] The customs service described it as the first seizure of such a quantity of cannabis resin in Mali. [@5]

Under Malian law the suspect faces three months to three years in prison and a
penalty of up to three times the value of the seized goods. [@1,3] The directorate said a commission would be convened to incinerate the product. [@3]
