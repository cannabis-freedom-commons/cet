Viliami Na'a was already a prisoner in Tonga serving six years and nine months'
imprisonment for grievous bodily harm when the cannabis decision added another
term to his custody. [@1] The courts item reporting the decision was published
on Monday 24 May 2021 in the early evening. [@1] Its headline described the
outcome plainly as a prisoner getting more jail time for a cannabis conviction.
[@1] The court sentenced him to serve an additional one month of imprisonment
for possession of cannabis on top of the long non-drug custody he was already
serving. [@1]

The possession behind the extra month involved only 0.99 grams of cannabis,
which is less than a single gram. [@1] Possession was the only drug conduct
stated for the additional sentence, while the long existing term of six years
and nine months was for grievous bodily harm rather than for drugs. [@1] The
additional one-month term therefore extended his custody beyond the
grievous-bodily-harm term he was already serving at the time of the cannabis
decision. [@1] The outcome thus left a short cannabis sentence sitting alongside
a much longer non-drug sentence within the same overall period of custody in
Tonga. [@1]
