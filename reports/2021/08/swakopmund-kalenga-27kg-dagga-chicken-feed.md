Kalenga was arrested in June 2021 at Swakopmund when he allegedly received 27 kg
of compressed dagga with a street value of N$1,35 million from a courier
service. [@3] Members of the Namibian Police's drug squad from Otjozondjupa and
Erongo arrested him while he was loading the boxes. [@1] Officers had tracked
the boxes after a suspicious delivery and the smell of dagga from one of them at
the Otjiwarongo depot. [@1] Erongo police said the arrest followed a covert
joint operation between Otjozondjupa and Erongo police. [@2]

Kalenga, 31, appeared before magistrate Conchita Olivier on a charge of dealing
in and/or being in possession of illicit drugs and was said to live at the Osire
refugee camp. [@2] He faced charges of drug trafficking, which he said he would
never have faced had he known the boxes held drugs. [@1] He said a 'Mr Gabriel'
from Windhoek asked him to receive chicken feed for a farm at Henties Bay. [@1]
After the arrest, officers searched his Swakopmund residence and found more
suspicious boxes. [@1]

Kalenga pleaded not guilty, and his lawyer Monty Karuaihe argued he could have
been framed. [@1] His lawyer said he picked up the boxes believing they held
chicken feed. [@1] State prosecutor Beate Mwahi opposed bail, arguing the
charges were serious and the state had a strong case. [@3] Magistrate Olivier
denied bail, saying Kalenga was 'not a good candidate for bail'. [@3] The case
was postponed to October for further investigation. [@3] Police were
investigating whether other people were involved in trafficking the dagga from
Otjiwarongo to Swakopmund. [@3]
