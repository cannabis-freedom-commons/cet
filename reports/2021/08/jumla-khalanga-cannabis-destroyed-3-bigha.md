Security personnel destroyed cannabis growing around Khalanga, the headquarters
of Jumla district. [@1] A joint team of Nepal Police, the Armed Police Force and
the Nepal Army carried out the clearing. [@1] According to the District Police
Office, Jumla, the plants were destroyed near Chandannath Temple at Sela Jiulo,
the bus park, Bhartibada, Hospital Line and Jugadkhola. [@1] Chief District
Officer Bijayakumari Prasai went to the site, and local people joined the
security personnel in pulling down the plants. [@1]

Deputy Superintendent of Police Dan Bahadur Thapa said the team destroyed about
three bighas of cannabis on the Saturday. [@3] Thapa said cannabis is not
cultivated in Jumla but grows naturally in the fields. [@2] He said local youths
had started smoking it and that rising use came with more criminal activity, so
officers were told to destroy all the cannabis they could find. [@1] Police said
one well-grown plant yields half a kilogram to a kilogram of prepared cannabis.
[@2] Traders carry processed cannabis to the Indian market, where police said it
sells for up to 11,000 Indian rupees a kilogram. [@1]

The District Police Office, Jumla, said it had instructed police posts and units
across the district to destroy cannabis. [@2] Ward representatives, civil
society and political party representatives were to take part in the clearing at
every local level. [@4] Police acted in the first week of Bhadra, the Nepali
month that runs from mid-August to mid-September, before the crop matured. [@2]
Cannabis matures from the second or third week of Bhadra, when traders collect
the plants to make the drug. [@2]
