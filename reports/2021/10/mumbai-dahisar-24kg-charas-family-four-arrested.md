Mumbai crime branch units 6 and 7 arrested four members of one family, two of
them women, on Monday 25 October 2021. [@2] The four were Bandu Udanshive, 52, a
garment trader, his daughter Cynthia, 23, his wife Claira, 52, and his
son-in-law Jazar Shaikh, 24. [@2] Officers laid a trap on the western express
highway near Dahisar police chowki in Dahisar (east) and stopped a Santro car
carrying the drugs. [@2] The charas was hidden in cavities in the car's doors
and trunk. [@2] Police said the drugs had been brought from Srinagar in Jammu
and Kashmir. [@2] The two men took the women along and used a private car with a
Delhi number plate to avoid suspicion in Jammu and Kashmir. [@2]

Police seized 24 kg of pure charas, the Santro car, five mobile phones and Rs
45,000 in cash. [@1] Police put the value of the charas at Rs 14.5 crore. [@1]
The four were produced before a magistrate and remanded to police custody until
3 November 2021. [@2] Udanshive had been arrested by the Mumbai Anti-Narcotics
Cell in 2010 for possessing 39 kg of charas and came out of prison about a year
earlier after ten years in jail. [@2] Two of the four lived in Andheri East and
two in Powai. [@2]

An anti-narcotics officer said cannabis from Jammu and Kashmir is supplied to
Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa and some southern states. [@1] The estimated price of
charas in India is about Rs 3 lakh a kilogram, and charas was the city's
second-most smuggled drug, most of it coming from Jammu and Kashmir and then
Himachal Pradesh. [@1]
