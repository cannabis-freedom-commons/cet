Customs officers at the Nioro du Sahel post stopped a Moroccan man driving a
Toyota Hilux on the afternoon of Thursday 24 December 2015. [@1] He had entered
Mali from Mauritania and was to travel on through Algeria to reach Europe. [@1]
Studio Tamani described the driver as a 34-year-old Moroccan. [@3] The Kayes
prosecutor's office opened an investigation, and officials said the man had
partners in the Bamako district. [@1]

Officers found 500 kilograms of cannabis resin hidden in the chassis of the
vehicle. [@2] The vehicle looked empty, but its wheels seemed to carry a load; a
mechanic cut open the chassis and the drug came out. [@1] The Kayes regional
customs directorate valued the haul at 659 million CFA francs. [@1] Customs
described it as the largest seizure in the Kayes region since the start of 2015.
[@2] The load was taken to the regional customs office in Kayes on Saturday 26
December 2015. [@2]

Regional customs director Amadou Konaté said the route from Morocco through
Mauritania and Mali to Algeria, toward Europe, was new to his service. [@2] He
said the resin was made in Morocco for the European market and not for local
consumption. [@2] The Malijet account said the product was better prepared than
ordinary chanvre indien, the cannabis it called the poor man's drug. [@1] The
customs directorate in Bamako announced the seizure, according to the Chinese
state news service republished by Cridem. [@4]
