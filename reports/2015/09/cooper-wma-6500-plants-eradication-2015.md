Public hunters pursuing feral hogs stumbled on a sophisticated marijuana growing camp on the Cooper Wildlife Management Area over the opening weekend of the season. [@1,2] The 14,480-acre public reserve sits about 70 miles northeast of Dallas. [@1,2] Alerted by the hunters, Texas game wardens led a multi-agency investigation of the well-established site tucked in a remote swampy area. [@1]

Wardens found more than 6,500 mature plants with an estimated street value of more than 6 million dollars. [@1,2] The camp held tents, farming tools, fertilizer, a generator and water-pump irrigation equipment, indicating the operation had run for some time. [@1,2] Captain Steve Stapleton said the camp sat at least a mile from any road in harsh conditions and that its crew had clearly spent day and night there. [@1,2]

An extensive air-and-ground search by game wardens, the Delta and Hopkins county sheriff's offices, prison tracking dogs and state troopers netted no arrests. [@1] Officials destroyed all the marijuana on site and seized the property and equipment. [@1] Warden Chris Fried called the habitat destruction the worst part: mature hardwoods cut down, parts of a levee cleared, and insecticides and pesticides left to run off into streams. [@1]
