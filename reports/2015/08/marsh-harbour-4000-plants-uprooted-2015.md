Police uprooted more than 4,000 marijuana plants on Tuesday after officers from
the Drug Enforcement Unit discovered three large marijuana fields in Marsh
Harbour, Abaco. [@1] No arrest was made, and investigations were continuing.
[@1]

Superintendent Samuel Butler, officer-in-charge of the Drug Enforcement Unit,
said the discovery near the southern end of Marsh Harbour was one of the biggest
of the year. [@1] Shortly after 2pm, Bahamian police and officers from Operation
Bahamas, Turks and Caicos, known as OPBAT, were on routine patrol when they
found the three fields in pine forest. [@1] The plants ranged from 6 inches to 6
feet in height and were all uprooted. [@1]

Chief Superintendent Sam Butler said the plants had an estimated street value of
$2 million. [@2] He said Drug Enforcement Unit officers and their international
counterparts spotted the large fields during a helicopter patrol, secured the
fields for evidence, and destroyed a portion of one field on site. [@2] The
plants were all uprooted, and Butler said officers secured the fields for
evidence while a portion of one field was destroyed on site. [@2] Investigators
were following leads, and no arrests had been made by the following day. [@2]
Butler said police were satisfied they were making an impact on drug trafficking
and cultivation and urged the public to keep supplying information. [@2]
