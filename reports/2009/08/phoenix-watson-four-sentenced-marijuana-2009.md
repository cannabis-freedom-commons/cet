A federal judge in Phoenix sentenced four people for their roles in a
large-scale marijuana trafficking and money-laundering organization. [@1] The
sentencing hearing was held on 10 August 2009. [@2] Joy Doreen Watson, 45, of
Sherman Oaks, California, the organization's leader, was sentenced to 18 years
in prison. [@1] Juan Lomas Jr., 29, of Phoenix, received 108 months in prison, a
$15,000 fine and five years of supervised release. [@1] Stewart Edwards, 36, a
Jamaican resident alien, received 60 months in prison and four years of
supervised release. [@1] Joel Eras-Machado, 28, a Mexican citizen living
illegally in the United States, received 54 months in prison and three years of
supervised release. [@1]

A jury found Watson and Edwards guilty in April 2009 of conspiracy to possess
and possession with intent to distribute more than 100 kilograms of marijuana,
and Watson was also convicted of conspiracy to commit money laundering. [@1]
Eras-Machado was found guilty of possessing between 50 and 100 kilograms with
intent to distribute. [@1] Lomas had pleaded guilty in March 2009. [@1] Edwards
and Eras-Machado faced potential deportation after their prison terms. [@1]

DEA agents seized more than 450 pounds (about 204 kilograms) of marijuana from
two suspected stash houses in Laveen, Arizona. [@1] The marijuana was intended
for a planned 760-pound deal at $550 a pound. [@1] The DEA put the bulk
transaction value of the deal at $418,000. [@1] Eras-Machado and others brought
about 200 pounds (90 kilograms) to one of the Laveen stash houses for the larger
transaction. [@1] Investigators found bank accounts in another person's name
with nearly $400,000 in deposits over eight months, and receipts showing cash
purchases used to promote the organization. [@1]

The investigation was conducted by the DEA, IRS Criminal Investigation and the
U.S. Postal Inspection Service. [@1] Prosecutors Jennifer E. Green and Glenn B.
McCormick handled the case. [@1]
