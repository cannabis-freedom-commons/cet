Upper Arlington police charged Scott Macre, 43, with endangering children,
possession of drugs and obstructing official business after his 10-year-old
daughter took his THC gummies to Windermere Elementary School. [@1] Court
records say she found five 50-mg THC gummies in a glass jar in a kitchen cabinet
and took them to school. [@1] She believed they were leftover Easter candy and
put them in her lunchbox for herself and some friends. [@1] She and four other
fourth-grade pupils, all 11 or younger, ate the gummies at lunch. [@1] Five
students were sent from school to the hospital after the 22 April 2022 incident.
[@2] All five showed signs of impairment and were nauseous, with hallucinations
and elevated heart rates, and were taken to Nationwide Children's Hospital for
treatment. [@1] Macre told police he used the gummies for a medical condition
and usually kept them in his room. [@1] He said he fell asleep downstairs after
using them on the night of 21 April and put them in a kitchen cabinet where his
daughter could reach them when he woke. [@1] When detectives came to his home,
he asked them to wait outside and put the remaining gummies down the kitchen
garbage disposal. [@1] He was not arrested and was issued a summons to appear in
Franklin County Municipal Court on 11 May. [@1]

Macre pleaded guilty in May 2022 to inducing panic, obstructing official
business and misdemeanor possession of a controlled substance. [@2] The
child-endangering charge was changed to inducing panic. [@3]

He learned his sentence in Franklin County Municipal Court on 14 June 2022. [@2]
He was sentenced to 180 days in jail, suspended for two years pending community
control. [@2] The obstruction charge brought 90 days in jail, also suspended for
two years pending community control. [@2] He was ordered to pay court fees, a
$300 fine on the inducing panic charge and $5,000 restitution to the families,
divided so each received at most $1,250. [@2] The possession charge carried a
further $100 fine. [@2] Judge James P. O'Grady read victim impact statements
from two of the families at the sentencing. [@2] Macre told the court he was
truly sorry for all of the worry and the turmoil the families experienced. [@2]
