During the operation, police arrested five people and charged them with crimes
against public health and electricity fraud. [@1] The investigation began in
March 2021, when investigators found a 32-hectare plot in Vilassar de Mar, about
30 kilometres up the coast from Barcelona, being used to grow hemp, or cannabis
plants, on an industrial scale. [@1]

The National Police and the Guardia Civil said in a joint statement that they
had confiscated just over six tonnes of marijuana, including 3.5 tonnes of buds
in vacuum-packed thermal bags. [@1] They described the haul as the biggest
quantity of marijuana ever seized in Spain. [@1] El País reported that the
search was carried out on 15 June and that officers found more than 100,000
plants on the 32-hectare estate. [@3] La Vanguardia reported the seizure of more
than 100,000 marijuana plants, about 2,000 of them large mother plants, and more
than 97,000 cuttings at different stages of growth. [@2] Within the plant
material, officers also seized 305 kilograms of marijuana pollen and 422
kilograms of marijuana. [@2] On the estate they had begun pressing hashish
tablets, and the search found 17 kilograms of that substance packed in 100-gram
tablets. [@2]

Signs suggesting a legal purpose were false, and police said the site was being
used for large-scale marijuana cultivation. [@1] The plantation had no
authorisation from the Spanish Agency of Medicines and Medical Devices to obtain
CBD. [@2] Thousands of plants were being grown in warehouses and greenhouses,
monitored by more than 100 cameras. [@1] The site had been illegally tapping the
power grid and had been operational since at least 2018. [@1]
Electricity-company inspectors found two fraudulent connections, the largest
such fraud the company had detected on a marijuana plantation, exceeding two
million kilowatt-hours and one million euros in loss. [@2] Officers also seized
two air guns, a bladed weapon, two stun guns, pepper spray and 5,385 euros in
cash. [@2]
