Guam Gov. Lou Leon Guerrero announced plans to pardon people who were convicted
of petty crimes or misdemeanors related to marijuana while it was still illegal
on the island. [@1,3,4,5] She said in a video message that she was seriously looking
at pardoning people convicted of misdemeanors or single crimes related to
marijuana, and was discussing it with key stakeholders. [@1,5] She said several
people who had made the mistake while marijuana was illegal were now having
difficulty getting back to work. [@1,5] Leon Guerrero said she was concerned about
barriers to gainful employment and that her administration believed in second
chances. [@2]

Guam legalized marijuana in 2019, and people at least 21 years old may use and
possess up to an ounce of it. [@1,4,5] Sen. Clynt Ridgell, whose efforts led to the
legalization bill, praised the governor and said it was time to make right what
had been wrong in the legal realm for years. [@1,5] The governor said she would
issue an executive order. [@1,5] Paco-San Agustin also said the administration was working with the Judiciary on those procedures. [@4] In October 2022 the governor's director of communications, Krystal Paco-San Agustin, said the procedures for deciding who would be eligible for the local pardon program and how requests would be considered were still being developed. [@4] Ridgell's legislative committee scheduled a May
12 public hearing on the industry rules and regulations drafted by the Cannabis
Control Board. [@1,5]

The Cannabis Control Board had given lawmakers the final proposed industry rules
and regulations in late February, and lawmakers had until early June to change,
adopt or reject them. [@2]
