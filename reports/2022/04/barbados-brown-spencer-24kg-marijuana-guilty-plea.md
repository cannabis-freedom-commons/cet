Chief Magistrate Ian Weekes addressed American drug couriers Brittany Georgina
Brown, a 28-year-old medical assistant from Brooklyn, New York, and Samiyah
Nicole Spencer, a 20-year-old exotic dancer from Croton-on-Hudson, New York.
[@1] The two women, who imported marijuana into Barbados, were being held at
Dodds Prison awaiting sentence. [@1] They were separately charged and pleaded
guilty on 30 March to importation, possession, possession with intent to supply
and trafficking in marijuana. [@1]

Customs officials searching Brown's luggage after she arrived from New York
found 25 plastic wrappings of vegetable matter weighing 12.15 kg, with an
estimated street value of $97,200, but she denied knowledge of it and was
detained and handed to police. [@1] Spencer arrived the same day with 24
wrapped, sealed packages of vegetable matter weighing 11.80 kg and an estimated
street value of $94,400, and she also denied knowledge of the drugs. [@1]
Defence counsel submitted that the cannabis intended for the streets of Barbados
had been confiscated and no longer posed a threat. [@1]

Defence lawyer Shadia Simpson told the court her clients were couriers rather
than the masterminds of the enterprise. [@1] She said they had pleaded guilty,
cooperated with police, were contrite and remorseful and were not known to the
courts before. [@1] Simpson asked the court to impose fines of about $15,000 and
allow time to pay. [@1] Weekes said the quantity of the drugs was also a
concern. [@1] He adjourned the case until the following day for sentencing,
saying the court must not send a message that those who seek to do the devil's
work should be accommodated. [@1] The women were to reappear before the District
‘A’ Magistrates’ Court on Friday to be sentenced. [@1]
