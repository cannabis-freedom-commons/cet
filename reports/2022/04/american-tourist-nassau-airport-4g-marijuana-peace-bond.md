John Horouritz, 63, was arrested at Lynden Pindling International Airport on
April 1 after customs officers found him with four grams of marijuana as he was
preparing to leave The Bahamas. [@1] Police said that after the accused was
arrested, he admitted to the offence during an interview with police, explaining
the drugs were for his personal use. [@1]

During his hearing before Magistrate Samuel McKinney yesterday, the American
said the drugs in question were really for medical purposes. [@1] His attorney,
Ryszard Humes, also confirmed the claims, saying his client used the drugs
because he suffered from brain trauma. [@1] The attorney showed the court his
client’s medical marijuana card – which was on a cellular device. [@1] However,
after reviewing the card, Magistrate McKinney found that it had expired. [@1] In
view of this, Magistrate McKinney said he could not take the fact into
consideration when determining his sentencing. [@1]

In a plea in mitigation, the attorney noted that his American client was a
surgeon and played a very important role in society. [@1] Mr Humes said Horowitz
had no prior convictions in the jurisdiction of the Bahamas and was extremely
remorseful for his actions. [@1] Because of this, the attorney asked the court
not to impose a custodial sentence. [@1] As a result, the accused was bound to
keep the peace for one year. [@1] He was told that should he re-offend or commit
another crime while in the jurisdiction of the Bahamas, he would be fined $500
or have to serve one month behind bars. [@1]
