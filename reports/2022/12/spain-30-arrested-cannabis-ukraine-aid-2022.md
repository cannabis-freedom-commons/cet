Police in Spain arrested 30 people accused of smuggling cannabis disguised as
aid for Ukraine. [@1] Ukrainians, Spaniards, Germans and Moroccans were among
those held in the Andalusia region. [@1] The Guardia Civil said the group formed
a criminal organisation supplied by marijuana plantations across Andalusia, in
an operation named Marlin. [@2]

In raids in Malaga province and the cities of Granada, Cordoba and Seville,
police seized nearly €800,000, six guns and 2,500 cannabis plants. [@1] Officers
found two Ukrainian-registered vans in Fuengirola carrying 109 kilograms of
vacuum-packed marijuana hidden in cardboard boxes. [@2] Searches in Malaga
province seized 740,400 euros, 25,250 US dollars, 20 kilograms of cannabis buds,
1,000 plants and a handgun, along with bulletproof vests and GPS trackers. [@2]
A second phase targeted suppliers in Granada, Cordoba and Seville provinces.
[@2] Those searches seized 1,500 plants, 10 kilograms of packaged marijuana,
five guns and 15,000 euros in cash. [@2]

The Guardia Civil became suspicious after a group of Ukrainians on the Costa del
Sol collected cannabis and stored it in a flat in Mijas, near Malaga. [@1] The
drugs were packed in vacuum bags in cardboard boxes on vans registered in
Ukraine, which travelled "as a solidarity convoy so they could pass under the
radar of police and border controls". [@1] The drugs came from across the
southern region and travelled in convoys through several countries, police said.
[@1] Those arrested in the first phase were detained for drug trafficking,
membership of a criminal organisation, illegal possession of firearms and
electricity fraud. [@2] Two suspects tried to flee during the arrests and rammed
a police car, causing minor injuries to two officers. [@2]
