A shopkeeper in central Brussels sells cannabidiol, known as CBD. [@1] In
October 2018 police seized more than 6 kilograms of cannabis from his warehouse.
[@1] Passers-by had noticed a strong smell of cannabis coming from the shop
before the seizure. [@1] During the search officers seized thirteen bags holding
a total of 6.837 kilograms of cannabis. [@1] Officers took a sample from each
bag for analysis. [@1] The shopkeeper did not resist and asked to get his goods
back as quickly as possible. [@1]

Six months later he was told he could recover the seized goods, but at the
police station the CBD flowers had been badly stored. [@1] He refused to take
the cannabis back, saying it was unsellable. [@1] The prosecutor then ordered
the flowers destroyed, and the shopkeeper hired a lawyer to seek compensation
for his losses. [@1]

Late in 2021 the French-speaking Court of First Instance in Brussels ordered the
Belgian state to pay more than 90,000 euros in damages. [@1] The court found it
certain that police had erred by not storing the flowers correctly. [@1] For
each gram lost he would receive 12.50 euros. [@1] On top of the 85,462.50 euros,
he was awarded 5,000 euros in additional damages. [@1]
