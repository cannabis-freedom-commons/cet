Spain's Guardia Civil, working with France's anti-narcotics office OFAST and
Europol, broke up a ring of French nationals that bought hashish and marijuana
in Morocco and Andalusia and took it to France. [@3] The operation, code-named
Copa-Sucre, began in 2020 after the two forces detected the group's activity in
southern Spain. [@3] Investigators first spotted a helicopter entering the
Andalusian coast from Morocco without a flight plan. [@2] Police arrested 11
people, one of them held by the French police, and nine more were wanted in
France under European arrest warrants. [@3]

Officers seized more than 2.4 tonnes of hashish and 112 kilograms of marijuana,
the Guardia Civil said. [@1] A French-registered truck stopped at Narbonne was
carrying 419 kg of hashish among its legal load, and its driver was arrested.
[@3] Officers found another 327 kg of hashish in a car at Baza and 860 kg hidden
in a truck's legal load at Chauchina, both in Granada province. [@2] The 112 kg
of marijuana was found in a vehicle at Portbou in Girona province. [@3] At
Torremolinos officers seized a helicopter that had flown to Morocco without a
flight plan, together with 211 kg of hashish. [@3] In Marbella a car with false
plates was stopped after a chase in which the driver tried to run over an
officer; 329 kg of hashish was inside and another 266 kg was found in the house
it had left, where three people were arrested. [@2] Searches at seven addresses
in Cádiz, Málaga, Almería and Castellón turned up three more helicopters, four
firearms, ammunition, body armour and electronic equipment. [@3]

The Guardia Civil said the group used the proceeds to pay for transport and hid
their origin through shell companies. [@3] Spain's proximity to Morocco, a major
hashish producer, has made it a key entry point for drugs bound for Europe. [@1]
