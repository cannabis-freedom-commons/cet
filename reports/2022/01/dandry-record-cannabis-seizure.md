In January 2022, Strike Force Harthouse detectives executed a crime-scene
warrant on a rural property at Dandry in western New South Wales. [@1,2] Detectives executed the warrant at the property, about 35 kilometres north of Coonabarabran, at about 8am on 17 January 2022. [@2] Police
seized more than 19,000 cannabis plants, which they valued at about A$66.8
million, calling it the biggest cannabis bust on the New South Wales
record. [@1,2] Watson said the property had two large dams, commercial generators and earth-moving equipment, and needed workers seven days a week. [@2] Police said about 90,000 square metres of land had been illegally cleared for more than 20 greenhouses holding plants at various stages of maturity. [@2] Drug and Firearms Squad Commander Detective Superintendent John Watson said the plants weighed about 11.5 tonnes in total and were believed to be a national record. [@2] By then police had counted 19,082 plants, and the search was expected to continue over the coming days. [@2] Six people aged 23 to 42 were arrested and taken to Dubbo and
Coonabarabran police stations. [@1,2] Investigators were working with the Department of Home Affairs on the visa status of the six. [@2]

All six were charged with cultivating a prohibited plant, taking part in
cultivating large commercial quantities of cannabis, and participating in a
criminal group, and were refused bail to appear at Coonamble Local Court on 19
January 2022. [@1,2] State Crime Commander Assistant Commissioner Stuart Smith said the short cannabis crop cycle made cultivation a quick source of revenue for organised crime groups. [@2] Strike Force Harthouse was set up in 2019 to investigate
cannabis cultivation and trafficking across the state, and had charged 51
people and seized almost 70,000 plants worth more than A$217 million by the
time of the report. [@1]
