The criminal chamber of the Rabat court of appeal sentenced French-Algerian drug
boss Sofiane Hambli to 20 years firm imprisonment on the evening of Thursday 22
September 2022. [@2] The court convicted him notably for international drug
trafficking, money laundering and sequestration. [@3] Hambli, 46, is described
as a key figure in the 2015 importation of seven tonnes of cannabis into France
that led to the removal of former anti-drugs chief François Thierry, of whom he
was said to have been an informant. [@2]

He had been arrested on 22 October 2021 in Morocco after seven months on the
run. [@2] He was seized in a clinic in Tangier where he was being treated after
being attacked and seriously wounded with a bladed weapon in the street. [@2]
Since that arrest he has been held at Salé 2 prison near Rabat under high
security because of his dangerousness. [@2]

He had been held in detention in France in November 2020 then freed under
judicial supervision in March 2021 before receiving a new committal order from
the Paris appeal court investigating chamber. [@2] After failing to appear, he
was considered on the run and Interpol issued an international arrest warrant
against him through a red notice at the French authorities’ request. [@2] He is
suspected of receiving 2.5 million euros for the logistics of a planned
four-tonne cannabis import, accusations denied through his lawyer Hugues Vigier.
[@2] His temporary extradition requested by the French prosecution was refused
by the Moroccan authorities because of the proceedings before the Moroccan
courts. [@2] He remains wanted in France on further cannabis trafficking
charges. [@1]
