Luke Datson, of Sardis Close, Waunarlwydd, Swansea, was sentenced to nine months
in prison suspended for 12 months. [@1] He had pleaded guilty to two counts of
being knowingly concerned in the fraudulent evasion of the prohibition on
importing cannabis and to possession of cannabis with intent to supply. [@1] The
Court of Appeal heard his appeal against conviction on 17 March 2022. [@3] The
court allowed the appeal and quashed all three convictions. [@3] It found the
trial judge had wrongly ruled that Datson's belief that the goods were not
prohibited could not be argued as a defence. [@3] There was no application for a
retrial after the appeal succeeded. [@3]

Datson bought the cannabis from Green Brothers, a company in Luxembourg. [@3] He
sold it to customers through his website. [@1] The trade came to light in March
2019 when a parcel addressed to him opened at the UPS courier depot in Neath and
was found to contain 13 kg of cannabis leaf and bud. [@1] When police went to
his house in Swansea on 20 April 2019 they found CBD oil products and boxes
containing bags of cannabis. [@1] When questioned, Datson said he was only doing
what Holland & Barrett do and asked why the chain was not being prosecuted. [@2]
A second box of cannabis bound for him was intercepted at the UPS unit in
Swansea on 3 May 2019. [@3] The court was told the cannabis seized had a total
street value of more than £100,000. [@3]

Datson believed that because the material had a THC level below 0.2% he did not
need a licence to import it. [@1] At the close of the prosecution case the trial
judge ruled he had no defence in law, and Datson changed his pleas to guilty.
[@1] Judge Christopher Vosper QC said what Datson was selling seemed 'a
medicinal product rather than a drug of abuse' and that it would be
inappropriate to treat him like a cannabis dealer. [@1] The Court of Appeal held
that a genuine mistaken belief of law that goods were not subject to an import
prohibition can be relied on to show the prosecution has not proved an essential
ingredient of the offence. [@3]
