National Guard officers and two canine teams found about 100 small plastic jars
of tamarind candy mixed with apparent cannabis extract at a courier and parcel
company in Silao, Guanajuato. [@2] The officers came to the firm for preventive
checks, carried out with the company's authorisation. [@1] The parcels were set
out for two dogs trained to detect illegal substances, and both showed interest
in the same one, which officers separated from the rest. [@2]

The cardboard box held the chilli-flavoured tamarind sweet sold in Mexico as
Pelón Pelo Rico, mixed with cannabis. [@1] The parcel weighed about three
kilograms and was addressed to a home in the State of Mexico. [@1]

Pelón Pelo Rico is a chilli-flavoured tamarind pulp sweet known to Mexican
children, first launched by a Jalisco company in 1985. [@1] Cannabis has also
been sold in Mexico in other sweets and baked goods, including lollipops,
gummies, brownies, hot cakes and muffins, according to users familiar with the
products quoted by Infobae. [@1]
