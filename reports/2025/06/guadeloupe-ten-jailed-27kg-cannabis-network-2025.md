Ten people were placed in pre-trial detention in Guadeloupe as suspects in a
network that introduced at least 15 tonnes of drugs, essentially cocaine, over
two years via Venezuela and Dominica, the Fort-de-France prosecutor announced on
11 June 2025. [@1] The ten, described as principal members of the network, were
intercepted between 30 May and 2 June 2025, charged and placed in pre-trial
detention. [@2] Three people were caught in the act in July 2024 as they landed
778 kg of cocaine and 27 kg of cannabis on a beach at Capesterre-Belle-Eau. [@1]
The specialised interregional court, or JIRS, of Fort-de-France took the case
after that seizure. [@1]

Investigators traced large-scale drug shipments from Venezuela to Guadeloupe
beaches through transhipments managed by Dominican nationals at sea off
Dominica, according to the prosecutor's statement. [@1] The network was also
suspected of running a money-laundering circuit, with more than 450,000 euros
transferred by Western Union to Dominica and Venezuela over 17 months. [@1] The
May-to-June 2025 operation also seized a handgun, a double-barrelled shotgun,
ammunition, nearly 900 grams of cocaine, 20.8 kg of cannabis, vehicles, cash,
jewellery and luxury goods, according to the prosecutor's statement. [@2]
Guadeloupe La 1ère reported the same operation's haul as 880 grams of cocaine
and 840 grams of cannabis, plus six vehicles and luxury goods. [@3]
France-Antilles reported the July 2024 beach landing as 780 kg of cocaine and 26
kg of cannabis. [@4] The three people caught then, two Guadeloupeans and a
Dominican, were charged with organised drug offences and criminal association
and jailed. [@4]

The inquiry was conducted by the Guadeloupe gendarmerie research section under
an investigating judge of the JIRS. [@3] Overseas minister Manuel Valls said on
CNews that fighting drug trafficking was a government priority because the
violence it brought threatened Caribbean and Guyanese societies. [@1]
