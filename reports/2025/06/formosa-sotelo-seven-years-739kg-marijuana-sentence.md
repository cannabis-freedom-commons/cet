The Federal Oral Court of Formosa sentenced Federico Sotelo to seven years in
prison as a necessary participant in aggravated drug transport involving three
or more people, in two concurrent counts. [@1] The court found that Sotelo
planned and coordinated the transport of marijuana and cocaine from Paraguay to
Argentine provinces and Uruguay, hidden in double bottoms in high-end vehicles
with Argentine plates. [@1] Federal prosecutor Luis Benitez led the
investigation and represented the prosecution at the trial. [@1] Benitez had
sought 12 years in prison as organizer and financier plus criminal association,
and Sotelo was acquitted of the association charge. [@1]

Across the investigations into the organization, authorities seized 739.597
kilograms of marijuana and 97.115 kilograms of cocaine. [@1] Sotelo was arrested
on 3 October 2024 in the city of Formosa under a national and international
capture order after joint work by Argentine and Paraguayan investigators. [@1]
The case began with the detention of the Añazco brothers on 18 September 2022 at
Puente Libertad in El Colorado, Formosa, carrying marijuana hidden in a towed
Volkswagen Golf bound for Santa Fe. [@1] Investigators found that Sotelo had
hired the Añazco brothers to carry out that transport. [@1] Investigators also
linked Sotelo, then a fugitive, to a May 2022 marijuana transport in Santa Fe in
a Toyota Hilux with another man. [@1]

The trial began on 5 June and the verdict came the next day before judges Fabian
Gustavo Cardozo, Juan Manuel Iglesias and Enrique Jorge Bosch. [@1] Sotelo,
though from Formosa, lived in Encarnacion, Paraguay, from where he coordinated
drug shipments. [@1] In September 2024 the Argentine prosecution and Paraguay's
Public Ministry created a joint investigation team through the DIGCRI under
Diego Solernó. [@1] Ten other members had already been convicted in different
jurisdictions in Argentina and Uruguay. [@1] Two women, one Paraguayan and
Sotelo's partner, and a man remained detained in pending proceedings. [@1]
