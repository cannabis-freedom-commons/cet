Amilcar Rivera Paz and Juan Jose Garcia were charged by the Public Prosecutor
with trafficking of narcotics, psychotropic substances and other controlled
substances and with manufacture, possession and use of restricted firearms. [@1]
Their trial was due to start on Wednesday in Managua, but both men decided to
admit guilt in an effort to spend less time in prison. [@1] Juan Jose told the
court he wanted to admit the acts in order to qualify for the minimum penalty
and leave prison sooner. [@1] Amilcar said he knew what he had done and accepted
responsibility for all of the acts. [@1] The judge of the Seventh Criminal Trial
District Court in Managua questioned both men to confirm the decision was
voluntary, and they replied that nobody was forcing them and that they
understood what it entailed. [@1] After the legality review, both men were
declared guilty. [@1]

The penalty debate was held immediately afterwards. [@1] The prosecution
requested eight years in prison for Amilcar for drug trafficking plus six years
for manufacture, possession and use of restricted weapons and use of explosive
devices, plus 350 fine days for each offence. [@1] For Juan Jose Garcia Rivas,
the prosecutor requested eight years for drug trafficking and one year for
illegal carrying or possession of a firearm. [@1] The public defender
representing both men asked for the minimum penalty for each offence and for the
fine days to be annulled because both men have limited resources. [@1]

Both men belonged to the criminal gang known as Halcon and had been detained on
1 April following a long National Police investigation with a large quantity of
marijuana. [@1] The marijuana had been brought from the Caribbean region of
Nicaragua for distribution in different departments. [@1] Both men, who are not
from Managua, were awaiting the sentence they will serve in the prison system.
