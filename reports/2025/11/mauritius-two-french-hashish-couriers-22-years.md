Mauritius's Anti-Drug and Smuggling Unit arrested three residents of Goodlands
and two French couriers in July 2019 over hashish smuggled into the country.
[@2] The former constable Gary Daniel Bruno Gopaul was arrested with two
accomplices on Saturday 13 July as they came to collect the Rs 3.7 million of
hashish that two French mules had brought in. [@1] The pair arrested with him
were his neighbours Marie Nicole Pascale Cangy, 41, and Leeroy Harold. [@1] A
fourth suspect, Tian Telcide, who was carrying the policeman on his motorbike,
escaped. [@1]

An airport ADSU team and customs anti-narcotics officers stopped Diafra Traoré
as he left his plane after a profiling exercise. [@2] Traoré was taken to
Jawaharlal Nehru Hospital at Rose-Belle, where he passed 39 pellets of hashish
that the newspaper valued at Rs 1.4 million. [@1] The second courier, Youssouf
Cherif, handed officers 53 pellets at the hotel where they were waiting before
passing 25 more. [@1] Officers mounted a controlled delivery at a hotel in
Pointe-aux-Piments where the two couriers were to stay four days, and the three
Mauritian suspects were arrested there. [@2] The three alleged organisers and
Cherif were brought before the Bail and Remand Court. [@2]

On Friday 7 November 2025 the Assizes Court in Port-Louis sentenced Diafra
Trarore and Youssouf Cherif to 22 years' penal servitude each after they pleaded
guilty to hashish trafficking. [@3] Judge Mehdi Manrakhan said the importation
of the drug into Mauritius was carried out for profit. [@3] The court put
Trarore's 39 pellets at 405.09 g, worth Rs 1.2 million. [@3] Cherif's 78 pellets
weighed 781.49 g and were valued at Rs 2.3 million. [@3] Both men told
investigators they had carried the drugs for money. [@3] The two had arrived in
Mauritius from Paris on two different flights. [@3] The couriers admitted before
the magistrates that they had agreed to become mules out of greed. [@4]
