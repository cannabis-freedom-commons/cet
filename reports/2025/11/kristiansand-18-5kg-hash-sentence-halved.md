A 43-year-old German man arrived in Kristiansand just before midnight on 6
November 2025. [@1] He had driven from Hamburg and taken the ferry from
Hirtshals in Denmark, driving out of the ferry terminal in his sister's car.
[@1] Customs officers stopped him at the ferry terminal and decided to search
the car thoroughly after an initial conversation. [@1] He admitted the smuggling
on the same day he was caught. [@1] The district court sentenced him to four
years in prison, but the sentence was then halved. [@1] The reduction followed
an appeal hearing in Agder lagmannsrett, which concluded it could not be proved
the man knew he was carrying cocaine. [@1] He was credited with 374 days for
time spent in custody. [@1] An earlier account of the same case says a man in
his 40s was sentenced in Agder district court to four years for smuggling large
quantities of hashish and cocaine via the Kristiansand ferry terminal. [@2]

Hidden under the boot floor, beneath several Wunderbaum air fresheners bearing
the man's DNA, officers found vacuum packs containing 18.5 kg of hashish
alongside one kilo of cocaine. [@1] The drugs had been placed under the
luggage-compartment floor beneath the fresheners carrying his DNA. [@1] The
court of appeal found it could not be excluded that he believed the load
consisted only of hashish, noting among other things that no DNA from him was
found on the drug packages. [@1]

The man told the court that debt led him to take the job after borrowing 3,500
euros to establish himself in a new rented home. [@1] When he could not repay
the loan he was pressed to smuggle drugs to clear the debt and was also promised
an extra 1,000 euros, according to the judgment. [@1] He said two men visited
him at home, showed a firearm and threatened to shoot him in the foot and to
harm his partner and family. [@1] He had a few weeks earlier been forced to make
a trial trip to Norway to check how strict border controls were. [@1] When he
refused the job, his little finger was broken and he was threatened with a
pistol to the head to carry out the smuggling. [@1] He handed the car to the
organisers a day before the trip so the drugs were loaded without him being
present. [@1] His lawyer said he was very happy to be believed, having admitted
guilt for the hashish transport but maintaining he did not know other drugs were
in the car. [@1]
