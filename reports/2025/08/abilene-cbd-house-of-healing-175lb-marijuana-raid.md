Abilene police executed a search warrant at CBD House of Healing on Catclaw
Drive during an investigation into possession of tetrahydrocannabinol. [@1] The
warrant was obtained on 22 August and executed the following day, 23 August
2025. [@2] Owners Brittany and Brennon Manske said officers arrived at their
shop on Saturday 23 August. [@3] No arrests were made as the investigation
continued. [@1]

Police listed 17.5 pounds (7.94 kg) of marijuana. [@1] They listed 3,580
pre-rolled marijuana cigarettes. [@1] They listed 28 jars of marijuana shake.
[@1] The affidavit listed 191 THC vapes and 400 THC vape cartridges. [@3]
Officers seized 538 containers of THC gummies. [@1] They seized 147 containers
of wax or shatter. [@1] Various THC candy and edibles were also seized. [@1]
Officers also took $1,176.52 in US currency. [@1]

An undercover officer bought a vape at the shop in June and a Type Y
Typification test indicated THC. [@1] A state lab reported on 14 August that the
vape contained more than 1% Delta 9 THC, above the 0.3% limit for hemp under the
2018 Hemp Bill. [@1] In July police wrote to the shop saying it was violating
Texas Health and Safety Code Section 481.113 and city zoning rules by selling
what the warrant called THC products and drug paraphernalia. [@1] The shop's
attorney, Chaile Allen, said she was denied access to her client during the raid
and called it a violation of his constitutional rights. [@1] She resigned from
her appointed position as an associate judge of Abilene's Municipal Court over
the raid. [@1] The owners said the seized goods were legal hemp. [@3]
