Honduras's armed forces said they had found and secured a plantation of about
18,000 suspected marijuana bushes at Las Minas in Tocoa, Colón. [@2] Prensa
Latina reported from Tegucigalpa on 20 August 2025 that the armed forces had
seized the plantation that day. [@1] The military said tactical teams and army
special forces found the illicit crop in a hard-to-reach part of the Las Minas
community. [@1] Hondudiario said a tactical team of the Fifteenth Special Forces
Battalion and the Strategic Intelligence Directorate C-2 carried out the
operation together with the anti-drug directorate DLCN. [@2]

The plants stood between one and two metres tall and covered three manzanas,
which the military put at 2.1 hectares. [@1] The armed forces said that in 2025
up to that point they had seized 19 marijuana plantations with more than 200,000
bushes across 46 manzanas, or more than 32 hectares. [@1] Those cumulative
seizures were made in the northern departments of Colón, Atlántida and Yoro and
in Olancho in the east. [@1] Hondudiario described the bushes as suspected
marijuana in the mountainous Las Minas area. [@2]

The military said the result reaffirmed its commitment to national security,
sovereignty and the fight against drug-trafficking structures. [@1] Despite the
reinforced anti-drug campaign, media reports describe Honduras as a key corridor
for drugs moving from South America to the United States. [@1] The most used
routes include the Honduran Caribbean and La Mosquitia, where difficult terrain
facilitates the passage of illegal shipments. [@1]
