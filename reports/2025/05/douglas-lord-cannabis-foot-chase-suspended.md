Luke Lord, 20, of Brookfield Crescent in Ramsey, was given a 14-month prison
sentence suspended for two years and a two-year supervision order at Douglas
Courthouse. [@1] He had pleaded guilty to possession of cannabis with intent to
supply, possession of cannabis, common assault and criminal damage. [@2] Isle of
Man Today reported that the term was made up of eight months for possession with
intent to supply, one week concurrent for possession, four months consecutive
for the assault and two months consecutive for criminal damage. [@2] He was also
ordered to pay £250 in costs. [@2]

Police on patrol saw Lord and another man acting suspiciously near Woodbourne
Square in Douglas at about 21:40 on 28 August 2024. [@1] Both ran off when
officers approached, but Lord was caught and told them: "Get the weed out of my
pocket already." [@1] Two green plastic wraps of cannabis were found in his
jacket pocket and he was arrested. [@1] Officers recovered the rucksack and a JD
Sports bag holding 176.7 grams of cannabis, which police valued at £3,526, plus
cannabis vapes worth £269. [@2] The BBC reported cannabis worth £2,250 in the
sports bag, £956 in the rucksack and £320 in a tin recovered on the route. [@1]
The court accepted that only the drugs in Lord's pocket and rucksack belonged to
him, with the larger amounts belonging to another person. [@1]

Prosecutors said that while on bail for the drugs offences Lord assaulted door
staff at the 1886 club in Regent Street in the early hours of 31 August 2024.
[@1] He punched one of them in the side of the head, causing the man to fall
down the steps in front of the building. [@1] After his arrest he damaged the
police cell he was held in; Isle of Man Today reported that he punched a wall,
cut his hand and smeared blood around the cell. [@2] His defence advocate said
Lord, who is autistic, had become friends with "unsuitable" people after moving
out of home for the first time. [@1] The court heard he had accumulated a drug
debt of £9,000. [@1]

Deemster Graeme Cook said the offending had breached the custody threshold but
there was cause to suspend the sentence. [@1] Isle of Man Today reported that
Cook suspended the term because of Lord's lack of previous convictions, his
background and his return to live with his mother. [@2] Cook warned that
supplying drugs would pass the custody threshold. [@2]
