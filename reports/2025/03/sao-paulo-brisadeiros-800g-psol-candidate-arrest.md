A former PSOL candidate for São Paulo city council was arrested in the city
centre for selling brigadeiros made with cannabis, known in Brazil as
"brisadeiros", and indicted for drug trafficking. [@1] She was arrested in
flagrante on Sunday 2 March 2025 by military police patrolling the area. [@3]
Military police on patrol caught her selling the sweets on Avenida São João,
near Praça da República. [@1] According to the police report obtained by g1,
officers searched her after she intervened to help a street vendor with a cart
enter a Carnival block, found the brigadeiros in her bag and took her to the 2nd
Police District in Bom Retiro. [@2]

The São Paulo state public security secretariat said Brunella Hilton was
carrying 800 grams of portions of brigadeiro made with cannabis. [@1] The public
prosecutor's office said police had approached her with about 100 portions. [@2]
Her lawyer, Roberto Guastelli, argued that the case could not be treated as
trafficking because she had no cash that would indicate sales. [@2] The defence
said the forensic report was provisional and did not specify how much of the
substance each sweet contained. [@2] In the release request the defence said she
carried 800 grams of sweets and a bag with 10 grams of cannabis. [@4]

Her family reported her missing to police on 15 March, saying they had not heard
from her for more than 15 days. [@1] A custody hearing the day after the arrest
converted the detention into pre-trial detention. [@5] She was held at the
Pinheiros II provisional detention centre in the west of the city. [@1] Her
lawyers said she was held in a cell for trans people in the men's wing of the
Pinheiros II centre and that her social name was not respected. [@4] The prison
administration said she was in a cell reserved for trans people and that her
registration used her social name and gender identity. [@2] She had been part of
the collective PSOL candidacy Bancada dos LGBTQIA+, which won 1,113 votes in the
2024 municipal election. [@1]

The São Paulo court accepted the prosecutor's charge against her, making her a
defendant. [@2] The court also issued a release order, and she answers the case
at liberty. [@2] The release order was issued on 20 March 2025. [@5] The judge
of the 3rd Criminal Court cited her lack of previous convictions and the fact
that the charge concerned only cannabis, not more serious drugs, in not very
large quantities. [@5]
