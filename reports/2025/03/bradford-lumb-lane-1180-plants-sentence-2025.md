Giap Chi Le and An Van Tran, both 38, were each sentenced to 20 months in prison
at Bradford Crown Court on 28 March 2025 for producing cannabis at a house on
Lumb Lane in Bradford. [@1] Both were construction workers who had been doing
jobs around the country before two Chinese men offered them work in Bradford.
[@1] The pair were found locked inside the house looking after the cannabis
farm, which had hundreds of plants in various stages of growth. [@3] Bradford
Crown Court heard how the two were found looking "scared and shaken" after
officers forced entry to the locked site on 22 January this year. [@1]

A total of 1,180 plants were growing in several rooms, and the electricity
supply to the house had been bypassed. [@1] Prosecutor Mollie Briggs told the
court the crop could have been worth about £434,000 if sold in bulk. [@1] Sold
as street deals, the estimated 124 kg yield was put at about £1,239,000. [@1]
Recorder Abdul Iqbal said the bypassed electricity supply also put people in
neighbouring premises at risk. [@2]

Le and Tran told officers they had been locked inside and forced to look after
the plants. [@1] Their barristers conceded they had no defence to the charge of
producing cannabis but said the pair had been exploited and pressured into it.
[@1] Both had pleaded guilty at their first hearing before the magistrates'
court. [@2] Barrister Howard Shaw, for Le, said the operation was organised by
far more sophisticated criminals and that his client had been ripe for
exploitation. [@2]

Sentencing them, Recorder Abdul Iqbal said each man played a lesser role in what
was a very large operation. [@1] The judge added that the operation would, on
one view, be capable of producing industrial quantities of cannabis. [@1] Their
illegal entry status means both face deportation after serving their sentences.
[@2]
