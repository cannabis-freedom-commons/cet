Sheriff's office officials worked with the Bureau of Land Management to clear an
illegal marijuana grow site on September 26. [@1] The Calaveras County Sheriff's
Office Marijuana Enforcement Team identified a large-scale outdoor grow of
multiple marijuana fields hidden in remote terrain. [@2] No arrests were made
and no suspects were located during the operation. [@1]

More than 1,500 marijuana plants across multiple fields were located on a remote
hillside on public land near New Hogan Lake, the Calaveras County Sheriff's
Office said. [@1] Law enforcement eradicated 1,501 marijuana plants from the
hillside grow. [@2] Based on an estimated yield of over 1,500 pounds, officials
said the grow would have produced nearly $472,815 worth of marijuana. [@2]

The illegal grow was on land managed by the Bureau of Land Management. [@1] The
suspects operating the site had cleared native foliage to cultivate the
marijuana plants among the remaining greenery. [@1] Investigators documented
environmental damage associated with the cultivation. [@2] Bureau of Land
Management personnel remain active in a detailed investigation into those
environmental violations. [@2]
