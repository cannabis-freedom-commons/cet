KBI agents with support from local law enforcement executed search warrants at
18 vape shops and CBD dispensaries in Wichita, Topeka, Salina, Great Bend,
McPherson, Independence, Pratt, Abilene, Ulysses, Concordia and Sedan between
October 1 and 2, 2025. [@2] Two people were arrested in Ulysses on October 2 for
moving illegal THC products to evade law enforcement detection. [@2] Gregory
Reese, 63, of Ulysses, was arrested around 12:15 p.m. at North Stubbs Road and
East Road 9 in rural Grant County on charges including possession with intent to
distribute, unlawful possession of a controlled substance, possession of
paraphernalia, interference with law enforcement and having no Kansas drug tax
stamp. [@2] Tammy Reese, 59, of Ulysses, was arrested around 12:30 p.m. at
Reese’s Enterprises at 119 West Oklahoma in Ulysses on charges including
distribution of a controlled substance, possession of paraphernalia, unlawful
possession of a controlled substance, interference with law enforcement,
criminal use of a weapon and having no Kansas drug tax stamp. [@2]

The KBI said it seized 5,494 ounces or 343.38 pounds, about 155.75 kg, of flower
described as marijuana bud. [@2] Agents also seized 43,867 THC pre-rolls
described as pre-rolled joints. [@2] Agents also seized 35,627 THC vapes. [@2]
Agents also seized 84,481 packages of THC and psilocybin edibles. [@2] Agents
seized $35,436 in currency. [@3] Around 5 p.m. on October 2 the Kansas Highway
Patrol stopped a 20-foot box truck on U.S. Highway 400 near Fredonia that was
transporting THC products removed from the shelves of 19 Kansas shops, and KBI
agents seized the truck and its contents. [@2]

The operation began as raids targeting Kansas stores alleged to be selling
illegal marijuana and THC products that stretched into a second day on October
2. [@1] An early account said the KBI operation under Attorney General Kris
Kobach and KBI director Tony Mattivi had targeted ten stores on October 1 and
another five on October 2. [@1] The KBI described the seized marijuana products
as prohibited by Kansas law or suspected to contain unlawful amounts of THC.
[@2] Evidence from the cases was being tested and the investigations were
ongoing. [@2] The KBI said it would submit case findings to the prosecutor of
jurisdiction for charging decisions, with possible charges including unlawful
distribution of controlled substances and possession with intent to sell. [@2]
