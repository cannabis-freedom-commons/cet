A St John Ambulance Brigade volunteer and a Port police officer were arrested in
two cannabis interdictions in Saint Lucia on 16 July 2025. [@2] The volunteer
was detained at the George F. L. Charles Airport cargo shed while trying to
clear packages of suspected cannabis and cannabis-derived products. [@1] Later
that day Port police officer Yannick Leon was arrested at Shed 6 in Port
Castries as he tried to collect a barrel of cannabis resin hidden in sealed
metal cans. [@1] Leon, who worked for the Saint Lucia Air and Seaports
Authority, presented a fraudulent letter of authorisation to clear the
consignment for its consignee. [@3]

Officers found 19 sealed metal cans in the barrel and seized 266 packs of
cannabis resin weighing about 26.8 kilogrammes. [@4] Police put the street value
of the port consignment at about EC$1.34 million. [@3] Inspector Shervon
Matthieu said the combined street value of the drugs seized in both
interdictions was more than EC$1.5 million. [@1]

Leon was arrested at the scene and later released pending further investigation.
[@3] He was re-arrested on 27 October 2025 and formally charged two days later.
[@3] The charges were uttering a false document, forgery, fraudulent evasion,
importation of a controlled drug and possession of a controlled drug with intent
to supply. [@4] He appeared before the First District Court and was remanded in
custody until 9 December 2025. [@4]

At the time of the arrests police said both suspects remained in custody as
investigations continued. [@1] Police called for tighter screening of port
workers and closer scrutiny of shipments linked to NGOs and charitable
organisations, citing the concealment methods used. [@1] The charges came amid
heightened scrutiny of Saint Lucia's ports after several SLASPA and Customs
employees were arrested in recent weeks over similar offences. [@3]
