Four United States citizens travelling aboard a Carnival cruise ship were
arrested in Bermuda for alleged drug possession. [@1,4,5,8] The ship's security staff
intervened after detecting suspicious behaviour among the passengers and held
them. [@1,4] Bermuda Customs routinely boards visiting cruise ships with drug-sniffing dogs and arrests anyone found with illegal drugs in their cabin. [@8] Carnival Cruise Line said it follows a zero-tolerance drug policy and strictly complies with all applicable laws and regulations. [@8] The travellers, who were not named, remained in police custody with
charges expected to follow. [@2,8]

Police said the guests were detained for holding a significant quantity of
illegal drugs and paraphernalia believed intended for distribution on the
island. [@2,8] The alleged items included multiple compressed cannabis packages,
vape pens and suspected THC gummies, alongside a package of carfentanil. [@2,4,6,8]
Police described the synthetic opioid carfentanil as about 100 times more potent
than fentanyl and used to tranquilize elephants. [@3,4]

The four faced controlled-substance possession allegations and could face severe
penalties under Bermuda law. [@1] Acting Detective Superintendent Derricka Burns
said the seizure may have prevented a potential tragedy, noting that even tiny
amounts of carfentanil can be deadly. [@1,4,8] The investigation continued while
authorities determined the legal fate of the accused. [@1] The Crown said it could not establish that Williams had agreed with a male alleged co-conspirator, whose case was still pending, to import the carfentanyl. [@7] The prosecutor said the cannabis had a street value of $6,000 to $22,700 and the THC powder a value of $4,000. [@7] She told police she had invited her two sisters and her daughter on the cruise. [@7] Williams was arrested with three family members when the Carnival Venezia docked in Bermuda in July. [@7] At 9.30pm on July 18, while the vessel was in international waters, the ship’s security officers were on patrol with a drug-detecting canine. [@7] Williams and her family left New York aboard the Venezia on July 17, and the ship docked at King’s Wharf on July 19. [@7] The same pouch held just over 37 grams of carfentanyl, which is not counted in the cannabis weight. [@7] A locked pouch among her effects also contained 227 grams of cannabis and 20 grams of powder found to be greater than 1 per cent delta-9 THC. [@7] She was sentenced to 21 months’ imprisonment on both counts, to be served concurrently, with time in custody taken into account. [@7] Those charges did not include conspiracy to import carfentanyl. [@7] Williams admitted two counts of conspiracy to import a controlled drug. [@7] Neither entered a plea as their matter is indictable. [@6] Tracy Ann Williams, 45, and Sabrina Lewis, 49, both from Michigan, were accused of importing THC between an unknown date and July 17. [@6] Magistrate Craig Attridge remanded both in custody until the September arraignments session. [@6] Williams was also charged with importing cannabis and carfentanyl. [@6] Tracy Ann Williams, 45, and Sabrina Lewis, 49, both from Michigan, appeared in the magistrate’s court charged with importing THC, and neither entered a plea. [@5]
