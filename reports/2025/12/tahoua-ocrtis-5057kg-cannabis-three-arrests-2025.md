Officers of the Tahoua regional branch of Niger's OCRTIS anti-drug office seized
57 bricks of cannabis weighing 50.57 kg in the city on 2 December 2025. [@1]
Investigators acting on an anonymous tip arrested a first suspect, a Nigerien
national, in the Koweit district with 20 of the bricks. [@1] A search in the
Bilbis district found the other 37 bricks hidden in piles of millet stalks. [@1]
Two more Nigerien suspects were arrested on 2 and 3 December, and officers
seized a motorcycle and two mobile phones. [@1] The cannabis and the suspects
were presented to the governor of Tahoua region, Colonel-Major Souleymane Amadou
Moussa, at the regional police headquarters on 4 December 2025. [@1]

The deputy head of the Tahoua OCRTIS branch, police officer Djafarou Idi, said
the operation began with an anonymous tip that a large quantity of drugs had
been brought into the city. [@1] According to OCRTIS, accomplices in three
neighbouring countries truck large quantities of cannabis to Konni, with part
sold locally and the rest sent towards the Maghreb. [@1] Les Echos du Niger
reported that the deputy branch chief described the cannabis being brought from
Konni into Tahoua by motorcycle. [@2]

Governor Moussa called the seizure a feat and said it was only the latest in a
series of successful operations. [@1] He urged residents to redouble their
vigilance to cut off the supply routes of terrorist groups active in the region.
[@1] The Journal du Niger quoted him saying drug trafficking is a direct source
of financing for organised crime and the terrorist groups active in the region.
[@3] The deputy prosecutor at the Tahoua court of first instance praised the
professionalism of the OCRTIS teams and the security forces. [@1] Les Echos du
Niger reported two arrests, while the ActuNiger and Journal du Niger accounts
describe three suspects. [@2]
