Russian drug police working with the Belarusian customs service shut down a
transnational group moving large hashish consignments from EU states to Russia
via Belarus in an international controlled delivery. [@1] The group built
double-bottom caches for hashish slabs inside tool-storage boxes in a truck
semi-trailer. [@1] Belarusian customs officers found the concealed drugs, with a
total weight of 135 kg. [@1]

Five group members were detained in Belarus, including the truck drivers, their
accomplices and the head of the transport company that provided the vehicle.
[@1] A Belarusian citizen was detained in Russia on suspicion of coordinating
the smugglers and passing the goods to the final recipients. [@1] Two Russians
were detained in the Mozhaisky urban district of Moscow Oblast on suspicion that
they were to organise contactless sale of the seized consignment. [@1] Police
put the preliminary black-market value of the consignment at more than 400
million roubles. [@1]

Russian investigators opened criminal cases against those detained in Russia for
attempted especially large-scale drug sale and organised-group drug smuggling on
an especially large scale. [@1] Courts remanded all the defendants in custody,
while those detained in Belarus were prosecuted for drug smuggling. [@1] The
customs-service account of the same operation says officers of the FTS, FSB and
MVD acted jointly with Belarusian customs to stop the group. [@2] That account
says officers found a truck carrying more than 135 kg of hashish and detained
eight group members, citizens of Russia and Belarus. [@2] It says the detained
members ranged from rank-and-file perpetrators to accomplices and recipients,
and that the smuggling and attempted-sale charges carry up to life imprisonment.
[@2]
