On 10 December 2025 the court dispatched a second-instance judgment rejecting as
unfounded the appeals of the Prosecutor's Office of Bosnia and Herzegovina and
of the defence, and confirming the 10 July 2025 verdict in full. [@3] The
confirmed first-instance judgment is numbered S1 2 K 047599 24 K and is dated 10
July 2025. [@3]

Balkan Insight described him as a 33-year-old from Sarajevo. [@1] In late
November 2020 he drove a van with Dutch licence plates to Novi Pazar in
southwestern Serbia and picked up 100 kilograms of marijuana for delivery to
Germany via Bosnia and Herzegovina and Croatia. [@1] The court's summary of the
indictment says that from 20 to 27 November 2020 he and others illegally
transferred about 100 kilograms of marijuana-skunk from Bosnia and Herzegovina
to Germany. [@2] The same indictment summary says that from 29 November to 12
December 2020 they illegally transferred 109.072 kilograms of marijuana-skunk
from Serbia into Bosnia and Herzegovina and then tried to take it on to Germany
and other EU countries. [@2] The group was organised by a person identified only
as S. B. and used the encrypted apps Sky ECC and ANOM to communicate. [@2]

The group was formed to traffic Cannabis sativa L, described as marijuana-skunk.
[@4] Between 20 November and 12 December 2020 members moved the drug from Serbia
into Bosnia and Herzegovina and sold it onward, especially to Croatia and
Germany. [@4] They communicated on mobile phones fitted with the encrypted Sky
ECC app. [@4] The Court of Bosnia and Herzegovina said this was the first case
in which it decided, on the merits, whether evidence taken from encrypted
communication apps, specifically Sky ECC, was lawful and admissible. [@2] Balkan
Insight said the July conviction was the first by a Bosnian court based largely
on intercepted Sky ECC messages in a drug-smuggling case. [@1]
