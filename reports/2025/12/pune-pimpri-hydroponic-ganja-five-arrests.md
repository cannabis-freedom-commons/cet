Pune police arrested five persons from different cities over the past month in a
drug-peddling case. [@1] The five were identified as Swaraj Anant Bhosale,
Tushar Chetan Verma, Sumit Santosh Dedwal, Akshay Sukhlal Meher and Malay Rajesh
Deliwala, aged 21 to 28. [@2,4] Verma was arrested first in Khadki during routine
patrolling. [@3] Verma was arrested for possession of hydroponic marijuana on 28 November 2025. [@5] A case against him was registered under the Narcotic Drugs and
Psychotropic Substances Act. [@3] All the accused were held in police custody
under the NDPS Act. [@2]

Police seized 1.194 kg of ganja and an electronic weighing scale from Verma.
[@3] The accused had rented a flat in a densely populated area of
Pimpri-Chinchwad where they had set up a soil-based hydroponic ganja cultivation
unit. [@2,4] Police dismantled the illegal setup and seized live plants, ganja
stock and cultivation equipment from the premises. [@3] Police said nine
different types of narcotics seized included ganja and hydroponic OG Kush,
charas, cannabis gummies and CBD oil alongside MD, mushrooms, LSD papers and
pills. [@2] Police put narcotics and related material seized in the case at
approximately Rs 3.45 crore. [@2] Police also froze Rs 7.80 lakh in bank
accounts, crypto wallets and foreign currency linked to the accused. [@2] Police sought the Financial Intelligence Unit of India's help to establish how the money was converted into digital currencies and which bank accounts were used, and said they would try to freeze the wallets. [@6]

The investigation uncovered links to an international drug-peddling network in
Thailand and Bhutan using the dark web and hawala payments in cryptocurrency.
[@1] The drugs reached Bhutan from Thailand, and police had also identified a Thailand-based man, he said. [@6] He said two Bhutan-based persons had been identified because they couriered drugs to Pune inside LED light strips, and police seized the courier packets and recorded the senders' address. [@6] Munde said police would also seek help from law enforcement agencies in Bhutan and Thailand to trace two suspects based in those countries, from where the raw material for producing the drugs was supplied. [@6] Deputy commissioner of police Somay Munde said the wallets were operating on the dark web and from Scandinavian countries. [@6] Police identified 25 crypto wallets holding $5 lakh, which they estimated at Rs 4.5 crore, generated from the drug trade in Pune and other parts of Maharashtra. [@6] Dark web and cryptocurrency were used to bring OG-Kush ganja and other
substances into India, with drugs imported from Thailand and Bhutan to Assam and
then to Pune hidden in courier services. [@1] The operation, code-named
Operation Alakh Niranjan, was carried out under the supervision of senior Pune
police officers. [@3] Police said the prime accused, Tushar Verma, hid his identity on the dark web under the name Alakh Niranjan, and the operation was named after that alias. [@4] Further investigation was under way and police said more
arrests were likely. [@3]
