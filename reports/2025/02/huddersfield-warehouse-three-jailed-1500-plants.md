Three Vietnamese nationals were jailed at Leeds Crown Court on 27 February 2025
after admitting production of a Class B drug. [@2] Van Dinh was jailed for 22
months, Lau for 27 months and Nguyen for 32 months after they earlier admitted
production of a Class B drug. [@1] The three men were paid to work at the
factory, which was based at a warehouse in Huddersfield. [@1]

Police found more than 1,500 plants at the site when it was raided on 27 August
2024, which had a collective street value of nearly £1m. [@1] Eleven rooms in
the building were dedicated to cultivating the drug, the court heard. [@1] The
court was told the three men had a "lesser role" in keeping the factory running
and were working "on behalf of others". [@1] The court heard that a guard was
placed outside the premises while the trio worked, although the operation did
not amount to "modern slavery". [@1]

Mitigating for Lau, John Batchelor said the 34-year-old had a live asylum
application which would be affected by his conviction. [@1] Representing
54-year-old Nguyen, Eleanor Mitten said the father-of-two had moved to the UK to
pay off a debt he had accrued in Vietnam. [@1] Nguyen had three previous
convictions for drug-related offences. [@1] Van Dinh, 49, was said to have come
to the UK from south-east Asia, although his personal circumstances were not
explained. [@1] All three men, who had no fixed abode, appeared for the
sentencing hearing via video-link from custody. [@1] Judge Christopher Batty
said each of the three played an "important role" in a large-scale cannabis
production operation. [@1] Judge Batty said it was the largest such operation he
had seen in 16 years on the bench and that the men acted under those making the
money. [@1] The court was told the Home Office did not intend to deport any of
the defendants. [@1]
