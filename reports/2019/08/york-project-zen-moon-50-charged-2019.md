York Regional Police charged 50 people in two separate investigations, Project
Zen and Project Moon. [@1] Supt. Mike Slack opened a news conference at York
Regional Police headquarters in Aurora on 8 August 2019. [@1] Project Moon was a
year-long operation against a synthetic drug network funded through the legal
medical marijuana programme. [@3] It was run with Toronto Police and the Ontario
Provincial Police against a network tied to street gangs. [@2] Project Moon led
to charges against 42 people, who faced 118 criminal charges in total. [@3]
Project Zen began in early 2019, worked with the Canada Border Services Agency
and targeted a drug ring running out of a house in Vaughan. [@2] Project Zen led
to charges against eight people. [@2]

Police said the Project Moon network was funded mainly through the illicit
cultivation and sale of cannabis. [@3] Police said the group fraudulently
obtained Health Canada medical cannabis licences, illegally leased licences and
pooled licences. [@3] Project Moon searches across southern Ontario seized more
than 20,000 illegally grown cannabis plants and more than 560 kilograms of dried
cannabis. [@2] Seven thousand plants were seized from one farm in Leamington.
[@3] Police said the group turned farms in rural southern Ontario into cannabis
factories producing marijuana and hash for the black market. [@3] Police said
there was evidence that some of the marijuana grown was destined for the United
States. [@3] Project Zen searches seized more than 56 kilograms of cannabis,
along with 19 kilograms of cocaine and five semi-automatic handguns. [@3]
Project Moon also yielded 23 kilograms of methamphetamine, 15,300 MDMA pills,
four firearms and $220,000 in currency. [@2]

At the Aurora news conference police displayed the drugs, cash and firearms
seized in the two projects. [@4]
