Sean Cody Charfauros Ada, 23, pleaded guilty in the Superior Court of Guam on 9
August 2019 to criminal facilitation to importation of a Schedule I controlled
substance as a third-degree felony. [@1] He had been charged with conspiracy to
import a Schedule I controlled substance as a first-degree felony, and that
charge was dismissed under the plea deal. [@1] He faces up to three years in
prison under the agreement but is not expected to serve jail time, and he agreed
to cooperate with prosecutors and testify against co-defendant Robin Young. [@1]
Judge Anita Sukola entered the plea as a two-year deferred plea under which the
case would be dismissed if Ada meets its conditions, with a five-year probation
period. [@1] Both defendants were scheduled to return to court on 11 September
2019. [@1]

Ada and Young were arrested in August 2018, before Guam's recreational marijuana
law took effect, after a K-9 alerted its handler to a package sent by priority
mail. [@1] Sean C. Ada, then 22, and Robin A. Young, then 32, were charged in a Superior Court of Guam magistrate's complaint with first-degree felony counts of importing and conspiracy to import a Schedule I controlled substance. [@2] The package held 321 grams of a green waxy-like substance that tested
positive for THC, the active ingredient in marijuana, and had been fitted with
an electronic tracking device. [@1,2] Ada had the package when Guam Police
Department officers approached him, though it was addressed to Young, who told
investigators he had let Ada use his mailbox. [@1] Ada told investigators he
bought the marijuana products from a website that accepted Bitcoin. [@1,2]

Guam's Cannabis Industry Act, signed in 2019, made recreational marijuana use
legal for adults 21 and older, but commercial sale awaited industry rules. [@1]
