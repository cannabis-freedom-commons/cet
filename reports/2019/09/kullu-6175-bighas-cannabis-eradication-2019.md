Police and anti-narcotics teams that had been camping in the forests of Kullu
district for over a month destroyed cannabis plants on 6,175 bighas (1,235
acres) of forest and private land. [@1] The area cleared was nearly five square
kilometres, about 500 hectares. [@1] The campaign began on 6 August 2019 and was
to continue until 4 October. [@1]

The teams included police, the Narcotics Control Bureau, the India Reserve
Battalion, a Central Narcotics Bureau, and revenue and forest department staff,
alongside mahila mandals (women's groups) and yuvak mandals (youth groups). [@1]
They destroyed both cultivated and naturally growing cannabis, and also poppy
plants used to make opium. [@1]

Police booked eight people for cultivating cannabis on their private land and
five for poppy cultivation. [@1] Officers warned residents to destroy illegal
crops on their land before police reached them. [@1] Kullu superintendent of
police Gaurav Singh said six additional NCB teams were destroying cannabis in
Manikaran, Malana, Pini, Fozal, Banjar and the Sainj valley. [@1] Police
department data put the 2019 area far above the 2,700 bighas destroyed in Kullu
in 2018. [@2] Thirteen cases were registered in Kullu district in 2019 against
cannabis and opium cultivators. [@2]

The charas season had begun, with fresh charas being prepared and stored. [@1]
Most hybrid cannabis is grown on forest land, with some on private land. [@1]
The district's hotspots include Malana, Manikaran, Kasol, Tosh, Nathan, Pulga,
Fozal and Banjar. [@1] Local growers extract charas and sell it cheaply to
networks that move it to other states, and the networks sometimes hire people to
grow and extract it on contract. [@1]
