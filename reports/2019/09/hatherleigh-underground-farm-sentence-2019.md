Daniel Palmer, 40, of Hooper Close, Hatherleigh, was jailed for six years and three months for building an underground cannabis farm beneath a barn on his father's land near Okehampton. [@1,2] He had worked with a professional grower to bury five interlinked shipping containers fitted with high-tech hydroponic rooms, lights, water supplies and fans powered by their own generator. [@1] Palmer ran a log-cabin business on the 200-acre Easter Hall Park estate at Petrockstowe, and hid the farm by building the barn over the containers with only a fertiliser-sack-covered floor hatch for access. [@1,2]

Police raided the barn at 7pm on 10 February 2017 and found the containers holding lights, fans, growing beds, watering systems and two water butts of freshly cropped cannabis, with more than a kilogram of skunk recovered. [@1] Police put the retail value of the skunk they seized at £384,000. [@2] Palmer denied production, claiming a man calling himself Richard Jones had rented the land with false identity documents, but his DNA was found inside the growing area including on a light fitting. [@1] Palmer had a previous conviction for growing cannabis on a different farm in 2001. [@2] A jury at Exeter Crown Court convicted him in August 2019, and Judge Paul Cook, sitting at Taunton, jailed him and set a timetable to seize his assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act. [@1,2] An order was made to destroy the drugs, and a proceeds of crime hearing was set for January 2020 to establish how much money the operation made and where it went. [@2] The judge said he could not conclude with certainty that there was nobody higher up the chain. [@2] In mitigation, defence barrister Nicolas Gerasimides said a man named Stephen Parker, who had previously served nine years for drug importation, was at the top of the tree, but police did not know where he was. [@2]
