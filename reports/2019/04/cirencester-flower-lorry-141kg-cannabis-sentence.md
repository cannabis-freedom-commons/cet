Mohammed Abdulrehman, 25, of Golbourne Avenue, Manchester, was sentenced at
Gloucester Crown Court on 5 April 2019. [@2] A man was jailed for his part in
trying to smuggle £1.4m worth of cannabis into the UK in the back of a Dutch
flower lorry. [@1] Abdulrehman was sentenced at Gloucester Crown Court to two
years in jail. [@1] He pleaded guilty at an earlier hearing to possession with
intent to supply cannabis and attempted possession with intent to supply
cannabis. [@1] Police observed cardboard boxes containing the drugs being
transferred from the lorry into Mohammed Abdulrehman's van. [@1] He was arrested
at Cirencester Services off the A417 in October. [@1]

A total of 141kg of herbal cannabis was seized by the South West Regional
Organised Crime Unit, which had received intelligence about the handover. [@1]
Detective Inspector Paul Catton said the drugs had been meticulously packaged in
vacuum sealed bags to disguise the smell, and stems of fresh flowers had been
placed on top of the bags, visible through the boxes, to make the packages fit
in with the lorry's load. [@1] The court heard Abdulrehman acted as a courier,
in order to make £700 towards paying off a business debt of £15,000. [@1]
Detective Superintendent Steve Bean of Gloucestershire Police said the cannabis
was imported from Europe, concealed within a large shipment of flowers and
destined for wider distribution across the country. [@2]

The lorry driver, John Lader, 51, of Voorschoten in the Netherlands, was given a
suspended sentence last month for his part in the operation. [@1] Lader denied
knowing the packages contained cannabis, saying he believed he was delivering
tobacco, and was given a 16-month suspended sentence. [@2] Both men were
arrested on 29 October 2018 at Cirencester Services. [@2] The seized fresh
flowers were handed out to various local organisations, including
Gloucestershire Rape and Sexual Abuse Centre, Gloucestershire Domestic Abuse
Support Services and a youth and community cafe. [@1]
