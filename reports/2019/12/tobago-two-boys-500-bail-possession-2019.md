Two Tobago boys who had been remanded in Trinidad since September on simple
marijuana possession charges were expected to be released in Tobago on $500 bail
on Friday. [@1] Justice Norton Jack chose not to discharge the boys but reduced
their bail to $500 each, with a parent or suitable person standing as surety so
they could be immediately released. [@1] Jack also ordered that the two be
immediately transported from Trinidad to Tobago as part of the bail reduction
order. [@1]

One boy was charged with possession of ten grammes and the other with 19
grammes. [@1] The Children’s Court had granted the two bail of $5,000 and $7,500
respectively, but their parents were unable to travel to Trinidad to sign the
bonds. [@1] The Registrar of the High Court identified the boys as eligible for
discharge. [@1] Petitions for discharge were made by lead Attorney General
attorney Fyard Hosein at the hearing of Attorney General Faris Al-Rawi’s Monday
application to speed pardons for those benefiting from the new law. [@1] In
granting reduced bail, the judge said the two can apply to have their matters
discharged when they return to court under the amended legislation. [@1] Hosein
and registrar’s attorney John Heath confirmed the lists for others on remand
should be ready by next Friday for the 6 January hearing. [@1]

The proceeding is part of determining who is entitled to have charges
discontinued under amendments to the Dangerous Drugs Act, which decriminalised
certain quantities of cannabis. [@1] The court process will seek to have records
erased and fingerprints removed from the national database under sections 5 and
5D of the amended act. [@1] The United States Embassy advised that adoption of
the 2019 Amendment Bill does not exempt persons with expunged convictions from
disclosing that information on visa applications. [@1]
