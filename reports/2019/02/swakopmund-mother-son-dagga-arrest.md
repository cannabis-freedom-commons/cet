Police arrested a 57-year-old Swakopmund mother and her adult son on Saturday 23
February 2019 after finding them in possession of dagga and cultivating dagga
plants at home to make medicinal cannabis oil. [@1] Law-enforcement officers
stopped and searched the son, who is in his 20s, and found eight grammes of
dagga with a street value of N$80 on him. [@1] The son then led the officers to
his home in Swakopmund's Kramersdorf suburb, where they found his mother and
questioned her. [@1]

The mother allegedly told the officers she was manufacturing cannabis oil for
medical purposes. [@1] At the home, officers discovered 71 dagga plants weighing
about 3.5 kg, plus 26 grammes of seeds and oil, all with a total reported value
of about N$22,000. [@1] The duo was charged with possession of and dealing in
cannabis, possessing dagga seed and cultivating dagga plants, and possession of
cannabis oil. [@1]

The two were kept in police custody at Swakopmund and were due to appear in the
town's magistrate's court on Monday 25 February 2019. [@1] Police deputy
commissioner Edwin Kanguatjivi confirmed the incident, in which the police drug
law-enforcement unit and the neighbourhood watch took part in the discovery and
seizure. [@1] Possession and growing of dagga is illegal in Namibia, although
advocates argue it should be legalised as in neighbouring South Africa, citing
its medical use, especially the oil among cancer patients, and potential
products, jobs and income. [@1] The government is not yet persuaded by the
legalisation argument. [@1]
