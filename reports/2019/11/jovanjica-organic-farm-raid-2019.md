Police stopped Predrag Koluvija for reckless driving on the Belgrade-Nis highway on 13 November 2019 and detained him over a suspected fake police ID; the same day officers raided his Jovanjica organic farm near Stara Pazova. [@2,3] The Interior Ministry said nine people were detained and almost four tons of marijuana seized in one of the country's biggest such swoops. [@1] Officers found 649.4 kg of dried marijuana and 65,581 raw stems, with a total weight of 3,954 kg, in tunnels and growing laboratories beneath the organic farm. [@1,2] They also seized two handguns, a falsified police ID card and a car fitted with police markings. [@1]

## Organised-crime charges and a state scandal

Koluvija and eight associates were charged with organising a criminal group and providing equipment for the lab, facing up to ten years in prison. [@2] Police and security officials were also arrested over links to the farm, including the head of security of a former police director and anti-corruption and criminal-police officers, causing a major scandal for the government. [@2] Prosecutors later split the case into Jovanjica 1, on the marijuana production itself, and Jovanjica 2, on alleged state-security links to the farm. [@3] No criminal proceedings were opened in North Macedonia against Koluvija or his associates. [@5] North Macedonia's government revoked THC Quality Group's licence on 16 December 2019, a month after Koluvija's arrest, on the basis of an inspection carried out on 7 November 2019. [@5] A health-ministry inspection could not establish how many plants were grown at the Rosoman facility or which cannabis strain was involved. [@5] The shipments were reported as industrial hemp and their THC levels were not measured during the transfer. [@4] Almost 60,000 cannabis seedlings weighing more than 4,700 kg were exported from Jovanjica to THC Quality Group during 2019. [@5] Koluvija was part-owner of THC Quality Group, based in the municipality of Rosoman, which held a licence to produce cannabis for medical use. [@4] North Macedonia's public prosecution told BIRN that there was no case in the prosecution over the events, and that it had delivered the data and evidence Serbia asked for. [@4] Serbian prosecutors sent a letter to their North Macedonian counterparts in February 2021 asking for help with the investigation into the Jovanjica operation. [@4]
