French customs and the French navy jointly seized seven tonnes of cannabis resin
on 23 May 2019 in a maritime operation off Algeria. [@1] The customs patrol boat
DFP3 Jean-François Deniau intercepted a speedboat carrying the drugs, supported
by the navy high-seas patrol boat Commandant Birot. [@1] An assault team of 12
soldiers and eight customs officers boarded from fast inflatable craft. [@2]
Three people were on board with Turkish passports and offered no resistance to
the boarding party. [@1] They were taken to the military port of Toulon and
placed in garde à vue, a form of police custody. [@1]

The resin was packed in 230 parcels with an estimated resale value of 52 million
euros. [@1] The fast boat, probably a former racing boat, had been detected by a
French customs Beechcraft KA350 aircraft. [@1] Two navy aircraft, a Falcon 50
and an Atlantique 2, provided high-altitude surveillance. [@1] It was
intercepted off Algeria carrying 14,000 litres of fuel, having sailed from
Morocco towards Libya. [@1] The interception took place about 100 km off the
Algerian coast. [@2]

The drugs were transferred to the Jean-François Deniau while the Commandant
Birot towed the speedboat. [@1] Both vessels made for the military port of
Toulon, docking on Saturday 25 May. [@3]

Nacer Lalam of France's national institute for higher security studies said
routes for cannabis resin ran from Morocco to the Middle East and the Arabian
peninsula. [@1] He said traffickers could exploit Libya's porous zones and local
networks to move their goods. [@1] He noted that Europe was becoming a producer
and consumer of cannabis herb, displacing resin. [@1] The defence and public
accounts ministers praised cooperation between the administrations at sea and
congratulated the customs officers and sailors involved. [@4]
