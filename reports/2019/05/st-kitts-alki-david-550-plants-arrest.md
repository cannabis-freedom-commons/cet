On 7 May 2019 a private jet carrying Alkiviades Alki David arrived at the Robert
Llewellyn Bradshaw International Airport in St Kitts. [@2,8] David was detained at
the airport until about 1:30 p.m. that day while police, customs and agriculture
officers investigated. [@2] The plant material he had brought was taken into
custody for analysis. [@2] On the advice of the Director of Public Prosecutions,
David was arrested on 9 May 2019. [@2,11] He was charged with possession of
cannabis, possession with intent to supply, importation of cannabis and
importation of a restricted item. [@2,5,7,8,9,10] He had landed with business partner Chase
Ergen and actor Jonathan Rhys Meyers. [@1,4,5,6,8,10] Ergen was subsequently released without charge. [@8] Meyers' wife and his mother-in-law were also reportedly on the plane. [@4,6,7] David said Lane and Meyers' mother-in-law were 'cavity searched' after landing in St Kitts, but the group were eventually allowed to leave. [@7] Meyers was released without
sustaining any charges. [@1] Police said David was arrested on May 9 and
appeared in court the following day. [@3,9]

On arrival David had declared 550 plants as hemp in 11 crates of 50 plants each.
[@2] Testing found the plants were of the cannabis genus under St Kitts and
Nevis drug law. [@2]

David said he had come to build relationships with farmers to grow hemp for his
Swissx business selling legal cannabis products abroad. [@3,4,7,10,11] Through his legal team he said he had cleared up the situation with the authorities in St Kitts-Nevis and looked forward to continuing plans to develop the medicinal cannabis industry on the island. [@4] Harris denied any conversation with foreigners about setting up cannabis businesses, saying it would be irresponsible while the bill was unfinished. [@7] Swissx said the pair had done nothing wrong and that talks with Prime Minister Timothy Harris broke down and led to the arrests of David and Ergen. [@7,9] A local lawyer said
the mere possession of the marijuana plant is illegal and THC content does not
matter. [@3] He was first released on $30,000 cash bail with $50,000 in
sureties, ordered to surrender travel documents and report daily to Frigate Bay
police. [@3,5,7,8,9,11] On 14 May the court varied bail to EC$300,000 cash with EC$300,000
in sureties and returned his travel documents. [@2,8] He was ordered to return to
court on 23 September 2019. [@2,4,7,8] He then posted $300,000 bail before leaving the
island on Tuesday. [@1,4,6]
