Three Southern California men were arrested on 7 March 2019 on federal charges
alleging they used millions of dollars wired from China to buy homes converted
into illegal marijuana grow operations. [@1] Lin "Aaron" Li, 37, of Chino, Ben
Chen, 42, of Alhambra, and Jimmy Yu, 44, of Pasadena, were each charged in Los
Angeles federal court with one count of manufacturing, distributing and
possessing with intent to distribute marijuana. [@1] Li, a real estate agent,
was alleged to have orchestrated the purchases, converted the houses into grow
operations and trafficked the marijuana, most of which was sold in California
and Nevada. [@1] Investigators said Li used Chen and Yu to run the grow houses
day to day, help distribute the marijuana out of state and return the sale
proceeds. [@2]

Searches of Li's home and seven grow houses in Chino, Ontario and Chino Hills
seized about 1,650 marijuana plants and at least $80,000 in cash, federal
prosecutors said. [@2] Federal authorities began proceedings to forfeit the
seven homes, cumulatively worth more than $5 million. [@2] The seven homes were
bought between July 2013 and September 2017 for a total of $4,067,882, according
to the affidavit. [@1]

Down payments for most of the grow houses were traced back to wire transfers
from China. [@1] Li also allegedly diverted electricity from power lines, hiding
the grows' high power use and creating fire risks in neighbourhoods. [@1] The
14-month investigation began with the San Bernardino County Sheriff's Department
and was later joined by federal Homeland Security Investigations. [@2] In June
2018 officers seized 1,038 marijuana plants at an eighth grow house in Chino
Hills believed part of the same scheme, a figure not included in the 1,650
total. [@2]
