Emma Appleby had a month's supply of medical cannabis oil, worth about £2,500,
confiscated at Gatwick airport after she bought it in the Netherlands for her
severely epileptic nine-year-old daughter Teagan. [@1] She was carrying the oil
even though she had a private prescription for the medicine. [@1] Labour MP
Tonia Antoniazzi, who has campaigned for greater access to medical cannabis,
accompanied Appleby on the trip to the Netherlands. [@1]

It was the second time medical cannabis for Teagan had been seized at an
airport. [@1] After Border Force officials seized the first batch, Appleby and
her partner Lee Moore obtained a prescription from a private consultant in the
United Kingdom and had that supply returned days later. [@1] She said she was
then told she needed an import licence she could not afford. [@1]

Teagan has a rare chromosomal disorder called Isodicentric 15 and Lennox-Gastaut
syndrome, a form of epilepsy that causes up to 300 seizures a day. [@1] A
government spokesman said it is unlawful to import unlicensed cannabis-based
products for medicinal use to the United Kingdom without a Home Office
importation licence. [@1] The spokesman said Border Force has a duty to enforce
the law and stop the unlawful import of controlled substances into the United
Kingdom. [@1]
