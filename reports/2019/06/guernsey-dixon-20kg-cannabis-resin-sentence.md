Darren Lee Dixon, a UK citizen resident in south-west Spain, was caught with
20kg of the class B drug in March and immediately admitted his guilt. [@1] He
was sentenced to seven years in prison for importing £500,000 worth of cannabis
to Guernsey. [@1] He was stopped by officers at the border post on 29 March as
he tried to drive off a Condor ferry in his Jaguar S-type. [@2] The Royal Court
case concerned 20 kg of cannabis resin brought into Guernsey from St Malo. [@3]
Judge Russell Finch said it was the largest case of its kind to come before
Guernsey's courts for some time. [@2]

The cannabis was found in 205 individual packages inside his car by officers
after Dixon boarded the Condor ferry from France. [@1] The Guernsey Border
Agency seized 19,781.4 grams of cannabis resin. [@2] Based on Guernsey's street
price of £20 to £30 a gram, the haul was worth between £395,000 and £593,000.
[@2]

Dixon had initially told officers he had nothing in the car and was visiting
Guernsey on holiday from his home in Spain. [@2] His advocate, Paul Lockwood,
said Dixon was involved in a drug debt and working for people he "would not want
to trifle with". [@2] The court accepted that but found he was clearly involved
with an organised enterprise and stood to profit. [@2] The seven-year term was
backdated to his arrest and remand in custody on 29 March. [@2] The drugs were
to be destroyed in line with the court order. [@3]
