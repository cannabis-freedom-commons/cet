Karl Anihia, president of the Tahiti Herb Culture association, was sentenced on
Monday to three months in prison, suspended, for planting a cannabis plant in
front of the French Polynesian assembly in Papeete. [@1] His lawyers immediately
announced that they would appeal the decision. [@1]

At a demonstration on 5 March he planted a two-metre paka plant — cannabis — in
front of the assembly of French Polynesia to demand legalisation of cannabis for
therapeutic use. [@1] The next day he was summoned to the Papeete public
security directorate and placed in police custody, then referred to the
prosecutor and brought before the judge of liberties and detention, who placed
him under judicial supervision. [@1] After requesting more time on 9 March to
prepare his defence, he appeared before the Papeete correctional court on Monday
to answer charges of unauthorised “transport and detention” of paka. [@1]

Tried under the immediate-appearance procedure, he received three months
suspended, whereas the prosecutor had requested six months suspended. [@1] His
lawyer, Me Millet, described the act as an “altruistic political gesture”. [@1]
The lawyer said he would appeal the principle of the conviction, invoking a
February Court of Cassation ruling in a Femen activist's case that such
prosecutions could disproportionately infringe freedom of expression. [@1]
