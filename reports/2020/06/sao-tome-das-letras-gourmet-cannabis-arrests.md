The Civil Police's Second DENARC precinct dismantled a group running a branded
'gourmet' trade in marijuana and skunk, including seed sales, in São Tomé das
Letras. [@1] The group sold marijuana seeds through Instagram as if it were an
online store. [@1,4] On Thursday 18 June 2020 officers arrested two 25-year-old
men in flagrante at the place used for planting and for drying and crystallising
the plant for sale. [@1,3,4] The two suspects lived in the rural locality known as Pontinha and sold the drugs to buyers in various regions of Brazil. [@4] Police found plantations equivalent to ten football fields in the suspects' houses. [@3] One suspect, a São Paulo native, had already been
sentenced to more than seven years for drug trafficking, while the Minas Gerais
accomplice had previously been arrested for cannabis cultivation and had a
criminal case under way. [@1,4] The lead investigator said one of the men had been imprisoned in São Paulo and was considered a sympathiser of the PCC prison gang. [@3] Investigators said the suspects had been active in the region for at least six months and both had prior records for cultivation and drug trafficking. [@3] Two other people who lived in another house on the
same lot were taken to the station to give statements while police examined
whether they were involved. [@1]

Police said the seized seeds could plant two football fields and the seized
plants could produce 300 to 4,000 kg of pressed marijuana. [@1] Inspector Vander Tavares said the plants were grown in a rotation scheme that guaranteed continuous production. [@4] Officers also seized a large quantity of packaging. [@4] The group grew
more than 20 different seed types to make a purer, elite drug aimed at tourists
from across Brazil. [@1] The drug sold for R$30 to R$100 per gram, averaging
R$30,000 to R$100,000 per kilo, with each planting seed averaging R$100. [@1] Investigators said the seeds came from Europe. [@3] Luiz Rossi said the seeds came mainly from Europe and were biologically altered, called feminized because of their greater reproductive potential. [@4] Investigator Luiz Rossi said the seeds were usually divided into packs of three and sent to the final customer through delivery companies. [@4]

The online advertisements included photos and a description of the quality of
the drugs. [@2,3] The lead investigator said sales were not seeds alone but
'gourmet' marijuana such as skunk with higher THC content. [@2,3,4] The suspects
grew the drugs in nurseries set up inside their houses and ran a souvenir stall
to identify interested tourists and offer them samples. [@2,3] Interested buyers could taste the product, which was separated by specific brand logos, before making a purchase. [@4] Police said the
suspects' tactic was to carry only a small quantity if approached so they could
claim to be users. [@2,3] The investigation lasted one month under delegate
Rodolpho Tadeu Machado, and police said inquiries continued to find other
participants and similar groups. [@2,3] The Três Corações police station was responsible for concluding the suspects' arrest report, while Denarc continued work to identify others involved in the scheme. [@4] Delegate Rodolpho Machado said the region's climate favoured the crop and São Tomé das Letras had drawn attention for marijuana tourism, a distinct trade in spices rather than the pressed, low-quality marijuana imported from Paraguay. [@4]
