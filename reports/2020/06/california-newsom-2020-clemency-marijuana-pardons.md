Gov. Gavin Newsom granted 13 pardons and commuted the sentences of 21 current
inmates on Friday 26 June 2020. [@1] Three of the 13 pardons were for marijuana
offences. [@1]

Carlos Castaneda was 21 when he was convicted of possessing marijuana for sale
in San Bernardino County in 1995. [@3] Tina Conklin, a Washington state
resident, was 23 when she was convicted in California in 1987 of possessing
marijuana for sale. [@1] Christopher Osborn was 21 when he was convicted in
Orange County in 2002 of selling marijuana. [@1]

The governor's office said a pardon does not expunge or erase a conviction. [@2]
It said a pardon can remove barriers to employment and public service and
prevent consequences such as deportation. [@2] Those who received commutations
must appear before the Board of Parole, which decides whether they can be safely
released. [@1]

Three of the pardons went to Cambodian refugees who had been deported or faced
deportation. [@3] Newsom's office said he had granted 41 pardons and 65
commutations while in office. [@2]
