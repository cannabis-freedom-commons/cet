Prosecutors in Bosnia and Herzegovina indicted seven men, Bosnian and Croatian
nationals, over trafficking marijuana from Montenegro through Bosnia and
Herzegovina to the European Union. [@1] The Prosecutor's Office called it the
most comprehensive indictment in the field of drug crime in post-Dayton Bosnia
and Herzegovina. [@2] The indictment was forwarded to the Court of Bosnia and
Herzegovina for confirmation. [@2] The indictment followed a months-long
investigation conducted with partner police and security institutions in Bosnia
and Herzegovina, countries in the region and the EU. [@3]

The prosecution charged the men, as an organised crime group, with smuggling the
marijuana, known as skunk. [@2] The drug was taken from Montenegro into Bosnia,
where it was stored or smuggled straight on to Croatia and other EU countries.
[@2] The prosecution put the amount at about 6.8 tonnes. [@1] It said the group
moved the drug on at least 25 occasions between 2016 and 2019. [@2] Shipments
were usually about 200 kilograms and reached 600 or 900 kilograms in some cases.
[@2] The drugs were hidden in vehicles hired from transport companies trading in
petroleum products or other goods. [@2] Police intercepted at least two tonnes
of the drug in Bosnia and Herzegovina, in the areas of East Sarajevo, Neum and
Gradiška, and some quantities in Croatia. [@2]

The prosecution estimated the group's illegal gain at a minimum of 2 million
euros. [@2] OCCRP put the value of the smuggled drugs at more than two million
euros, or about US$2.36 million. [@1] When the men were arrested, police
searched their homes and seized about half a million convertible marks in cash
and a quantity of counterfeit banknotes. [@2] OCCRP put the seized cash at some
US$301,900. [@1]

The prosecution said it would call about 20 witnesses, some of them protected,
and expert witnesses. [@2] It said it would present several hundred pieces of
evidence. [@2]
