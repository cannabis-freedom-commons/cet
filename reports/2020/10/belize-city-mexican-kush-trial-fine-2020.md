A joint team of police and Belize Defence Force soldiers acted on Special Branch
intelligence and searched a house near Driftwood Bay, at mile 4½ on the Philip
Goldson Highway in Belize City, on 16 July 2019. [@4] A police dog found a
hidden compartment under a van at the house. [@1] Officers seized 24.857
kilograms (about 58 pounds) of high-grade cannabis that police called Mexican
Kush. [@1] They also found $12,361.73 in cash at the house. [@1] Police arrested
and charged 34-year-old Lynroy Longsworth and 21-year-old Kenosha Beaton with
drug trafficking. [@1]

Channel 5 Belize reported the charge as possession of controlled drugs with
intent to supply. [@3] Both were arraigned before Magistrate Emerson Banner on
18 July 2019 and pleaded not guilty. [@2] The magistrate refused bail because of
the quantity of the drugs and remanded them to Belize Central Prison until 24
September 2019. [@2]

Both were later charged with two counts of drug trafficking over the 54 pounds
and a further 263 grams found in the search. [@4] At a full trial before
Magistrate Emmerson Banner, Beaton was found not guilty of both charges. [@4]
Longsworth was acquitted of the larger charge because police did not establish
that he owned the van in which the drugs were found. [@4] The prosecution proved
its case against him only for the 263 grams. [@4] He was fined $10,000 and told
to pay by 21 March 2021 or spend three years in jail in default. [@4] Longsworth
said he was repairing the van, which had Corozal plates, for someone else. [@4]
