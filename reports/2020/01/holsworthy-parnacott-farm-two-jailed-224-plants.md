Paul Hensher, 63, and Aleck Robbins, 57, admitted production of cannabis and
were both jailed for two years and four months by Judge Timothy Rose at Exeter
Crown Court. [@3] The operation was set up over two years at Hensher's Parnacott
Farm on the outskirts of Holsworthy, Devon. [@2] The men said they were getting
ready in the hope that medicinal cannabis would be legalised. [@1] They sold the
crop to friends for £170 an ounce, the court heard. [@1]

Police raided the farm on 30 October 2018. [@3] The men were caught with 224
plants in total. [@2] Officers seized 82 half-grown or mature plants and another
130 seedlings, and found 215 grams of recently cropped buds in a converted
drying room. [@2] The court was told the plants could have produced more than
£110,000 of drugs every couple of months if all had reached maturity. [@1]

They turned outbuildings into three growing rooms and a caravan into a nursery
for younger plants, and kept detailed notes on when each crop would be ready for
sale. [@1] Judge Timothy Rose described the set-up as 'sophisticated'. [@1] He
said the size and scale of the operation 'monumentally exceeded anything you
could possibly have needed for your own use or that of a moderately small local
group'. [@2] He called it 'a significant piece of criminality'. [@1] The judge
set a timetable for a financial investigation under the Proceeds of Crime Act
that could lead to the confiscation of assets derived from the growing. [@2]
