Gendarmes of Cameroon's North-West legion detained three people over the Sabga
cannabis seizure, the owner of the drug, the truck driver and his companion, and
placed them in preventive detention at the request of the public prosecutor.
[@2] The gendarmes had followed the suspect truck before stopping it and
arresting the suspects, their commander said. [@2] Colonel Boum Bissoue,
commander of the North-West gendarmerie legion, said the security forces would
give bandits and highway robbers no chance to exploit the socio-political
situation. [@2] The seizure was made public on Saturday 23 May 2020. [@1]

The gendarmes intercepted 45 sacks of cannabis in a goods truck at Sabga, in
Tubah subdivision. [@2] The state broadcaster CRTV put the load at 46 sacks of
Indian hemp estimated at 1,000 kg, brought from Nigeria. [@3] Koaci reported
that the defence forces seized a tonne of Indian hemp in the North-West region.
[@1] Koaci said the drug was hidden in a consignment of 46 sacks of goods from
neighbouring Nigeria. [@1] The sacks were hidden among sacks of potatoes, beans
and maize in the truck. [@3] The security forces also took a Toyota vehicle from
the traffickers and returned it to its owner. [@1]

Koaci reported that the seizure followed intelligence provided to the army. [@1]
The defence forces described the Nigeria-Cameroon border as a hub of the drug
trade. [@1] A national gendarmerie officer in the North-West told Koaci that
more than 20 tonnes of drugs from Nigeria had been seized in a year. [@1] The
officer said cannabis fields had been destroyed in the West and North-West
regions. [@1] StopBlaBlaCam described the West and North-West as internationally
recognised centres of cannabis cultivation, trafficking and heavy consumption.
[@2]
