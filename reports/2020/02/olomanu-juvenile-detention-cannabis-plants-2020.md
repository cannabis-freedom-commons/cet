A youth charged with escaping from Olomanu centre last December was blamed for
cultivating marijuana inside the juvenile detention centre. [@1] An escaped
detainee went straight to Faleolo Police post and reported that prison officers
were growing marijuana on the premises. [@1] Police Commissioner Fuiavailiili
Su'a Egon Keil said police charged the youth with escaping and investigated the
claim that marijuana was cultivated in the prison facility. [@1] The Samoa
Observer reported on 23 December 2019 that police were investigating after an
escaped detainee alerted them to marijuana inside the centre's main office. [@2]

Police said they went to the location and recovered several cannabis pot plants.
[@1] The commissioner said officers took the plants into their possession,
continued the investigation and found not enough evidence to charge anyone. [@1]
He said Prisons was conducting its own administrative investigation. [@1]
Assistant Commissioner of Prisons and Correctional Facilities Levaopolo Losa
Siaosi said only one bucket containing marijuana was found. [@1] She said the
bucket held marijuana about two or three inches tall. [@1] According to the
Assistant Commissioner, the cannabis was found at the plantations being worked
by the youth detainees. [@1]

Levaopolo disputed that the plants belonged to prison officers, saying the
escaped youth and the detainees owned the cannabis. [@1] No charges were laid in
relation to cultivating illegal drugs within the correctional facility. [@1] The
only case before the court at that time concerned the youth charged with
escaping. [@1]
