Crews of the Coast Guard cutters Legare and Winslow Griesser and the patrol ship
USS Shamal offloaded the drugs at Port Everglades on a Wednesday. [@2] The
offload took place on 5 August 2020 at Port Everglades, Florida. [@2] Coast
Guard crew members unloaded about 3,900 pounds of marijuana and about 1,100
pounds of cocaine at Port Everglades in Fort Lauderdale on Wednesday. [@3] The
Legare crew brought ashore nearly 5,000 pounds of interdicted contraband, which
the Coast Guard valued at an estimated $25.7 million. [@1] The Associated Press
described the same haul as almost $26 million. [@2]

The cannabis in the haul was about 3,900 pounds of marijuana, roughly 1,769
kilograms. [@1] The Winslow Griesser crew interdicted nearly 1,100 pounds of
cocaine, a separate substance excluded from the cannabis figures. [@1] The drugs
were taken from suspected drug-smuggling vessels while on patrol in the
Caribbean Sea, as part of a counternarcotics operation. [@2] The haul comprised
about 3,900 pounds of marijuana and about 1,100 pounds of cocaine from suspected
drug-smuggling vessels in the Caribbean Sea. [@4]

U.S. Southern Command began enhanced counter-narcotics operations in the Western
Hemisphere on 1 April, the Coast Guard said. [@1] Agencies from the Departments
of Defense, Justice and Homeland Security cooperated, including the Navy,
Customs and Border Protection, the FBI, the DEA and Immigration and Customs
Enforcement. [@1] The law-enforcement phase of Caribbean counter-smuggling
operations is led by the Coast Guard's 7th District in Miami, with boardings
conducted by Coast Guard members. [@1]
