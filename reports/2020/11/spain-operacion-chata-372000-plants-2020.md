Guardia Civil officers dismantled a network that grew cannabis on 52 plantations
in Madrid, Almería, Granada, Murcia, Ávila and Cáceres. [@3] They arrested three
people and placed two others under investigation. [@3] The investigation began
in May in Villa del Prado, a town in the Community of Madrid, after officers
learned that a company was seeking land there to grow cannabis it described as
medicinal. [@2]

During the raids police seized more than 372,000 plants, more than a third of
the number seized in Spain in a usual year. [@1] Officers seized more than
17,000 kilograms of marijuana in Madrid and Almería. [@3] They also found an
industrial machine for selecting plants. [@3] Police also seized several tonnes
of vacuum-packed buds ready for distribution. [@1] The Guardia Civil estimated
the plants would have yielded 615 tonnes of usable marijuana. [@3] It put the
potential illicit business at about 430 million euros. [@3]

The company gave the appearance of producing industrial CBD hemp, with technical
and legal advisers, while hiding an organisation dedicated to trafficking
marijuana. [@2] Investigators confirmed that the plantations had no legal cover
and that the plants, with dense buds, had a THC level that is not permitted.
[@2] The network had a pyramid structure, with three people at the top,
specialists in selecting and harvesting the plants in the middle and, at the
base, farmers who offered their land and work for a large share of the profits.
[@3] The farmer who cared for the Villa del Prado plantation told officers the
crop was intended for pharmaceutical use, which investigators took as evidence
that the farmers had been deceived. [@2]

Spain is the European Union's top cannabis producer, and the Guardia Civil and
the national police seize about a million cannabis plants a year, interior
ministry figures show. [@1]
