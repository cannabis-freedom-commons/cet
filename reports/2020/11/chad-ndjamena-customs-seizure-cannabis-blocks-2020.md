Chad's finance ministry and the directorate-general of customs and indirect
duties held an awareness day against fraud and smuggling in N'Djamena on
Saturday 7 November 2020, at which a large seizure of fraudulent and contraband
goods was presented. [@1] The goods displayed included seven packages containing
230 blocks of cannabis herb, which customs valued at 5,750,000 CFA francs. [@1]
The head of the national mobile customs brigade, Saleh Abakar Annour, said all
the seized goods were worth a total of 1,092,000,000 CFA francs, calling this a
huge loss of revenue for the state. [@1]

The rest of the seizure consisted of non-cannabis smuggled and fraudulent goods,
including mobile phones, gold, banknotes, foreign cigarettes, generic medicines,
adulterated whisky, motorcycles, vehicles, sugar and other items. [@1] Bidjeré
Bindjaki, director-general at the finance ministry, explained that the smugglers
unloaded goods on the other side of the borders and brought them in by pirogue,
moped or people with disabilities, hidden in specially adapted tyres, fuel
tanks, bodywork and engines. [@2]

Bindjaki said the fight against fraud should extend to terrorism and
cross-border crime in all its forms. [@1] The authorities said the whole city of
N'Djamena was under special customs control, giving officers powers ranging from
stop orders and pursuit to house visits. [@1]
