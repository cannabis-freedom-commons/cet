José 'Piculín' Ortiz, a former Puerto Rico basketball international, was
sentenced in federal court in San Juan to six months in prison, nine months in a
drug rehabilitation facility and four years of supervised release. [@3] U.S.
Judge Carmen C. Cerezo sentenced him for knowingly possessing with intent to
distribute 218 marijuana plants. [@3] He had pleaded guilty on 4 November 2011.
[@3] The four years of supervised release require 600 hours of community
service. [@3] That work includes anti-drug speeches at Puerto Rico elementary,
middle and high schools. [@3]

HSI agents executed a search warrant at the ground floor of his rented home in
Cayey, Puerto Rico, on 29 June 2011. [@3] There they found a fully operational
marijuana cultivation and grow room. [@3] Agents of U.S. Immigration and Customs
Enforcement and Puerto Rico Police officers seized 218 marijuana plants, which
the federal prosecutor said were for sale rather than personal use. [@2] The
federal prosecutor in San Juan, Rosa Emilia Rodríguez, said he was detained at
6:05 p.m. on the Wednesday. [@2] Officers also found 40 rounds of 5.56
ammunition compatible with AR-15 rifles, but no weapons. [@2] The prosecutor
estimated the cost of the marijuana at up to 150,000 dollars. [@2] She said the
investigation that led to the detention had lasted about a month. [@2]

Ortiz said a severe, long-untreated depression led him to alcohol and drugs, in
an interview with the newspaper Primera Hora. [@1] After his arrest he entered a
rehabilitation centre in Toa Alta, a town in northern Puerto Rico. [@1] As part
of his rehabilitation he kept helping at a basketball camp for children and
young people in Cayey, where he grew up. [@1] He said the medical and
psychological process could not be a matter of two months and had to continue.
