Edward Leigh Dickson was sentenced to four years in prison with a two-year
non-parole period for running 15.67 kg of cannabis from South Australia toward
Darwin, and his Toyota LandCruiser and the $1,990 cash he carried were
forfeited to the Northern Territory government. [@2] Police stopped the
LandCruiser at about 9:30 am on Tuesday 28 February 2012 at the Tanami and
Stuart highways intersection, about 20 km north of Alice Springs, where 35
one-pound cryovacced packages were hidden in three carry bags on the roof
rack. [@1,3] The court later analysed the haul at 15,671.71 g of cannabis
plant material, plus 13.85 g accepted as Dickson's own supply. [@3]

Dickson's travelling companion Anthony Kaissis was behind the wheel at the
stop, but the Crown accepted he knew nothing of the cannabis and he faced no
part in the case. [@3] Police believed the drugs were bound for Darwin and
possibly for remote communities, where profits would have been highest. [@1]
Dickson told police a man known only as "J" had loaded the bags in Adelaide
and promised him $5,000, plus two pounds of cannabis as payment, for the
Darwin run. [@3]

## Sentence

The Supreme Court rejected Dickson's courier story, finding he had been
cultivating rotational hydroponic crops in two grow rooms at his North
Plympton home in Adelaide, with twelve plants and 51 rooted clones found,
far beyond any personal need. [@3] He had pleaded guilty to aggravated
supply of a commercial quantity of cannabis, and the court called the
consignment a major quantity intended for the Top End market. [@3] The
forfeited LandCruiser was later sold at auction in Alice Springs for
$7,500. [@2]
