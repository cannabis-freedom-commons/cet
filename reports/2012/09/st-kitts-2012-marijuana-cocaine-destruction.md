Members of the Royal St. Christopher and Nevis Police Force Anti-Narcotics Unit
destroyed a large quantity of seized cocaine just outside Basseterre on the
morning of 15 September 2012. [@1] A small quantity of marijuana was destroyed
by fire just outside Basseterre in the presence of local reporters. [@1] The
unit's officers, smashing the hardened cocaine blocks into powder, wore white
protective gear, gloves, nose and mouth masks and eye protectors. [@1] The
destruction took less than two hours. [@1]

Police Press and Public Relations Officer Inspector Lyndon David said two
packets of every confiscated drug had been retained for training purposes for
the police, Customs and the Defence Force. [@1] David said the presence of
reporters demonstrated that the force was transparent in disposing of seized
narcotics. [@1] Anti-Narcotics Unit head Sergeant Travis Rogers said the cocaine
was loaded in St Lucia and was destined for the British Virgin Islands. [@1]

The seized cocaine was taken from a yacht intercepted in the federation's
territorial waters on 1 September 2012 carrying 134.108 kilograms with an
estimated street value of EC$11,816.255.00. [@1] Vincent Clerice, 56, and Nixon
Alexander, 42, both of Marigot, St Lucia, were charged with possession of
cocaine, possession with intent to supply and importation. [@1] At the
Basseterre Magistrate's Court Alexander's charges were withdrawn and he was
deported, while Clerice, the vessel's captain and owner, pleaded guilty to all
three. [@1] Clerice was convicted of importing cocaine and sentenced to two
years' imprisonment with hard labour. [@1] He was fined EC$1M, payable in nine
months, with a further four-year prison term if he defaulted. [@1] The Chief
Magistrate ordered the vessel forfeited to the Crown and advertised for sale at
public auction. [@1]
