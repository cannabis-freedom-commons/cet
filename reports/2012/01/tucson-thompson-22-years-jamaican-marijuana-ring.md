Mikado Nabob Thompson, a Jamaican national and lawful permanent resident of the
United States from Fort Lauderdale, Florida, was sentenced on 9 January 2012 to
22 years and two months. [@3] A federal jury convicted Thompson of running a
continuing criminal enterprise and of conspiracy and possession with intent to
distribute marijuana. [@1] The trial ran for five weeks, from August to October
2011. [@3] Co-defendant Paul L. Williams, also a Jamaican national, was
convicted on drug charges. [@1] Williams was sentenced to 121 months for
conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute marijuana. [@3] Twelve other
co-conspirators, many of them born in Jamaica, pleaded guilty before trial. [@3]

Prosecutors said that from 2002 to 2009 the organization distributed more than
6,000 kilograms of marijuana from Tucson to cities in the eastern United States,
including Pittsburgh, Philadelphia, New York City, Atlanta, Jacksonville and
Tampa. [@1] The DEA's statement on the sentencing put the volume at more than
11,000 pounds, about 5,000 kilograms. [@3]

Thompson bought a 2008 Lamborghini Murcielago for $335,000 in cash, and federal
agents seized a 2006 Rolls Royce Phantom worth about $253,000, more than
$100,000 in cash and several weapons. [@3] Judge Raner C. Collins also entered a
$4.4 million forfeiture order against Thompson. [@2] The DEA's Tucson District
Office led the investigation with Homeland Security Investigations, the Tucson
Police Department and the Marana Police Department. [@3] Assistant US Attorneys
James T. Lacey, Anthony E. Maingot and Ryan P. DeJoe prosecuted the case. [@3]
