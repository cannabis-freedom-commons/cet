Gendarmes questioned 25 people and placed seven in police custody while
dismantling a cannabis network on the Tahiti peninsula. [@1] Four suspects were
presented on Tuesday 5 June to an investigating judge; two were remanded in
pre-trial detention and two were placed under judicial supervision. [@1] The
investigation was still continuing. [@1] One family was suspected of running a
cannabis trafficking network that supplied the whole island of Tahiti. [@1]

Nearly 3,500 pakalolo (cannabis) plants were seized and destroyed on five
separate sites in the fenua aihere sector near the Vaipoiri cave, at the end of
an eight-month investigation. [@1] Officers also seized weapons and 519,000
Pacific francs in cash. [@1]

The operation was a rare show of force by gendarmes on Friday 1 June in the area
of the Tahiti peninsula known as Te Pari. [@1] Nearly 110 gendarmes took part at
about 6 a.m., acting simultaneously on five sites with a Dauphin N3 helicopter
and naval resources. [@1] The plantations held 700 to 1,000 plants each and were
hidden behind homes under translucent roofs to evade the gendarmerie's regular
aerial surveillance; the sites could be reached only by sea. [@1] Two family
heads in their forties ran production as a cooperative, using accomplice
gardeners paid from sales, and the crop was sold wholesale to dealers on the
island. [@1] The gendarmerie described it as the largest cannabis seizure in
Polynesia in several years. [@1]
