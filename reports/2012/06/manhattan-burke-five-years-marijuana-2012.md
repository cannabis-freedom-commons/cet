Kareem Burke, a co-founder of Roc-A-Fella Records, was sentenced to five years
in prison in Manhattan federal court on 4 June 2012. [@2] Burke, 38, of North
Bergen, New Jersey, had pleaded guilty in March 2012 to one count of conspiracy
to distribute and possess with intent to distribute more than 100 kilograms of
marijuana. [@1] He received the mandatory minimum five-year term under his plea
agreement. [@3] The charge carried a maximum of 40 years in prison. [@1] Under
the plea agreement Burke also forfeited $660,000, his New Jersey residence, the
$15,000 cash seized from it and his 2010 BMW. [@1]

Burke was arrested in October 2010 as part of Operation Green Venom. [@3]
Operation Green Venom targeted three separate but interconnected marijuana
trafficking cells that sold to customers in New York and New Jersey. [@1]
Prosecutors said the marijuana was grown using hydroponic systems in Florida and
California, then smuggled into New York and sold on the street. [@2] Burke was
recorded on a wiretap discussing activities at a grow house with the ring's
leader. [@2] When his New Jersey home was searched on the day of his arrest,
officers found the remains of a marijuana growing operation and about $15,000 in
cash. [@1]

At sentencing Burke told U.S. District Judge Loretta Swain she would never see
him in court again, and she replied that she would keep him to that promise.
[@2] The investigation involved Homeland Security Investigations, the Drug
Enforcement Administration, the New York City Police Department, the New York
City Department of Investigation, the U.S. Marshals Service and other federal,
state and local agencies. [@1] The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern
District of New York prosecuted the case through its Narcotics Unit. [@1]
Roc-A-Fella was founded in 1995 with Jay-Z and Damon Dash to release Jay-Z's
first album, Reasonable Doubt. [@2]
