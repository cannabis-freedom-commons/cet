On 30 August 2018, police came to the home of 54-year-old Mikhail
Tsinkverashvili. [@1] Tsinkverashvili lives in the small town of Akhmeta in
Kakheti. [@1] He was accused of cultivating marijuana. [@1] More than a year
after the search, criminal proceedings against him were still under way. [@1] He
remained free, which a member of the Girchi party attributed to public attention
to the case. [@1] He told officers that he smokes cannabis himself and does not
sell it. [@1]

A test showed that he was intoxicated because he had smoked shortly before the
police arrived. [@1] Police seized 22 kg of cannabis from his plot. [@1]
Officers found standing cannabis, dug it up and took it away. [@1] The
prosecutor offered Tsinkverashvili a procedural deal requiring an admission of
guilt. [@1] Under the deal, the prosecutor would seek one year of imprisonment
with four years conditional. [@1] If he refused, the judge would have to impose
six to twelve years of imprisonment. [@1]

In summer 2018, Georgia's Constitutional Court removed administrative liability
for marijuana use. [@1] Growing cannabis remained prohibited, with up to 70
grams punishable administratively and larger amounts criminally. [@1]
