Tomáš Houfek, a cannabis grower, had his appeal against an eight-year prison
sentence dismissed by the High Court (Vrchní soud) in Olomouc on 27 June 2018.
[@3] The Brno Regional Court had given him the term in December 2017 for
unauthorised production and other handling of narcotic and psychotropic
substances and poisons. [@4] He had spent four and a half months in pre-trial
detention, which he said was unlawful. [@2] He entered prison voluntarily after
the appeal failed. [@4] His lawyer said the case and its outcome had left him
psychologically crushed and exhausted. [@4]

Police found hundreds of cannabis plants and professional growing equipment in
the building of a former inn after someone reported him. [@4] He had first grown
five or six plants in a garden and then bought a building where he grew cannabis
at different stages of growth. [@2] The court also confiscated the seized
plants, the growing equipment and his car. [@4]

Houfek said a detoxification therapist had diagnosed him with colon cancer and
that he grew the plants to treat himself, having lost trust in conventional
medicine. [@4] Houfek, 35, said cannabis had cured his cancer, but that did not
help him in court. [@1] The appeal court said the therapist's written statement
did not show that he had suffered from any oncological disease. [@4] Judges had
his medical records from 2003 to 2017, and the chair of the appeal senate said
they showed no cancer diagnosis but did record alcohol and cannabis dependence
and experimentation with other drugs. [@4]

Supporters of cannabis treatment held a happening and relay runs in central
Olomouc on the day of the hearing. [@3] Dušan Dvořák, who led the Konopí je lék
(Cannabis Is Medicine) research programme, said the verdict had not taken any of
the defence's arguments into account. [@3]
