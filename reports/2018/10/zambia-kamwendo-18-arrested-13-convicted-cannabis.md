Zambia's Drug Enforcement Commission found more than 100 tonnes of fresh
cannabis in Kamwendo village in Mpika district, Muchinga Province. [@2] Eighteen
suspects were arrested, the commission's public relations officer, Kamufisa
Manchishi, said. [@2] The suspects were held at Mpika police station waiting to
appear in court. [@2]

The cannabis was found in three fields after a joint operation involving the
commission, the Department of National Parks and Wildlife, the Zambia Army and
the police. [@2] About 120 bags weighing more than six tonnes of loose cannabis
were confiscated, and the uprooting of the fresh plants began. [@2] The
commission's assistant spokesperson said the 18 were arrested after a joint
security operation in a remote part of Mpika district. [@1] Among those arrested
was a village chief suspected of masterminding a syndicate that supplied
cannabis to major cities. [@1]

The commission named Stanley Kazembe, a sub-headman known as 'Satana', as a key
suspect behind the cultivation. [@2] Manchishi said the plantation was believed
to have supplied the Copperbelt, Lusaka and Muchinga provinces in exchange for
goods. [@2] The commission warned farmers in the area to stop cultivating
cannabis. [@2] It said it was working with stakeholders to help small-scale
farmers take up genuine farming so as to discourage illegal cultivation. [@2]

The Chipata Magistrate Court sitting at Mfuwe under Mambwe District convicted 13
people of trafficking in loose cannabis, cannabis seeds and unlawful cultivation
of fresh cannabis plants. [@3] Seven of them were sentenced to two years for
trafficking in 11.0824 tonnes of loose cannabis and one year for cultivating
fresh plants weighing 94 tonnes, with the sentences to run concurrently. [@3] A
54-year-old woman was sentenced to one year simple imprisonment on the same
charges, and five juveniles were placed on probation for a year under the Mpika
Social Welfare Office. [@3]
