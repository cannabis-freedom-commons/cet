On 23 September 2018, the French frigate FS Floréal, operating under Combined
Task Force 150, boarded a fishing dhow behaving suspiciously in the Indian
Ocean and found 3,900 kg of hashish, which was confiscated and later
destroyed. [@1,2] A few hours later the ship's helicopter detected a second
suspicious dhow, and a flag-verification boarding with the ship's sniffer dog
Max turned up another 3,500 kg of hashish, also seized and destroyed. [@1,2]

## Operation

The two boardings struck within 24 hours, on 23 and 24 September, for a total
of more than seven tonnes of hashish. [@1,2] Combined Maritime Forces valued
the haul at $3.5 million at street values in Yemen, which it named as the
expected destination. [@1,2] Lieutenant Commander Carrard, the ship's
commanding officer, called the seizures a follow-on to the continuous efforts
of ships from many nations operating within CTF150. [@1] The command said the
seizure was its most significant in the Indian Ocean since the Royal Saudi
Naval Force took command of CTF150 in August, and that its warships had seized
and destroyed more than 1,500 kg of heroin and over 40,000 kg of hashish in
the previous twelve months. [@1,2]
