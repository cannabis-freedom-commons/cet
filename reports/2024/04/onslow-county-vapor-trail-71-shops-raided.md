On 3 April 2024 the Onslow County Sheriff's Office and other law enforcement
agencies raided 71 vape shops in Onslow County. [@5] Police called the operation
Vapor Trail. [@4] The operation made 17 arrests. [@2] Thirteen law enforcement
agencies took part, including the Drug Enforcement Administration and NCIS. [@3]
Law enforcement seized 3,006 pounds (about 1,363.5 kg) of THC and marijuana
products and $855,577 in cash. [@2] Officers entered two of the shops in full
tactical gear with guns drawn and were not wearing body cameras, according to
the owners' lawsuit. [@4]

On 8 November 2024 the county officially brought charges against the shops and
arrest warrants went out for shop owners and distributors. [@4] The owners say
the sheriff's office used gas chromatography, which they argue can raise THC
levels and turn a legal product into an illegal one. [@4] The owners' lawyer
said his clients had packaging and test results showing the products were legal.
[@3] Two years later the other criminal cases were awaiting trial or had been
dropped. [@5]

In December 2024 the owners of two Onslow County stores, Muammer Saleh and Tareq
Alsaede, sued Sheriff Chris Thomas and two deputies, alleging unlawful arrests
and seizures. [@3] The lawsuit claims that Onslow County sheriffs targeted the
stores owned by those of Middle Eastern descent while not raiding other stores
in the county that sell the same hemp products. [@3] The suit alleges officers
forced employees to turn off surveillance cameras and took money from registers
and safes. [@4] Muammer Saleh, one of the plaintiffs, died a month after filing
and his estate continued the suit. [@5] In November 2026 the sheriff and his
deputies asked a federal court to throw out the claims, saying the raid was
supported by a valid warrant. [@1]
