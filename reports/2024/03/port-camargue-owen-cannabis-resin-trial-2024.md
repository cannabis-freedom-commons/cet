Carmien Owen was one of 15 people implicated in a 1996 cannabis-resin
importation and trafficking case at the Port-Camargue marina, Le Grau-du-Roi.
[@1] He was a British man living in Canada, had been convicted in absentia and
sentenced to 10 years in prison, and was subject to an arrest warrant. [@1] He
was arrested and extradited in April 2023 and imprisoned at Nîmes remand prison.
[@1] The Gard assize court tried him on 28 and 29 March 2024. [@1]

The network moved cannabis resin from Morocco to France, and investigators
seized 2.7 tonnes of it from a sailboat at Port-Camargue in 1996. [@1] After
several days of surveillance, customs officers intercepted the sailboat Lady
Duncan at Le Grau-du-Roi on 21 November 1996, after it tried to dock at
Port-Camargue and put back to sea. [@1] The boat was carrying 2.7 tonnes of
cannabis resin. [@1] Customs valued the consignment at the time at 104.7 million
francs, about 16 million euros. [@1] Three men — a German, an Englishman and a
Belgian — were aboard and were arrested. [@1]

Michaël Sherman, an American described as the mastermind of the international
operation, escaped. [@1] Barry Daffern, described as Sherman's right-hand man,
was arrested. [@1] French customs, the judicial police (PJ) and the US Drug
Enforcement Administration worked on the case. [@1] Advocate general Jean-Luc
Vasserot requested 10 years of criminal imprisonment. [@1]
