The Court of First Instance in Philipsburg sentenced Wesley Primo (49) to three
years in prison for leading a network that smuggled marijuana into St. Maarten
and supplied local dealers. [@1] Primo was found guilty of marijuana importation
between February and October 2022, money laundering and membership of a criminal
organisation, including almost 1.6 kg of marijuana and two rounds of ammunition
found in a house search. [@1] The court gave Setember Holubiczko (57) a 30-month
prison sentence for arranging marijuana shipments from the United States, money
laundering and membership of a criminal organisation. [@1] The court gave Cesar
Cruz (35) 150 hours of community service and a suspended 360 days in prison on
two years’ probation for facilitating the smuggling with fake customer accounts,
monitoring shipments, delivering packages and concealment advice. [@1] Primo’s
wife G.A. (51) was acquitted of marijuana possession, money laundering and
membership of a criminal organisation. [@1]

The probe began on June 1, 2022, when Customs officers uncovered nearly 7 kg of
marijuana hidden in two boxes at a local shipping company. [@1] The drugs were
removed and the boxes resealed for delivery to “Steve Joseph”, a fake name and
account Cruz created for Primo. [@1] The investigation led to the four suspects
being arrested on October 1, 2022. [@1] A search of Primo’s home found nearly
1.6 kg of marijuana in a transparent container in the bedroom, two rounds of
ammunition, cash in several places and papers listing local dealers with
amounts. [@1] Holubiczko was picked up at Princess Juliana International Airport
with $8,050 in her bag as she was about to catch an outbound flight. [@1]

Primo’s phone showed WhatsApp groups for distribution, shipping, money and
pushers, which the prosecutor called a very organised and professional operation
aimed at drug trafficking and money laundering. [@1] The judge described Primo
as the “spider on the web” with different departments running the trafficking
smoothly, rejected his medical-use and legitimate-business claims, and rejected
the argument that Dutch authorities lacked jurisdiction over US-based
Holubiczko. [@1]
