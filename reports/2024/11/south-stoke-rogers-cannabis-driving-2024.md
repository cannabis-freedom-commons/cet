Amanda Rogers, headteacher of South Stoke Primary School in Wallingford,
Oxfordshire, was suspended from her role after being arrested by police as she
travelled to work and later appeared in court, where she admitted a drugs
offence. [@1]

She was arrested on 22 November 2024 on suspicion of driving while under the
influence of cannabis. [@1,2] She was suspended from her post five days after the arrest, followed by an internal disciplinary process. [@2] She had consumed cannabis in the early hours of the morning before driving towards the school. [@2] Reading Magistrates' Court sentenced her to a £269
fine and she was also banned from driving for a year. [@1,2] She was also ordered to pay a £108 surcharge and was disqualified from driving for 12 months. [@2] Her blood contained 3.7ug/L of THC, the active compound in cannabis. [@2] Rogers pleaded guilty and was convicted at Reading Magistrates' Court on 30 January 2025 of driving with a controlled drug above the specified limit. [@2] The Teaching
Regulation Agency (TRA), the regulator for England's teachers, found that if Mrs
Rogers had not been arrested, she would have had "interactions with pupils"
while high on cannabis. [@1]

The TRA panel decided not to ban her from future teaching, saying she had
"learned important lessons". [@1,2] The panel noted that Mrs Rogers is not
currently teaching but could "make a positive contribution" if she chose to
return to the profession in the future. [@1]

Rogers, former headteacher of South Stoke Primary School near Reading, appeared before a Teaching Regulation Agency professional conduct panel on 22 October. [@2] Instead of a ban, publication of the panel’s findings was considered sufficient to uphold professional standards. [@2]
