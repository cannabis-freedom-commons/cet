Gendarmes at Meyo-Kyé, on the border between Gabon, Cameroon and Equatorial
Guinea, intercepted two Gabonese women in their thirties trying to cross with
prohibited goods hidden in sanitary pads. [@1] The two were Gaina-Vanelle Mengue
m'Ondo, a trader on a gold-panning site, and her friend Nicole Frieda-Folyse
Obame Maganga. [@1]

They had concealed about 300 Tramadol tablets, known as kobolos, and an
unspecified quantity of Indian hemp (chanvre indien) inside sanitary pads. [@1]
According to L'Union, the trip began in Kyè-Ossi, Cameroon, where they bought
300 kobolo tablets for 150,000 CFA francs. [@1] Gaina-Vanelle, who supplied gold
panners with provisions, had expanded into drug sales to meet demand on the
sites. [@1]

Officers found the concealed products during a thorough body search. [@3] After
being questioned, both suspects were released on provisional liberty by the Oyem
prosecutor's office, a decision that surprised investigators and public opinion.
[@1] L'Union said the hearing took place in the families' presence without the
direct involvement of law enforcement officers. [@2]
