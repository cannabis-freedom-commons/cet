Comorian police commissioner Abou Salim, in charge of the fight against drugs,
was arrested and placed in pre-trial detention. [@1] The Moroni prosecutor says
he made 14 of the 15 sacks of cannabis seized in the operation disappear rather
than deliver them to the police station. [@2] The cannabis was seized at
Ouropveni, in the south of Ngazidja, from a fishing boat that had come
clandestinely from Madagascar. [@2] The prosecutor said the boat had landed
Malagasy migrants and 15 sacks of cannabis of 20 kilograms each. [@2] Only one
sack was deposited at the police station; the other 14 disappeared while under
the commissioner's surveillance, the prosecutor said. [@2]

He is prosecuted for complicity in importing, transporting, distributing and
selling narcotics, and for corruption and receiving stolen goods. [@2] La
Gazette des Comores described the suspected diversion as nearly 300 kilograms of
cannabis. [@2] Comores Infos reported that he allegedly ordered his subordinates
to divide the last seized sack among themselves before telling superiors that
the drugs had been stolen. [@1] Comores Infos put the value of the diverted
cannabis at several million Comorian francs. [@1] The national brigade against
migrant smuggling, which carried out the 22 June operation, denies that any
cannabis was seized at Ouropveni that day. [@1] Comores Infos links the arrest
to the interior minister's clean-up of the police, led by Mahamoud Fakri. [@1]
