On 22 July 2024 Mexico's Navy Ministry announced the seizure of about 6.88
tonnes of suspected marijuana at the port of Veracruz. [@1] Mexico's maritime customs office in Veracruz carried out the inspection on Saturday 20 July 2024. [@2] The cannabis was
concealed in pine-wood doors inside a container bound for the Dominican
Republic. [@1] Inside each door officers found up to 18 packages of the drug in different sizes, marked with numbers and letters. [@3] Officers found the load after anomalies appeared during a scan,
which led them to open the container and recover the wrapped packages. [@1] Customs kept the identity of the company that sent the shipment confidential while the investigation continues. [@3] No one was arrested over the load. [@3] Customs counted 3,333 packages with a net weight of 6,877.77 kg, which were placed at the disposal of the federal prosecutor's office in Veracruz. [@2] The container had been flagged by the customs platform SIA ALERTAS, and the federal prosecutor's office, the Defence Secretariat and the Navy were asked to help with a deeper inspection. [@3]

The case was the largest of four seizures announced at the port between July
2024 and January 2025 that used export merchandise to hide marijuana for
shipment abroad. [@1] In three of those four cases, including this one, the
authorities named the Dominican Republic as the destination. [@1] The account,
built from transparency responses, says the documentation traces some movements
of the cargo but leaves unanswered who produced or paid for the shipments and
who was to receive them. [@1] None of the reports it reviewed establishes that
the marijuana had been grown in Veracruz. [@1]

Official reports allow the export route to be followed to its seizure at the
port of Veracruz, but not the farm-to-port chain between cultivation in
marginalized municipalities, transport and embarkation. [@1]
