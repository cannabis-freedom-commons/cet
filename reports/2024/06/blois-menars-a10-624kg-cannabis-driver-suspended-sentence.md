Customs officers from the Tours and Orléans brigades found cartons of cannabis
in a lorry from Spain during a check at the Blois-Ménars rest area on the A10 on
5 June 2024. [@3] A sniffer dog marked the cab of the lorry straight away. [@2]
Officers found cartons hidden under the driver's bunk in the cab and more
cartons among the load in the trailer. [@2] After weighing, the regional customs
directorate counted 564.5 kg of cannabis resin and 60.2 kg of herbal cannabis,
valued at 5,118,000 euros. [@2] The lorry was carrying the load from Spain to
Belgium. [@1]

Gendarmes arrested the driver, a Moroccan national in his forties, and he was
brought before the Blois criminal court on 10 June 2024 for an immediate
appearance hearing. [@2] The prosecutor sought seven years in prison. [@1] The
court acquitted him of the drug offences, finding the evidence of intent
insufficient. [@4] It convicted him only of customs offences, for not checking
what his lorry carried, and sentenced him to five years in prison suspended, a
customs fine of 51,180 euros and a ban from French territory until 2029. [@1] He
told the court he had collected the lorry in a Spanish port without checking the
cartons, which sat beside car parts. [@4]

The Blois prosecutor appealed within the ten-day legal deadline. [@4] No date
had been fixed for the new hearing before the Orléans appeal court. [@4] The
driver had returned home to Spain. [@4] The lorry was seized and its owner was
to be identified to establish whether a wider network was involved. [@3]
