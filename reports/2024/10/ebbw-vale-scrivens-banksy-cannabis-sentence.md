Christopher Scrivens, 37, was the leader of a cannabis gang operating from Ebbw
Vale. [@2] Newport Crown Court sentenced him to three years in prison on 11
October 2024. [@1] He had pleaded guilty to cannabis importation and conspiracy
to supply cannabis. [@1] He was one of eight people who appeared in court that
Friday for offences including conspiracy to supply drugs and money laundering.
[@1] The judge said he invested in expensive art to distance himself from his
crimes. [@1]

He imported about 15.8 kg of cannabis from California for onward supply. [@3]
The cannabis was valued at £104,880. [@1] He told a co-conspirator he intended
to "flood the valleys with cannabis". [@1] Gwent Police seized Banksy works
bought with the proceeds, including Grappling Hook, Monkey Queen and The
Watchtower Swing. [@4] The works were estimated to be worth more than £190,000.
[@4] Prosecutor Roger Griffiths said Scrivens used someone else's bank account
to buy the Grappling Hook and that ill-gotten gains were part of the purchase.
[@1] Police also seized designer goods, a caravan, quad bikes and jet skis worth
an estimated £48,400. [@4]

David Jenkins, 47, invested £23,000 of his own money in a Banksy work and
received £4,000 of cannabis cash from Scrivens. [@1] Jenkins was given a
12-month community order with 50 hours of unpaid work. [@1] Kyle Webber supplied
cannabis to street users and grew his own. [@1] Webber was given a two-year
community order. [@1] Daniel Jones, 39, was given a 12-month community order
with 40 hours of unpaid work. [@1] Cory Roe, 39, acted as a cannabis plant
"babysitter". [@1] Roe was given a 12-month community order with 80 hours of
unpaid work. [@1] Scott Hodkinson held cash for Scrivens. [@1] Hodkinson was
given an 18-month community order with 150 hours of unpaid work. [@1] Becky
Williams was asked to remove £23,000 from a bag for Scrivens. [@1] Williams was
given an 18-month community order with 40 hours of unpaid work. [@1] Rebecca
Mills, Scrivens' partner, allowed £22,000 to pass through her account, and her
sentencing was adjourned to a later date. [@1]
