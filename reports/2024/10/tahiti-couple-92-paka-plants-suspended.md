A couple appeared before the Papeete correctional court on 29 October 2024
accused of cultivating 92 cannabis plants in a shed at their home. [@1] Their
home was in Taiarapu Est, where gendarmes had seized 92 paka plants from a
greenhouse. [@2] Both were sentenced to three months of imprisonment, fully
suspended. [@2] The woman, 31, told the court she had suffered joint pain since
birth and no longer wanted medicines that upset her stomach. [@2] Her partner,
13 years her senior, said he was firmly opposed to the project. [@2]

Gendarmes found the crop in May 2023, alerted by a strong smell of cannabis from
the house, and the greenhouse was fitted with LED lamps. [@2] The couple said
they started with six plants three months before they were caught, but already
had 92 when the crop was seized. [@1] The prosecutor said there were no CBD
plants in French Polynesia and that such a quantity with that equipment was
often intended for resale. [@2]

The couple argued the plants were CBD, a cannabinoid without psychoactive
properties, grown solely for personal therapeutic use. [@1] Their lawyer noted
the plants had been destroyed before any analysis, so their identity could never
be established. [@1] French Polynesia had legalised possession of CBD products
in August 2024, but cultivation of CBD and cannabis remained prohibited. [@1]
The prosecutor had requested six months of suspended imprisonment. [@1]
