Customs officers of the mobile brigade of Bobo-Dioulasso seized more than a
tonne of chanvre indien, the term used locally for cannabis, on the night of 20
to 21 July 2011. [@1] Six officers made the seizure 3 km from Koumbia, a town
about 60 km from Bobo-Dioulasso. [@1] The operation was an ambush and a chase on
the road between Bobo-Dioulasso and Ouagadougou. [@2] Eleven cyclists carrying
the drug abandoned the cannabis and their bicycles and fled. [@1]

Officers counted 19 packages weighing 1,055 kg. [@1] The customs service
estimated the value of the haul at 105,500,000 CFA francs. [@2] The cannabis was
tied to the backs of the bicycles in about twenty plastic bags. [@2] It was
stored at the regional customs directorate for the west and shown to journalists
on the afternoon of 21 July. [@2]

Customs had received information since January 2011 that traffickers used the
area's rural tracks. [@1] Souleymane Tou, who heads the brigade, said the
cannabis probably came from Ghana and was in transit through Burkina Faso to
Mali. [@1] Tou called it the brigade's biggest seizure of the year and said
local people acting as informers had helped make it possible. [@1]
