By mid-summer 2016, Sheriff Lopey concluded that fining the growers was useless.
[@1] Sheriff’s volunteers teamed up with the county prosecutor’s office and
state Fish and Game officials to raid the fields. [@1] Officers cleared as many
as three fields a day, raiding 113 grows in four months. [@1] Fifty Hmong
growers were hauled off to jail. [@1]

The teams seized generators and water pumps and destroyed 9,200 cannabis plants
and 3,000 pounds of marijuana. [@1] Lopey estimated the raids destroyed as much
as 20% of the 2016 crop. [@1] The summer operation produced fewer than two dozen
criminal cases, which county judges dismissed after California voters made
illegal cultivation a misdemeanor that November. [@1]

Siskiyou County supervisors had banned outdoor cultivation in November 2015
after complaints about fences, water trucks, fertilizer runoff and trash. [@1]
