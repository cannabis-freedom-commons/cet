A former British war correspondent and an Australian were arrested in Bali with
17 grams of hashish in total, police on the island said on Monday, and they face
up to 20 years in prison. [@1] They were David Fox, 54, a former Reuters
correspondent, and Giuseppe Serafino, 48, an Australian businessman, detained on
Saturday for allegedly possessing small quantities of the drug. [@2] The two
were picked up during a police operation on Saturday, 8 October 2016. [@1] Fox
told investigators he had taken cannabis resin for years to cope with stress in
war zones. [@1] He began using hashish because of a Reuters assignment covering
a conflict zone in Somalia, said Nyoman Artana, deputy police chief in Denpasar.
[@2]

Police searched Serafino's home in the Sanur area of south Bali after residents
reported that a foreigner there was using drugs. [@1] Investigators seized about
seven grams of hashish there, supplied by Fox, according to police. [@1] Fox was
arrested later in a bar. [@1] Ten grams of hashish were seized on him and at his
home. [@1] ABC reported the pair were taken into custody with 17 grams of
hashish between them. [@3] Serafino said he used the drug to help fight cancer,
according to Artana. [@1] Fox worked for Thomson Reuters for more than 20 years,
with postings in Bosnia, Rwanda, Afghanistan and Iraq, and left the agency in
2011. [@1]

Both men faced up to 20 years in prison but avoided the death penalty because of
the small quantities involved. [@1] The Baron reported that they escaped a
capital charge because they were not accused of drug trafficking. [@2] ABC
reported that, if convicted, they faced between five years and life in prison.
[@3] Indonesia has one of the world's strictest anti-drug laws. [@1] Dozens of
people sentenced to death for drug trafficking, including the Frenchman Serge
Atlaoui, are on death row. [@1]
