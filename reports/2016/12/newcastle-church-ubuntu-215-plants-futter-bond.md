NSW police raided a medicinal cannabis growing dispensary in Newcastle run by
the Church of Ubuntu. [@1] Officers seized 215 plants from the group's
hydroponic operation. [@1] The raid took place at Barry John Futter's Newcastle
residence on 1 December 2016. [@2] The plants were being grown for distribution
without charge to patients registered with the church. [@2] The church served
close to 2,000 customers. [@1] It provided small plants for patients to grow at
home to treat cancer and ease extreme epilepsy. [@1] No one was arrested during
the raid, though police said charges would follow. [@1] Co-founder Karen Burge
said the group had grown for cancer patients and parents of children with
epilepsy for two years and that authorities knew about it. [@1]

Detective Inspector Peter Mahon said the operation remained in breach of the
Drug Misuse and Trafficking Act 1985 despite its medicinal purpose. [@1] Some
patients held terminal-condition waivers under the Medicinal Cannabis
Compassionate Use Scheme, which gives police discretion not to charge terminally
ill adults. [@1] Futter later pleaded guilty to cultivating a large commercial
quantity by enhanced indoor means and to drug supply. [@2] Futter, the president
of Ubuntu, faced up to 20 years on the cultivation charge. [@2] Judge Roy Ellis
sentenced him in Newcastle District Court on 24 October. [@2] The judge imposed
a conditional release order without recording a conviction, requiring a 12-month
good behaviour bond. [@3] The judge found the cultivation was for non-profit
medicinal purposes amid rising awareness of potential medicinal benefits. [@2]

At the time of the raid Ubuntu was open about producing and distributing
cannabis medicine, and Futter had discussed the operations with senior police.
[@2] Burge said police had been told many times and then premier Mike Baird had
been invited at Dan Haslam's funeral to come and see the operation. [@1]
