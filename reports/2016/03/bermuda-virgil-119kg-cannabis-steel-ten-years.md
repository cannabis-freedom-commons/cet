Eight people, Iman Gibbons, Corte Gibbons, Shannon Trott, Damon Edwards, Brian
Anderson, Dina Tucker, John Vernell Bernell Jefferies and Darrin Virgil,
appeared in court on 30 August 2013 charged with conspiring to supply cannabis.
[@1] They did not have to enter a plea because the charge was to be heard in the
Supreme Court. [@1] Magistrate Archie Warner released all eight on bail of
$100,000 and ordered them to surrender their passports, with a further
appearance set for 27 September. [@1] Seven men and one woman had been arrested
on 30 November 2012 after a joint Customs and Bermuda Police Service operation
at a Limehouse Lane residence in Hamilton Parish. [@6]

On 26 November 2012, customs officers X-rayed a container of steel sheets in the
cargo of the Oleander and saw rectangular objects hidden inside. [@3] On 29
November police watched the container delivered to Abbott's Cliff in Hamilton
Parish. [@3] Officers later found the steel plates at a Lime House Lane property
that contained G&D Auto and the home of Corte and Iman Gibbons. [@3] Hidden in
the steel sheets were 119 kg of cannabis, which the Royal Gazette said had an
estimated street value of more than $5.9 million. [@3] The steel had been sent
from Canada, and the court heard that Jefferis and the Gibbons brothers had
flown to Ontario, where it originated, in the months before the shipment. [@3]

After a two-month Supreme Court trial, Darrin Virgil was found guilty of
conspiring to import and supply the cannabis hidden in the steel. [@3] John
Jefferis was found not guilty by a 10-2 majority, and Corte and Iman Gibbons
were cleared unanimously. [@3] Brian Anderson pleaded guilty during the trial,
and Judge Carlisle Greaves ruled there was insufficient evidence to convict Dina
Tucker, Damon Edwards and Shannon Trott. [@3]

Darrin Virgil was sentenced to ten years in prison, and Brian Anderson, who
admitted helping to move the drugs after they arrived in Bermuda, was sentenced
to three years. [@4] The Appeal Panel found the conviction safe and dismissed
the appeal. [@5] Written reasons for that decision were dated 18 March. [@5]

The prosecutor told the magistrate that the amount was 120 kilos, about 264
pounds, with a street value of approximately $5.9 million. [@2]
