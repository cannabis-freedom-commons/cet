A police officer who sold details of suspected cannabis farms in a drugs plot
was jailed for seven years. [@1] He was Barry Parkinson, 46, from Liverpool,
convicted of burglary, supplying drugs and admitting misconduct in public
office. [@1] He was convicted with three other men of two conspiracies to commit
burglary and to supply cannabis. [@2] The others were Robert Sloan, 45, Shaun
Blackburn, 32 and David Gould, 53, all from Liverpool. [@1] It was a re-trial
after the earlier jury could not agree verdicts. [@2]

Parkinson used the force's computer system to gather information about suspected
cannabis crops and passed it to his co-conspirators for money. [@1] Sloan, who
admitted aiding and abetting misconduct in public office, was jailed for six
years. [@1] Gould received five years and Blackburn four-and-a-half years. [@1]
The judge told Parkinson: 'The scale of your corruption was spectacular and
persistent. Far from upholding the law, you were assisting others to break it in
a serious way.' [@2]

The plot was to identify suitable targets so that cannabis could be stolen from
the farms and sold on. [@2] Parkinson, formerly of the force's Major Crime Unit,
was exposed by texts and a listening device in a suspect's car. [@3] He admitted
two misconduct in public office charges, accepting that he had falsified an
arrest report. [@2] A covert listening device in Sloan's car and text messages
showed that Parkinson, who had huge debts, was supplying information for money.
[@2] Judge Conrad said there was no direct evidence that burglaries had taken
place, only circumstantial evidence that one was attempted or planned. [@2]
Merseyside Deputy Chief Constable Nikki Holland said the former officer had let
his colleagues down and his behaviour was completely unacceptable. [@1]
