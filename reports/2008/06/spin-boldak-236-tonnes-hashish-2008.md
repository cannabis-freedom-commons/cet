Afghan police found and destroyed a vast stash of hashish near Spin Boldak in Kandahar province, close to the Pakistan border, on Monday 9 June 2008.[@3,4] The DEA said Operation Albatross was a joint effort with the Afghan government and its Foreign-Deployed Advisory and Support Teams, supported by NATO-ISAF, the U.S. Department of Defense and the State Department. [@7] The cannabis resin was buried in trenches and bunkers in the desert, and the cache was so extensive that NATO aircraft were called in to bomb it.[@3,4] Search warrants were executed at five narcotics super-labs and numerous underground drug bunkers in Spin Boldak, in Taliban-controlled areas of Kandahar province. [@7]

UNODC executive director Antonio Maria Costa congratulated Afghanistan's interior minister for the seizure of 236.8 tons of hashish with a potential wholesale value of $400 million, according to the NATO operation in the country.[@1,2,4] NATO's International Security Assistance Force said the hashish would have netted the Taliban about $14 million in profit. [@6] He called it a massive seizure and a major success for counter-narcotics in Afghanistan.[@1,2] NATO's International Security Assistance Force said the find seriously crippled the Taliban's ability to buy weapons, and its commander General David McKiernan said the Afghan National Police Special Task Force had proved its capability against the drug trade.[@3,4,5]

The same operation also turned up five tons of opium, which is not counted in this report's cannabis figures.[@3]

The operation also resulted in the arrest of multiple drug dealers, including some from Iran. [@4] Afghanistan's National Interdiction Unit and the DEA arrested 12 suspects during the operation. [@8] Deputy Interior Minister Lieutenant General Abdul Hadi Khalid said three men were arrested in the raid when he announced the bust on Wednesday. [@5]
