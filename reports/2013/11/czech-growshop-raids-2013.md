From Monday 4 November 2013, police across the Czech Republic raided shops selling
indoor-growing supplies, in what the cannabis-legalisation movement Legalizace called
an unprecedented operation to liquidate grow shops. [@1,2] Officers inspected at least
50 shops, confiscating fertiliser, lamps and other equipment and closing some of them
down; the raid was expected to continue for possibly several more weeks. [@2] Other
accounts put the number at more than 50 cleared-out shops [@1] or dozens of stores
[@3], with fertiliser, grow lights and cultivation guidebooks seized. [@3]

Police spokeswoman Ivana Ježková said grow shops had often been overlooked in the
fight against marijuana distribution, noting a marked rise in cannabis farming since
2007 linked to the spread of equipment stores. [@2] The crackdown relied on a 2012
Supreme Court decision and the spreading-toxicomania law, applied to shops selling the
full chain from seeds to literature even after many had restricted those ranges. [@1]
At least three people were charged, with dozens more suspected of distributing and
promoting marijuana use. [@2]

Legalizace said the confiscated legal goods were worth millions of crowns and organised
a demonstration in Prague's Old Town Square on 9 November attended by several hundred
people. [@1]
