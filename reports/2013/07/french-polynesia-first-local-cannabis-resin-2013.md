French Polynesia customs surveillance officers seized the territory's first
locally made cannabis resin on 16 July 2013. [@1] Until then, the cannabis resin
used in French Polynesia had been imported by post from metropolitan France.
[@1] The two bars seized weighed 15 grams in all and had been made on an island
of the Raromatai, the Leeward Islands. [@1] The customs account named that
island group as the Leeward Islands. [@2] They were to be sold on the market of
the Papeete urban area. [@1]

Customs put the local market value at 75,000 CFP francs, or 5,000 CFP francs a
gram, five times the price of cannabis herb, known locally as pakalolo. [@1] The
tetrahydrocannabinol content was presumed high, and a laboratory analysis was
still to confirm it. [@1] The resin was to be sold retail as bars the size of
small Le Griffon or Union Match matchboxes, the usual pack for local cannabis
herb. [@1]

Customs said a locally made resin matching metropolitan and European habits
pointed to a thriving local trade and a growing risk from cannabis use. [@1] The
offender, a resident of Tahiti, was referred to the correctional court to answer
two offences, one under ordinary criminal law and one under customs law. [@1]
