Israeli security forces seized 140 kilograms of hashish that had been smuggled
into Israel from Egypt early on Tuesday 30 July 2013. [@1] Police said the
interception was carried out by officers of the Magen unit together with
undercover Yamas South operatives, after a prolonged chase of a jeep on the
Egyptian border. [@3] The smugglers reportedly abandoned their vehicle with the
drugs inside and fled on foot into the desert, with security personnel in
pursuit. [@1] The Times of Israel said the seized hashish was worth millions of
shekels on the street. [@1]

Forces spotted a suspicious vehicle near the border fence north of Har Harif, in
the Southern Brigade sector of the Egyptian border, and after a chase its
occupants got out and left the drugs in it. [@2] The police report said the
smugglers fled in a jeep of the Sufa model loaded with the drugs, and that the
chase ended with the seizure of the drugs and the smugglers. [@3] The smugglers
escaped and the pursuit continued, and no arrests were reported. [@2]

A police detective said smugglers in Sinai were in trouble because of the border
fence, with the vast majority of the goods now seized at the fence. [@3] The
same officer said such a smuggling run could earn dealers between one and three
million shekels, which is why they had moved to larger weights. [@3] The
commander of the Magen unit said coordination between the professional bodies
would continue in order to thwart drug smuggling into Israel from the Egyptian
border. [@3]
