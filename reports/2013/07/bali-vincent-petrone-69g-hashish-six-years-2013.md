On 15 July 2013, the Denpasar court in Bali sentenced French national Vincent
Roger Petrone to six years in prison for drug trafficking after he was caught
carrying 69 grams of hashish in his intestines. [@1,3] Petrone, born in Dijon on
31 May 1969 and living in Nouméa in New Caledonia, had been stopped on arrival
at Bali international airport on 29 January. [@1,3] Customs officers, struck by
his nervousness, had him X-rayed, revealing four capsules in his intestines
containing 69 grams of hashish with a market value of about 42 million rupiah.
[@1,3] He had arrived on a Tuesday evening aboard a Malaysia Airlines flight from
Kuala Lumpur. [@2] Officers took him to the Bali International Medical Center in
Kuta, where it was found that he had swallowed four capsules containing hashish.
[@2]

Judge Parulian Saragih found him legally and convincingly guilty of importing
narcotics. [@1,3] The court imposed six years in prison and a one-billion-rupiah
fine, about 76,500 euros. [@1,3] The judge delivered the sentence and the fine in a five-minute ruling that visibly shocked Petrone and his lawyer. [@3] The sentence was heavier than the prosecution's
request for five years' imprisonment. [@1,3] Prosecutors had taken his clean
record into account in requesting only five years, although drug trafficking in
Indonesia is punishable by death for any quantity above five grams. [@1,3]
Prosecutor I Gusti Putu Atmaja said he had quickly confessed, said he regretted
his act and had never been convicted, which was why the death penalty had not
been sought. [@1,3] Customs chief Made Wijaya valued the haul at 42 million rupiah
in total at 600,000 rupiah per gram. [@2] Bali police then took over the case,
with Petrone charged under Article 113 of the Narcotics Law, which carries the
death penalty. [@2]

The defence had pleaded addiction and asked for the lightest possible sentence
so Petrone could undergo detoxification treatment. [@1,3] Petrone said the drug
was for his personal consumption on a doctor's recommendation because he had
long suffered back pain. [@1,3] He said he had carried drugs for the first time,
had bought them in Bangkok and wanted to take them to New Caledonia after his
Bali holidays. [@1] His lawyer Erwin Siregar called the sentence severe, said
they had hoped for five years and said they would consider an appeal. [@1,3]
