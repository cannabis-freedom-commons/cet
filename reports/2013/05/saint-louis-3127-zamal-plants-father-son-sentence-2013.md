A father and son appeared before the Saint-Pierre correctional court on 30 May
2013 and received suspended prison terms of one and three months for the
unauthorised possession of more than 3,000 zamal plants. [@2] The court
sentenced the father to one month in prison with suspension and a 5,000-euro
suspended fine, and the son to three months with suspension and a 10,000-euro
fine of which 8,500 euros was suspended. [@2] Zamal is the Réunion name for
cannabis. [@1]

The case began on 12 February 2013, when gendarmes seized 3,127 zamal plants
weighing 1,150 tonne, or about 1.15 tonnes, in a sugar-cane field at Le Gol,
Saint-Louis. [@3] The gendarmes of the Étang-Salé brigade intervened at about 6
a.m. after several days of surveillance. [@3] The plants were hidden among sugar
cane on a farm near the Gol sugar factory. [@3] Some of the plants reached three
metres in height. [@3] The plot covered about two hectares within a sugar-cane
plantation. [@2]

Two people, the farm operator and his son, were placed in police custody on the
morning of the seizure. [@3] The first account said the father was released
later that morning and appeared to be out of the case. [@1] The son told the
court he had started the plantation to support his family and speed up a return
to farming. [@2] Preliminary estimates put the commercial value of the whole
seizure at several thousand euros. [@3]
