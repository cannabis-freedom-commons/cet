Bahamian police arrested nine suspects after 1,220 pounds (about 553 kg) of
marijuana was found on Andros Island and flown to Nassau. [@1] A joint task
force from Operation Bahamas, Turks and Caicos, known as OPBAT, seized the
marijuana in the vicinity of Andros Island. [@1] Butler credited the seizure to local law enforcement and the Bahamas' partnership with OPBAT. [@3] Assistant Superintendent Samuel Butler, officer in charge of the Drug Enforcement Unit, said the suspects were apprehended in Mars Bay and that the drugs were part of a smuggling effort that began in Jamaica. [@3] The Coast Guard put the
wholesale value of the haul at an estimated $1.1 million. [@1]

The task force included the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration, the U.S. Coast
Guard, the Bahamas Drug Enforcement Unit and the Royal Bahamas Police Force.
[@1] A Coast Guard aircrew stationed at the Atlantic Undersea Test and
Evaluation Center, the U.S. Navy's AUTEC facility on Andros, spotted the stash
from an MH-60T Jayhawk helicopter. [@1] The aircrew guided investigators to a
remote site in the island's mangroves. [@1] The Jayhawk crew then hoisted the
marijuana and carried it to Bahamian law enforcement in Nassau, on New
Providence Island. [@1] A U.S. Coast Guard helicopter flew the drugs to New Providence, and they arrived at Odyssey Airport on Wednesday afternoon. [@3] The seizure took place on 28 August 2013, a Wednesday.
[@2] Police said five more men were taken into custody on Wednesday 28 August in connection with the previous day's Andros seizure, and that the suspects and the drugs were transported to New Providence. [@5] Butler said that Tuesday stop recovered a drug vessel and five Bahamian men with a small quantity of marijuana, and that officers believed the group was responsible for a greater portion. [@3] On Tuesday 27 August, police and the Drug Enforcement Unit arrested five men around 7:00 a.m. at a marina at Mangrove Cay after a large quantity of drugs was found on their 36-foot boat. [@4]

Commander Mark Driver, the Coast Guard's OPBAT director, said the seizure
prevented nearly $1.1 million of illegal narcotics from harming Bahamian
citizens or crossing international borders. [@1] Three of them were also charged over an incident on 27 August at Mangrove Cay, where they were allegedly found with 10 pounds of Indian hemp. [@6] Prosecutors alleged the four were found with 1,201 pounds of Indian hemp on 28 August at Mars Bay, South Andros. [@6] Four men were arraigned the day before a 3 September 2013 report on charges connected to the Andros bust, pleaded not guilty, and were remanded without bail until hearings on 24 and 25 March 2014. [@6] OPBAT is a partnership of the
DEA, the Coast Guard and the government of the Bahamas against drug smuggling to
and from the Bahamas. [@1]
