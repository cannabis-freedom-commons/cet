Former New Zealand Detective Inspector Mark Franklin was sentenced in August
2013 to one year's jail in the Cook Islands after pleading guilty to selling
cannabis to an undercover officer in Rarotonga in 2010. [@1] Franklin, one of
New Zealand's most respected homicide detectives, is serving the term at
Arorangi Prison on Rarotonga. [@1]

Franklin was one of 13 people arrested during Operation Eagle, a joint
investigation between Cook Islands and New Zealand police, whose other arrests
included the son of a prominent politician and a former female police officer.
[@1] Franklin had just been diagnosed with cancer when he was arrested and says
he used cannabis to help with nausea. [@1]

## The wider ring

A year before Franklin's sentence, the operation's three main targets pleaded
guilty midway through their trial and were jailed in July 2012. Giovanni
Marsters, called the ringleader by the judge, received six years for importing
cannabis from New Zealand, selling it and growing it with sophisticated
equipment; postal worker Samuel Tangaroa, who intercepted drug parcels before
Customs, received four years; and Marsters' partner, senior police sergeant
Inano Matapo, received two and a half years for corruption and a cannabis
sale. [@2,3] The undercover operation ran from September 2010 to May 2011
with about thirty officers, eighteen of them from New Zealand. [@2] It ended
with thirteen arrests. [@3]

Franklin left New Zealand police in 2005 on psychological grounds and moved to
the Cook Islands, where he worked as a musician while helping local police on
fraud and corruption cases. [@1]
